# Motorcycles (a prototype)

`"type": "motorcycle"` in a `car.json` makes a car a motorcycle. The first one is the Honda CBR 600 RR (`web/cars/honda-cbr600`, made by `tools/make-cbr600.py`).

## Riding it (the short version)
* **Keys.** Steer left and right asks for a lean (the standard, *Motorcycle steering: lean only* in the Escape menu; *Countersteer + lean* adds a push on the bars that is off while braking). Gas and brake as a car; the front brake eases as the rear comes up, in a bend the rear brake does the work. `<` and `>` move the rider back and forward (weight for a wheelie or a stoppie). No reverse: hold the brake at a standstill and the rider pushes the bike back with his feet. Brake and gas together at a standstill is a burnout.
* **Menu options.** *Wheelie control* rolls the gas off as the front lifts. *Lean at top speed* is what the keys can still ask for at the top of the speed (15 to 45 degrees, 30 as standard). *Motorcycle follow camera*: classic chase, or one that leans with the bike. ESC does nothing on a bike; TC and ABS work as on a car.
* **What puts it down.** More than about 63 degrees of lean (10 past what the tyres hold), a hit of a wall or a barrier, 3.5 g, or a wheelie that goes over onto its back. Then the rider is a ragdoll, the bike slides as a body, the camera watches from 2.5 m, and nothing works until **Reset** or **Repair** (Backspace).
* **The key 9** makes the rider limp on the bike (to try the crash): the bike rides on with no help until it goes down.
* **At a standstill** the bike stays upright and both feet go down on the road under the pegs, the toes towards the nearest point of the road; above 5 km/h they go back to the pegs in a second, the left foot first and the right foot a third of a second later (they go down again below 4 km/h, and with the nose up in a wheelie they come up).
* **Replays** keep the lean, the posture, the foot and the ragdoll; a file from before the motorcycle had two wheels opens with two.

## How it works
The game's physics has four wheels, and a motorcycle is that physics with changes, not a second engine:

* **Two wheels.** The physics has four wheel slots; for a motorcycle the second of each pair is a lost wheel for good (`soloWheels` in Vehicle.js): the wheel that stays carries its end on the spring and the brakes of both, the drive goes to it alone, and there is no load transfer or anti-roll bar across the 4 cm track the model's two dots still give.
* **A rider that keeps it up.** A bike does not stand by itself. `web/js/Motorcycle.js` (`OpenRacer.Motorcycle`, made from the `motorcycle` settings of `car.json`; the physics of the cars in `Vehicle.js` only has a few hooks for a controller and a car has none) is the rider. The steering keys, the stick or the wheel are a **lean command**: how far the lean is wanted, from 0 to `max_lean_deg`, whatever the speed (the keys do not get smaller with the speed for a bike, and held they keep the lean; the time and the curve are the keyboard settings). The rider follows it with a delay (`lean_response`, 0.16 s) and a torque about the long axis of the bike (a spring, `balance`, and a damper) keeps the lean. At a walking pace the lean is held at zero and the command is the angle of the front wheel. A throttle that is open in a corner stands the bike up a little (`throttle_stand_up`, 0.18). Past about 66 degrees of lean the rider lets go and the bike goes down.
* **How fast the lean can change.** The lean that the rider takes can change by at most `max_roll_rate_deg` (90) degrees a second at speed, and a bit more below 40 km/h. Quick keys from the left to the right ask for more, and a rider's torque about the long axis rolled the bike at 130 degrees a second and more, unloaded both wheels and slid it sideways (`node tools/bike-flick-test.js`).
* **Steering.** The front wheel is turned to the angle that a bend of the wanted lean needs at this speed (`a = g tan(lean)`, `delta = L a / u^2`), and to start a lean it first goes a little the other way (countersteering, at most about half a g of sideways push, gone as the lean arrives). The leaned tyre helps by itself: the **camber thrust** (`camber_thrust`, 0.3) pushes the bike to the inside with that part of the load times the sine of the lean. The front end turns about the axis of the fork (`motorcycle.rake_deg`, 24.5), not about the vertical. The steering assist of the keyboard (the front wheel that follows a slide) is off for a bike. The camera of the follow view rolls with the lean (`camera_roll`, 0.45 of it).
* **Staying straight.** The rider also damps the yaw that the steering did not ask for (a torque about the vertical), which a bike with a light rear wheel in hard braking needs.
* **Braking.** The front brake is let off as the rear wheel loses its load (a stoppie goes over the front), and the rear brake goes with the load of the rear wheel (a light rear wheel that locks has no side grip). The engine braking of the CBR is 0.3 (a slipper clutch): at 1 the rear wheel slid at 30 % slip when the throttle was shut and the bike swung round.
* **A lost wheel.** A bike whose front or rear wheel is torn off (`softbody.wheel_strength`) stands on its fork or its swingarm: its body spheres touch the road from then on (it used to fall through the road, since the spheres of an upright bike stay off it). The rider holds it up; it crashes when it leans over, as always.
* **The bodywork can lean.** While the lean is less than 66 degrees the body spheres do not touch the road (a bike in a corner has its fairing near it); past that they do, and the bike slides on its side.
* **Different suspension at the front and the rear:** `spring_front`/`spring_rear`, `damping_front`/`damping_rear` as for a car (a fork of 17 kN/m and a shock of 24 kN/m).

## The car editor
*New motorcycle* in the Car card makes one (or set *Type* to motorcycle): the form then shows only what a motorcycle has. The wheels are *Front wheel* and *Rear wheel* (the physics gets a pair 4 cm apart from them), the drive is to the rear wheel through a welded diff, and the *Motorcycle* card has the rider and the lean: the max lean, the lean at top speed (`fast_lean_deg`), the rake of the fork, the rider mass (on top of the mass of the bike), the hang off, the max lateral g and the balance. The origin of a grip, a lever or a footpeg has to be at the part: the rider's hands and feet pivot on it.

## The model
**The steering axis.** Put an empty called `steerhead` in the model: its origin is a point on the axis of the fork, and its local up axis (Z in Blender) points up the fork. Turn the empty to the angle of the fork you want. The parts that steer and the front wheel turn about that line, and the wheel moves along it with the springs; `rake_deg` is then not used. Without the empty the axis goes through the middle of the front wheel at `rake_deg`.

**The geometry of the travel.** The front wheel moves along the fork and the rear wheel on the arc of the swingarm, in the physics as well as in the picture: the road force along the wheel (the brake, the drive) loads the spring with the part that goes along the travel, so the fork dives under the brake and a swingarm with its pivot above the axle resists squat. `fork_dive` in `motorcycle` (1 by default, 0 switches it off) scales it. The slope comes from the `steerhead` empty (or the rake) and from the origin of the `swingarm` part (its pivot).

**The rear shock.** A part called `rear_spring` (or `rear_shock`, `rear_damper`, `shock_rear`) is the shock between the frame and the swingarm: its long axis is measured from its geometry, the higher end is the mount on the frame and stays, the lower end is on the swingarm and goes round with it; every frame the part is turned and stretched between the two (`OpenRacer.updateRearShock`).

**The sprockets and the chain.** A part called `rear_sprocket` (or `sprocket_RL`) spins with the rear wheel without being a wheel part, so the tyre keeps its size. With `front_sprocket` (on the engine) and one `chain_link` the bike gets a chain (`Chain.js`): the link is repeated along the path round the two sprockets, every frame, so the chain follows the swingarm, moves with the rear wheel and turns the front sprocket.

**The front end.** Two sets of parts, by name. What stays on the bike and only turns with the steering is `steerhead_...` (`steerhead_cylinder`: the upper tubes of the fork, `steerhead_clamp`: the triple clamp, `steerhead_handlebar`); the loader hangs them on the steering head. What goes with the front wheel is the parts of the wheel (the tyre and the disc spin) and the parts called `brake_..._FL` (`brake_fork_leg_FL`: the lower leg, `brake_caliper_FL`, `brake_mudguard_FL`): they turn with the steering and slide along the fork with the springs, in line with the cylinders (the physics lets the wheel go straight up and down; the visual moves it back a little as it goes up, `OpenRacer.updateFork`).

**The swingarm.** A part called `swingarm` (or `swing_arm`, `rear_arm`) turns about its own origin, the pivot on the frame, as the rear wheel goes up and down; the wheel is on the end of it (`OpenRacer.updateSwingarm`, the length from the pivot to the rear axle is measured from the model). Put the origin of the part at the pivot.

`tools/make-cbr600.py` makes `cbr600.glb`. Wheels: `wheel_FL` (the tyre), `wheel_FL_rim`, `wheel_FL_disc`, and the same for `RL`, listed in `wheels.nodes` of the car. What steers with the front wheel without spinning is a part whose name ends in `FL` and starts with or contains `brake` (`brake_fork_FL`, `brake_handlebar_FL`, ...): the loader hangs the parts of a brake caliper on the steering of its wheel.

## The rider
A driver (a model with a skeleton, added in the car editor as for a car) on a bike is a rider when the model has the places that a rider takes, found by name (`Driver.js`):

* `footpeg_L`, `footpeg_R`: the feet; the pivot of a foot is its peg, `driver.feet.offset` the offset from it. The left foot also follows the shift lever.
* `grip_L`, `grip_R`: the hands. The pivot of a hand is its grip, and `driver.hands.offset` (left, right: x, y, z in the frame of the grip, set by moving the hand in the driver editor) is the offset from it. They turn with the handlebar about the axis of the fork, and the hands go round with them. **The right wrist rolls with the throttle**: `grip_R` is turned (up to 43 degrees) by the gas and the hand with it.
* `frontbrake_R`, `clutch_L`: the fingers that are not the thumb are turned to the lever together: the middle finger is the base (its x rotation points it best at the lever) and the others get the same x rotation (the thumb is round the grip). They close a little more with the brake; the left ones pull the clutch lever with every shift. The levers themselves turn too: about an axis up through the inboard end of the lever (the vertex of the lever nearest the middle of the bike, where the perch is), so the origin of a lever can stay where the fingers go.
* `shifter_L`: the shift lever (its origin is its hinge, the lever goes forward from it to a toe peg). The game turns it with every shift and the left foot goes with its toe peg: **first gear is a tap down, every gear above it a tap up, and neutral, between the first and the second, a small tap: up from the first gear, down from the second**.

In the cockpit view the torso of the rider (the bones named hips, spine, chest, neck, shoulder, upper_arm) is not drawn, only the forearms, the hands and the legs: the camera sits in his chest. The grips and the levers hang on the handlebar and turn with the steering (the loader takes `grip_L`, `grip_R`, `frontbrake_R` and `clutch_L` with the steering head). Without these parts a driver is a driver of a car (the steering wheel and the pedals). Where the model sits (`position`, `rotation`, `scale`, the turn of the chest) is set in the car editor as for any driver; the hands can be turned with `hands.rotation`, the elbows and the knees pointed with `poles`. The CBR has no driver of its own yet: a model of a rider that is small enough for the bars to be in reach (the one of the kart needed a scale of 1.15 and the chest turned 85 degrees forward).

## The debug view
The 0 key draws what the physics sees (DebugView.js). On a bike it adds the mass of the rider as an orange block where the physics has it (at the centre of gravity, moved by the posture: back on the brake, flat on the gas, to the inside of a bend; a limp rider is where his ragdoll is), a line from the middle of the bike to it, and his weight as a red arrow (gravity less the acceleration of the bike, 0.5 m at 1 g: it tips forward on the brake). The fork is drawn too: the cylinders in white, up from the hub at rest along the steering axis, and the legs in orange, up from the hub as it is now, so the travel of the fork is seen. The yellow nodes beside each wheel are the pivots of the suspension arms of the soft body (CollisionProxy.js, `wheel_hubs`), the same rig as for a car wheel; a bike has them on both sides of a wheel because of the ghost wheel of each pair. The - key hides the model of the bike and the rider while the view is on.

## The engine sound
The sound is the combustion model of the game, with parameters. New presets in `web/js/EngineSim.js`: *Single 0.65 L (thumper)*, *Inline-3 0.8 L (triple)*, *Inline-4 0.6 L (supersport)* and *Inline-4 1.0 L (superbike)*. `node tools/check-engine-sound.js [preset]` (or `--car honda-cbr600`, `--wav file.wav` to write eight seconds from idle to the limiter to listen to) runs the real worklet without a browser and says if a preset is silent, clips or has not got the pitch of its firing frequency.

## What it cannot do yet
* A rider (no driver model; the driver editor has no bike seat).
* The tyre is the shell of nodes with a round crown: at a lean the contact patch moves to its shoulder. The camber thrust of a round tyre is a force of the controller, not of the tyre.
* A wheelie and a stoppie are there (the springs and the weight transfer do them), but the rider does not catch them.
* Crashes: after about 66 degrees the bike falls over and slides; the rider is not thrown off.
* The suspension of the model (the fork that goes in) does not move; the physics does.
* **Quick and braking in a bend.** `key_steer_rate` (2) is how much faster the keys bring the lean than the wheels of a car turn, `lean_response` (0.1 s) how fast the rider follows. In a bend the front brake is cut to a tenth (it stands the bike up) and the rear brake takes the bike deeper into it: `corner_brake` (0.2) more lean at full pedal.
* **The rider.** `rider_mass` (80 kg) is on top of the `mass` of the bike (without the rider; `cg_height` and `weight_distribution` are for the bike with the rider in his normal seat). He moves (`posture`): back (0.22 m) and up on the brake, forward (0.12 m) and down on the throttle at speed, `hang_off` (0.3 m) to the inside of a bend. The weight of the rider where he is makes a torque about the bike (the pitch and the yaw), the hang off makes the bike lean less for the same bend, and the air has more of a rider who sits up. Without a driver model the mass and the moves are the same; with one the body, the arms and legs and the knees follow.
* **Wheelie, bank, keys.** With the front wheel in the air the lean steers (the rider takes the bike round at `g tan(lean) / speed`). The lean is measured from the road (the normals of the wheels), not from the sky. The keys < and > (comma, full stop; settings menu) put the rider back or forward while held.
* **The tyres of a bike.** The camber thrust (`camber_thrust`, 0.85 of load × tan(lean)) is at the contact patch of every wheel, shared front and rear as the weight at rest: it does most of the turning and it rights the bike. The front wheel points along the bend (`L g tan(lean) / u²`) and the rider holds the yaw rate of the lean (`g tan(lean) / u`). On the brake and upright the front wheel castors into its slip angle (`front_comply`, 3), which keeps a hard stop straight.
* **The tyre** (the shell of nodes, see the README) of a motorcycle has a round crown: the outer rings of nodes lie 30 % of the width lower than the middle one, so the tyre rolls on to its shoulder as the bike leans; its wheels lean with the bike and nothing more (no camber of their own).
* **The rear brake alone.** The handbrake key of a car (Space) is the rear brake of the bike (a bike has no handbrake): the brake key works both brakes.
* **A landing.** After a flight of a quarter second or more the rider braces: for half a second after the touchdown the balance and the yaw damping are up to twice as strong, so the kick of the tyres on a landing that is a little off line does not throw the bike into a lean and a spin. The bike is down from 70 degrees of lean (`crash_lean_deg`; 63 before), and a slide sideways counts as a lowside after 0.8 s (0.5 before).
* **In the air.** What spins the rear wheel up (the gas) or slows the wheels (the brakes) turns the bike the other way, as the angular momentum is kept: gas brings the nose up, the brake brings it down, so a jump is landed on the wheels (`motorcycle.air_pitch`, a factor, 1; 0 switches it off); the wheelie control does not cut the gas in the air. A hard landing is held by the tyre: the hub cannot come closer to the road than the rim (a very stiff spring and a damper, `TyreShell.js`), and the load a tyre may give is 25 times its static load (6 before: a landing from 3 m went through the road).
* **A standstill.** Both feet go down on the road and the bike stays upright (`foot_lean_deg` is not used any more); above 5 km/h the left foot is back on its peg first, the right one a third of a second later, all in a second.
* **Wheelie control** (menu, on by default): the gas is rolled off as the front wheel goes light, to about a tenth of its load.
* **Two wheels.** The physics has four wheel slots; for a motorcycle the second of each pair is a lost wheel for good (Vehicle.js `soloWheels`: `wheelLost` 1 and 3, twice the mass and the rate on the wheel that stays, `torqueShares` to it alone, no geometric load transfer and no anti-roll bar, the brakes of both in `Motorcycle.brakeScale`). The model still has its two dots of a millimetre for the other slots.
* **A crash.** Past 78 degrees of lean (`crash_lean_deg`; a slide is ridden out to there, under power or with the gas shut: a powerslide, or backing it in with the rear at 45 degrees for a second), after a slide of more than 25 degrees for two seconds without power (the tyres have let go: a lowside), or after a hit (the body of the bike against something at more than 20 km/h, a loss of 8 m/s in a tenth of a second, or a blow of more than 7 g; the tyres that bite again after a long slide give 4 to 8 g sideways for a moment, which is no crash) the bike slides as a body and the rider is thrown off as a ragdoll (Driver.js: 15 particles for the hips, the chest, the head, the shoulders, the elbows, the hands, the hip joints, the knees and the feet, held together by sticks, a rigid torso and a minimum fold for every limb, Verlet at 240 Hz, 0.7 g of friction on the road, a sudden higher floor is a wall; the skeleton is posed on the particles with the inverse kinematics). The ragdoll collides with the body spheres of the bike (pushed out as a move, never as a speed); what he gives the bike is the hit in the first 0.3 s and after that no more than half his weight (a rider wedged in the bike pushed it away, and the two slid off together). A limp rider (the key 9) slumps on the bike: the seat and the tank hold his hips and knees while the bike is upright, the bike rides on with no help at all (the front wheel let go, no balance, no throttle) and his weight off the middle leans it until it goes down (past 40 degrees, or stopped). At a walking pace with the bars at the lock the throttle is eased to a third, and the rider holds the bike up as in a U-turn (the balance used to fade there and the bike went over). A burnout: brake and throttle together below about 3 m/s hold the front brake and let the rear brake off (`brakeStep`), and the traction control stays out of it (`controller.burnout`). The foot at a standstill comes up at 7 km/h of the bike itself or with the nose up more than 30 degrees, in a second, and reaches for the road (Car.js passes `roadY`, the road under the rear wheel). At speed the lean the keys can ask for goes down to `fast_lean_deg` (30) at `fast_from` + 22 m/s, and the rider hangs off for a small lean from 110 km/h. The lean is the dip of the bike's own left axis below the road (`leanOf`), so a wheelie reads 0 degrees however steep; a bike on its back (up more than 100 degrees from the road) is a crash. A hit that takes more than 6 m/s in a tenth of a second (a barrier) is a crash. The crash camera follows the rider: parented to him with the sky up (it goes with his place, not with his tumble; the offset it had at the crash is kept, 2.5 to 5 m away and at least 1.2 m up), smoothed, looking at him, and it stays in front of a wall as the chase camera does. The replay records the particles (a file of version 4). The joints have limits (elbows back, knees forward, in the frame of the limb itself and within 35 degrees of the plane of the joint, never quite straight (3 cm of bend); cones for the upper arms and the thighs, damped at the limit); the skeleton bends to a pole pushed out to the side of the joint's bend, so the knee of the model cannot flip. A limit moves a particle without giving it speed (and takes the speed into the limit out), by at most 2 cm an iteration and never into the road. The limbs keep out of each other: every pair of particles that no stick joins is held at least the sum of their radii apart (7 cm at least each). ESC is off on a bike (Vehicle.js: `!self.controller`). Crash limits: 7 g, `crash_lean_deg` (78), a slide of 25 degrees for 2 s without power, a hit of 8 m/s in 0.1 s; the rider can lean `over_lean_deg` (10) past what the grip holds, which is the lowside. Every part of the ragdoll starts with the speed it has (the bike's speed and turn at that place, plus the rider's own movement), and the spheres of the bike move on within the frame; the camera stays and watches; no input until Reset or Repair (Car.js `recoverCrash`).

## Model v2 (a separate model, in test)
`"motorcycle": { "model": "v2" }` in `car.json` switches a bike to the separate model (phase 2 of the plan: `tools/bike-model-proto.js` is phase 1). Off by default; the old model is untouched.
* **The bars are a degree of freedom** (`Motorcycle.steeringV2`): an angle and a rate with their own inertia (`steer_inertia`), a damper (`steer_damp`, 60), the hands as a spring (`arm_omega`, 40 rad/s) and the side force of the front tyre's slip angle behind the steering axis (`trail`, 0.015 m). The rider's only input is the angle he asks for: the bend that the lean he wants needs (from the wanted lean, not from the lean the bike has: that sat still wherever the lean was) plus a gain on the lean error (`rider_ka` 1, `rider_kad` 0.2, at 10 m/s, less as the speed goes up; `rider_err_cap_deg` and `rider_bars_cap_deg` limit it, off by default). He does not roll the bike with a torque any more (above 7 m/s the lean spring is gone), and the yaw damping is gone.
* **The lean that is asked stays under the grip** (`grip_g` 0.95 g, `over_lean_deg` 0 for v2): at 1.05 g and 10 degrees over, the lean of 45 to 48 degrees sat at the edge of the tyre and a flick threw the bike.
* **Countersteering is a result**: the first move of the bars is away from the bend (about 3.5 degrees at 40 km/h, 1.6 at 80, 1.2 at 140), because the lean error asks for it and the tyres do the rest.
* **The tyre gives the camber thrust** (`Wheel.tyreForce` with the lean of the bike as camber, `camber_slip`): the fixed `camber_thrust` force of the controller is not used. v2 bikes ride on the simple tyre (a slip curve, no shell of nodes), and their side force acts at the contact patch (no roll centre).
* **Test:** `node tools/bike-v2-test.js` (`--v1` for the old model, `--only=flick|straight|command`, `--trace=40`, `--set=name=value`). v2 holds a straight line at 40, 80 and 140 km/h, takes a lean command at three speeds and rides the flicks of `bike-flick-test.js`.
* **Slow and fast are two models** (the video of a bike that went down on every steer at 20 to 40 km/h): up to `kin_from` (7 m/s) the bars are the angle of the old model (the bend of the lean, a countersteer, the castor of the front wheel), from `kin_to` (13 m/s) they are the angle of the rider and the tyres, and in between it goes over. A bike has to be steered into its fall below about 5 m/s and away from it above, and in between the bars do little to the lean. The rider's hold on the lean (the torque about the long axis) stays until 6 to 14 m/s (`balance_from`, `balance_to`). A lean at a low speed may ask for 65 % of the lock of the bars at most (`lock_share`): at the lock the front tyre was at 15 degrees of slip and the bike slid when the lean was let go.
* **More tests:** `--only=rider` (every speed from 8 to 140 km/h with the keys held, on the gas and with it shut: stays up, the radius of the bend, hunting of the bars), `--only=straightcoast` (the gas shut and the brake, straight), `--only=launch` (a start with the gas open and a steer at once), `--bend=40 [--coast] [--from=s]` (the lean, bars and slip every 0.1 s), `--grip=1.3`.
* **The tyre has a round crown** (`crown_radius`, 0.1 m; Vehicle.js, v2 only): a thin wheel that leans sinks by R (1 - cos(lean)), 4 cm at 40 degrees; a bike on a round tyre stands on its shoulder: along the axis of the wheel the road is (R - rc) + rc / cos(lean) from the hub, and the contact point is to the inside of the lean by rc (-n - down / cos(lean)). Without it the bike came down 15 cm in a step at 40 degrees of lean with the gas tapped (the spheres of the body met the road) and was thrown.
* **The replay of a rider** (`replay-go-kart-track-1-...openracer-replay`, 61 s, the Supermoto in v2 from before the blend of the bars): the lean swung between -50 and +44 degrees every second or two at 48 km/h with the gas shut, and the bike went down five times, every time after a steer reversal with the brake or the gas let go. The scenarios `--only=replay` (gas tapped in a bend, gas shut, the brake in a bend, the key let go, flicks with the gas tapped) reproduce it; all of them stay up now.
* **Braking at speed** (three more replays of a rider, the Supermoto and the 600 at 100 to 145 km/h: the bars tick by 1 to 2 degrees at 5 Hz, and after a correction and the brake the lean runs from 3 to 30 and 65 degrees with the bars at the lock): the bench reproduces it with the real keyboard (a tap of 90 ms ramps the command as Car.js does, `--only=keys`: three or six taps, then the brake at 60, 100, 140 km/h). Cause: on the brake the rear wheel is light and the whole bike yaws back and forth at 1 rad/s and more (0.5 s a swing) with the bars still; the old model had a yaw damping for that and v2 had dropped it. v2 has it again **on the brake only** (`yaw_brake`, 1; 0.05 to 0.3 of the pedal). Lean on the brake after the taps: 4 to 8 degrees where it was 15 to 25, the slip angle 1 to 2 degrees where it was 8 to 46. The same scenario in the game: the 600 went down (spin, 57 degrees) and now stays up (4.5 degrees).
* **The gains of the rider come down with the speed** while no lean is asked for (`rider_fast_ka` 0.2, `rider_fast_kad` 0.5 of the gains from 8 to 14 m/s, `gain_down_from` and `gain_down_to`): with the full gain a bike on the brake from 140 km/h weaved at 1.7 Hz and the weave grew. In a bend the gains are full (the lean at 140 km/h was half of what was asked with the low gain). The answer of the bars to a lean error is limited to 12 degrees (`rider_bars_cap_deg`): a flick with the gas tapped at 35 km/h on the 600 pushed the bars to the lock, the front tyre slid and the bike was down.
* **More bench options:** `--car=sports-motorcycle-600cc`, `--bumps=0.005` (a road that is not smooth, 5 mm), `--only=wobble|keys`, `--shake=`, `--rider` for all scenarios. The 600 has been tuned only through these tests, with the settings of the Supermoto.
* **The body of the rider helps** (`body_assist` 1, `body_arm` 0.5 m, `assist_err_from_deg` 15 and `assist_err_to_deg` 35; v2 only): the bars do not steer the lean when the front wheel is light or in the air, and the balance torque of the old model is gone above 14 m/s, so a bike with its nose up at 70 km/h and a key for a bend had nothing that held its lean (a replay of a rider: the lean ran to 85 degrees in 0.7 s on the gas in first gear; in the test 73 to 75 degrees, `--only=assist` with the nose kicked up). Now the rider throws his weight to the one side: a spring and a damper on the lean error, as a torque about the long axis of at most rider mass x g x `body_arm` (390 Nm for 80 kg). Its share is 1 with the front wheel off the road, and comes in between 15 and 35 degrees of lean error (more when the lean runs away from what is asked); it is 0 in a bend that goes as asked, so the other tests are as they were. The weight of the rider goes sideways in the model too (`posture.assist`, Driver.js), and the head turns into the bend (`posture.look`, 35 degrees at 35 degrees of the lean that is asked; the replays have it too). `--only=assist` (gas wide open in first gear, wheelie control off, a key, a kick of the nose; `--lift=`, `--noimpulse`, `--adump`) and `--only=bendbrake` (the radius of a bend before and on the brake from the path of the speed: it goes down by 40 % at 70 km/h on the rear brake, since the speed does).
* **The radius is the tyre's** (`grip_g` 0.95 g on the bike's lean, the rider's hang off comes on top): the skidpad of 50 m holds 0.98 g at 74 km/h (44 degrees of lean of the whole needed, the bike 43 + 6 degrees of hang off), so 45 m at 90 km/h on a full key is speed squared over g tan(lean). (The 25 m at 70 km/h of `--only=bendbrake` is the bend before it has settled: not a steady radius, do not read a grip from it.) `grip_g` 1.05 gave 10 % less, but a flick after a wheelie at 120 km/h then went into a spin, so it stays.
* **The wheelie as a test of power** (`--only=wheelie`, `--gear=`, `--wspeed=`, `--wdump`): gas wide open in first gear, wheelie control off. It says when the front wheel comes up, how high, and what the bike pulls with the nose still down against the g at which the front comes up (g x the distance of the centre of gravity from the rear contact / its height, 1.09 g for the 600): a bike that pulls twice that loops over, and a rider cannot hold it (the 600 with the gears of the car.json of 5 November: 2.3 g, over the top in 0.15 s, at 70 to 80 km/h in first gear after the rear wheel had spun and gripped again). A warning, not a failure: the wheelie control, the traction control or a lower power is the answer. The weight of the rider forward (`pitch_assist`, `pitch_arm`, `pitch_lead`: the keys for forward and back in full, on its own from 20 degrees of nose up) is a torque of 390 Nm at most, and does not hold a wheelie that comes up at 400 degrees a second; it works for a front that comes up slowly. The `launch` scenarios of the 600 fail with the new gear ratios for the same reason (lean 76 degrees, a spin).
* **The skidpad** (`OpenRacer.Motorcycle.circleSteer`, used by main.js and by `--only=skidpad`, `--sk=w0=` `--sk=ki=` `--skdump`): the correction to the line is a spring and a damper in m/s^2 on the distance and the speed across it (the further from the line the more lean), `w0` 1.5 rad/s (0.7 before: 0.7 to 0.9 m from the line on the way up to the limit, now 0.3 m; 2.5 gave up to 0.9 m again, the lean cannot follow). The test counts the g only on the line (1.5 m): a bike that left it swept through 1.7 g for a moment. The bench prints the friction ellipse check: the lean that the g needs (atan(g)) against the lean of the bike plus the hang off of the rider; the 600 holds 0.98 g on the 50 m circle at 74 km/h (44 degrees needed, 43 + 6 there).
* **The warning for too much power** (`OpenRacer.wheelieCheck` in `web/js/CarDefaults.js`): the car editor (a red line under the summary) and the tuning menu (under the line with the hp and the top speed) say when the front wheel comes up on full throttle, live as the engine, the gears, the final drive, the mass or the centre of gravity change: the peak torque through every gear over the radius of the tyre and the mass with the rider, against g x (wheelbase x front share) / (height of the centre of gravity); "loops over" from 1.8 times the limit. It is the first-order sum (the bench measures less in first gear while the clutch slips, and more after a spinning wheel grips again), so it is a warning, and the bench (`--only=wheelie`, `--only=launch`) is the proof. The 600 as committed (six gears, 2.75 in first) lifts 13 degrees and passes the launch tests; the car.json of 5 November (four gears, torque converter, second 1.55) is flagged at 1.42 g against 1.09 g and fails them.
* **Riding style** (the menu, Settings, with a motorcycle: "Flat-track style", `bikePush`; `--push` in the bench): off is the road-race style as before (the rider hangs off to the inside, up to `hang_off` 0.3 m, the inside knee goes out); on is flat track and supermoto: the rider stays upright and pushes the bike under him. The weight goes a little to the outside (`push_hang` -0.4 of `hang_off`), so the bike leans further for the same bend (the skidpad of the 600: the whole lean 47 degrees, the bike 44 + 2.5, against 44 + 6 hanging off; the line is 0.56 m off at most against 0.40), and a lean at a low speed may ask 0.75 of the lock instead of 0.65 (`push_lock_share`: a tighter bend). The model of the rider (Driver.js) puts the inside foot out and down to the road, where it skids (a lean of 8 degrees and more, below 50 km/h, `posture.push`), the inside elbow goes up and out, the knee does not go out, and the torso leans the other way. `posture.inside` is the side of the bend either way. Not in the physics worker (it does not get the options of the menu, as with the other bike options).
* **The riding style per bike** (the tuning menu, the Motorcycle card: "Flat-track style"; the car editor, Motorcycle: the same box; car.json `motorcycle.push`; the box in Settings turns it on for every bike): a deeper lean (`push_extra_lean_deg` 4 degrees more than the lean that the tyres hold, below 50 km/h and not on the brake: more and the Supermoto spins in a flick at 40 km/h), the trunk leans out of the bike by 35 % of its lean (the body stays more upright than the bike), and a slide is countersteered by itself and subtly (`push_slide` -0.6 of the slip angle from 6 degrees up, at most `push_slide_cap_deg` 7 degrees of the bars, only in a bend: the body of a bike in a bend has 3 to 5 degrees of slip anyway; the sign is minus, plus made the spins worse in the bench). The tyres of the model hold the bike at a slip angle of 4 to 6 degrees even at a lean of 65 degrees (`--set=max_lean_deg=70 --set=push_extra_lean_deg=16`), so a real slide does not happen in the bench: `--only=slide` (a key, then the gas wide open in second) shows only that nothing gets worse. The supermoto-700 car.json of 5 November (`max_lean_deg` 55, `fast_lean_deg` 45, `balance` 3) spins in `--only=slide` at 40 km/h with the key held in one go, with the style or without; the committed one does not.
* **The tail steps out on the gas** (flat-track style, `push_drift` 0.3): full throttle in a bend (a lean of 12 degrees and more, below 65 km/h) takes up to 30 % of the lateral grip of the rear tyre (`Wheel.lateralGrip`, over a fifth of a second), and the lean that the tyres hold goes down with it, so the rider stands the bike up a little as it steps out. `--only=slide` (`--sgear=0|1`, `--wc`, `--sldump` with drift and rear grip) passes in both styles on both bikes. What it does not do is make a slide you can see: the slip angle of the body in the bench stays at 4 to 10 degrees whatever `push_drift` is (0.3, 0.6 and 0.8 gave the same numbers), because the rear tyre of the model carries so little of the lateral force in a bend at 25 to 55 km/h that a fifth less of it is not felt; a sustained slide of 15 to 25 degrees needs a tyre that carries its whole load at the limit on the gas, with its own rider (gas, weight and bars together), which is not built. The first gear at 55 km/h and more in `--sgear=0` is a bench artefact (the gear is forced from the start of the run).
* **The supermoto-700** with `balance` 3 (car.json of 5 November) spun in `--only=slide` at 40 km/h with the key held in one go; 1.5, 2 and 2.5 pass (the balance only works at a walking pace and a low speed). Set to 2.
* **The rider in flat-track style** (Driver.js, from a screenshot of the user): the head stays upright to the road whatever the bike and the trunk do, and only turns about the vertical (35 degrees at most, into the bend). Its roll is measured, not worked out: `headRoll` is how far the head leans from the vertical across the bike, and the roll of the head bone is corrected step by step (the sign and the size of the effect of the bone found once with a probe of plus and minus ten degrees, `headSlope`); on the skidpad at 54 km/h and 30 degrees of lean the head is 0.2 degrees off the vertical (`car.driver.headRollNow()`). The inside foot is put on the road (the global height, `state.roadY`) beside the peg, forward and out as the lean goes up (from 12 to 48 degrees): a bent knee at a small lean, the leg stretched out beside the front wheel at the most lean, within the reach of the leg from the hip; at 30 degrees the ankle of the inside foot is at 0.092 m with the road at 0 (`FOOT_ANKLE` 0.09). And for every style and every foot: an ankle is never put below the road (a peg at a deep lean is near it). Not done: a section of the car editor where the driver can be posed freely for the most lean to the right, left, forward and back (the poses now are in the code).
* **Foot out up to a speed, the head along the racing line:** the inside foot of the flat-track rider comes out in a bend up to `push_foot_kmh` (110 km/h; a slider in the tuning menu, shown when the style is on, and a field in the car editor; the foot comes in between 80 and 100 % of it). The head looks at a point of the racing line ahead of the bike (`OpenRacer.Motorcycle.lineLook`, from Car.js with `race.line`: a second of riding, 8 to 45 m ahead, found from the nearest point of the line), as the angle between the way the bike points and the way to that point, at most 60 degrees; without a line (the skidpad, a track that has none, the replay) it looks by the lean that is asked as before. Both styles.
* **The foot that flickered, the back and the pelvis** (from a screenshot against a photo of a supermoto): the road height for the foot came from the rear wheel only while it touched the road (`Car.js`: `roadY`), so on a bump or under the gas the inside foot jumped between the peg and the road; now it is the contact point of the wheel (`wheel.contact.y`, right at a lean too: the middle of the wheel less the radius is not), and the driver holds the last height and smooths it (`lastRoadY`). The foot is no longer laid flat on the road: it keeps its angle to the shin (the sole turned out, as a boot stood on its edge), the ankle 0.12 m over the road. In flat-track style the whole rider is no longer moved to the side (`posture.lat`): the pelvis slides 0.09 m to the inside on the saddle (at 25 degrees of lean and more), and the back leans out of the bike by 55 % of its lean (the chest and the bones of the back, upright to the road) with the head upright to the road. Checked on the skidpad at 56 km/h, the inside ankle moves by 2 cm in 4 s; not on a bumpy lap.
* **The feet in a replay** (two videos of the user: playing the replay from the game put both legs straight up at a standstill; a saved replay had the feet on the pegs, not following the road): the road height for the feet was the contact point of the rear wheel, and in a replay the wheels do not touch anything (the physics does not run), so it was the height of where the bike had stopped last, or none. Now `Car.js` gives the driver `roadAt(x, z, y)`, a ray down from a metre over the point against the ground of the track, and the driver asks it for the road under the place where the foot goes (the foot beside the peg, the foot at a standstill, the peg), smoothed and held per place (`roadHold`). In a replay of the skidpad at 60 km/h and 36 degrees of lean the inside ankle is at 0.13 m over a road at 0, the other on its peg at 0.5. The pelvis in flat-track style slides to the outside on the saddle (9 cm at 25 degrees of lean and more), the back leans in (it was the other way).
* **The pelvis tilts against the bike** (flat-track style, `Driver.pelvisTilt` 35 degrees): the hips bone turns against the lean of the bike by 0.7 of the lean asked (at most 35 degrees), so the pelvis stays about level with the road; the back keeps the turn that it had in the world (its own roll is less by what the pelvis took), and the head is still upright. On the skidpad at 60 km/h and 28 degrees of lean the pelvis turns 20 degrees and the chest 1 degree against it. Not a slider yet.
* **Not done yet:** the gyroscopic terms, the crash by saturation instead of thresholds, the rider's weight in the lean. The lean follows what is asked to within 1 to 4 degrees at 40 to 140 km/h and the bars do not hunt (they did: 3 to 9 degrees at 40 km/h). **The radius of a bend** (measured from the path, not from the yaw rate of the body: that is the rate about the leaned axis and was 25 % too low, which made the radius look 25 % too big): at a lean of 42 degrees the bike gives the 0.9 g that tan(lean) says, 28 m at 60 km/h, 85 m at 100 km/h and 190 m at 140 km/h with the keys held (`fast_lean_deg` 30 takes the lean down at top speed). The old model took the same lean but slowed down in the bend (30 km/h in a bend that it was asked at 40), so its radii look smaller at the same key. The lean limit is 0.95 g from a standstill to 13 m/s and rises to 1.15 g (`grip_fast`) from 18 m/s: a deeper lean at a low speed made the bike slide when the key was let go. Settings found by a sweep on the Supermoto only (`tools/bike-v2-test.js --score`); not run in the browser.
