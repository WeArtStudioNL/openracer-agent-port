# Changelog

What is new in every version of OpenRacer. The version is `OpenRacer.VERSION` in `web/js/Version.js`.

## 2.2.0 (2026-10-24)

Since 2.1.0 (267 commits): the track in 3D with a soft body for every car, motorcycles with a rider and a ragdoll crash, a driver in the car, replays as files with a video editor, a new menu, the drag strip, and much more. Per subject:

### Motorcycles

**After the first drive**
- The rear shock of a motorcycle (`rear_spring`, or `rear_shock`, `rear_damper`, `shock_rear`) goes with the swingarm: its upper end stays on the frame, its lower end goes round with the arm, and the part is turned and stretched between the two every frame (it stood still on the Supermoto 700).
- The toes of the rider went through the floor at a standstill: they go down only so far that their tip is on the road, with the ankle 9 cm above it.
- A motorcycle has no handbrake: the handbrake key (Space) is the rear brake alone (it locked the rear wheel with the handbrake of a car).
- **Both feet down at a standstill, the bike upright.** The bike no longer rests against the right foot: both feet go down on the road under the pegs, the toes towards the nearest point of the road. Above 5 km/h (they go down again below 4) the feet go back to the pegs in a second, the left foot first and the right one a third of a second later.
- **The video editor shows the posture of the rider and the feet.** The lean forward and back (tuck, sit), the hanging off sideways and the feet were eased from the frame before, so a jump in time, a pause or a frame without a step in the editor left them where they were; they are the posture of the sample now after a jump.
- The brakes: the rear keeps at least 22 % of its load while a lean is asked for or the bike leans or slides (a rear with no load has no grip sideways), and both brakes come off as the slip of the bike grows past 5 degrees, as the cornering ABS of a modern bike does. A hard brake with a little steering at 180 km/h still slides; that goes deeper, into the rear tyre under the brakes, and is an open point.
- The tyres squeal later on a motorcycle (the camber of its tyres carries a bend).
- On the brakes the rider holds the bars harder (the hold on the yaw is up to three times as strong, 9 000 N m): with the rear nearly off the road the front alone turned the bike 14 degrees out of line in a second at 145 km/h.
- A lowside: a bike that slides sideways past 15 degrees for half a second while it leans goes down (it crabbed along for seconds at the limit of the lean).
- Steering at speed: the lean the keys can ask for at the top of the speed is 30 degrees (`fast_lean_deg`; it was 18, and the bike hardly steered at 200 km/h), and from 110 km/h the rider hangs off for a small lean already (fully at 180), so his weight to the inside turns the bike where it hardly leans. The lean at the top of the speed is in the Escape menu (*Motorcycle: lean at top speed*, 15 to 45 degrees).
- A jump landed on a lean: the pressing force of the tyre across its suspension (what a tyre gives against a ramp side or a wall) is capped at the static load of the wheel on a motorcycle; a tyre that landed leaned on a slope at 170 km/h pressed with 4 000 N and threw the bike backwards at 100 km/h ("the bike shot into the ground"). A bike that is spun round anyway (more than 4 rad/s of yaw above 18 km/h) is a crash now (reason "spin"): it slides out instead of shooting off backwards.
- The brake lever and the clutch lever of a bike turn when they are pulled (about their hinge, the inboard end of the lever: up to 17 degrees with the front brake, and the clutch lever with every shift). In a replay the shift lever and the clutch hand follow the recorded gear (they followed the gear of the game, which does not change in a replay).
- Debug view (the 0 key) on a motorcycle: the mass of the rider as a block (orange) where the physics has it, moved by the posture, with his weight as an arrow (red: gravity less the acceleration of the bike), and the fork (the cylinders white, the legs orange, so the travel is seen). The - key hides the model of the car and the driver while the view is on, so only the lines are seen.
- A bike that loses its front or its rear wheel (the soft body tears it off) stands on its fork or its swingarm: its body touches the road from then on (it fell through the road, since the spheres of an upright bike stay off it and there was no wheel to hold it up). It is no crash by itself: the rider holds it up until it leans over.
- The fork legs and the mudguard of a motorcycle (the `brake_..._FL` parts) hang on the wheel that is drawn: the physics has a pair of wheels 4 cm apart and the model one wheel, and the parts hung on the empty slot, 4 cm beside the tyre; the wheel of the model is drawn in the middle of the pair.
- Cockpit view: the torso of the driver (the hips, the spine, the chest, the neck, the shoulders and the upper arms, by the names of the bones; on a Rigify rig the DEF- bones) is not drawn, as the head was not; the forearms, the hands and the legs stay. The cockpit thumbnail of the car editor shows the same.
- The front wheel of a motorcycle goes along the fork as the suspension works (it went straight up and down: the set-back along the rake was applied to the ghost wheel, not the wheel that is drawn), and the swingarm (a part called `swingarm`) turns about its pivot with the rear wheel, which is on the end of it (it was frozen to the body). In the game and in the test of the car editor.
- The tyres of a bike jittered back and forth at a standstill: the tyre force can only bring a wheel to the speed of the road in a step, not past it (a light wheel on a stiff tyre reversed every frame). Not while the engine drives the wheel: a wheel kept at the road speed has no slip, the drive went through the tyre unchecked and the bikes looped over backwards at every start (found a day later, and put right).
- Car editor: *New motorcycle* next to *New car*, and a *Type* field (car or motorcycle). A motorcycle shows only what a motorcycle has: one wheel at each end (Front wheel, Rear wheel), no differential, no reverse, no steering wheel, no tracks, and a *Motorcycle* card with the rider and the lean (max lean, lean at top speed, rake, rider mass, hang off, max lateral g, balance). A new motorcycle could not be made before: the type came only from a car that was loaded.
- A new motorcycle: the **Supermoto 700** (the GasGas SM 700 of 2023 as a setup: a 693 cc single, 65 hp at 7500 rpm, 9000 rpm, 155 kg, long travel), on the "2022 Husqvarna FS 450" model by OUTPISTON (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, see CREDITS.md). The origins of its grips and levers were moved to the parts (the hands of the rider pivot on them).
- The rider looks for his grips and pegs again when his frame changes or the bike changes: in the car editor he kept the grips of the bike that was loaded before (or of the model view, once he was on the test bench), and his hands went up in the air; a hand moved then was saved with a nonsense offset.
- The steering axis of a motorcycle can be set in the model: a node called exactly `steerhead` (an empty is best) gives the pivot (its origin) and the direction (its local up axis: Z in Blender, up the fork). Everything that steers (`steerhead_…`, the handlebar parts, `brake_…_FL`, the front wheel) turns about that line, and the wheel goes along it with the travel. Without the node it is as before: through the middle of the front wheel at the rake of the car.
- The geometry of the fork and of the swingarm is in the physics: the wheel does not go straight up, it goes along the fork (the `steerhead` axis, or the rake) and on the arc of the swingarm (from its pivot to the rear axle). A road force along the wheel loads the spring with the part of it that goes along the travel: under the brake the fork dives (at 30 m/s the front goes in 11 to 12 cm on the Supermoto, 6 to 7 cm on the CBR, against 2 to 7 cm before), and a swingarm with its pivot above the axle gives anti-squat. The body gets the same total force; only the spring goes in further. `motorcycle.fork_dive` (the *Motorcycle* card of the editor) scales it, 0 is off.
- Braking hard: the front brake no longer hunts. The limit of the front brake (the rear wheel keeps a few percent of its load) was a target from the load of the rear wheel with a delay, and it hopped about twice a second: the fork went in and out and the rear wheel came off the road and down again, with the ABS on or off. It is now a limit on the braking force of the road (what the tyres give, against the weight that the brake can throw onto the front by statics: the wheelbase, the height of the centre of gravity and the weight distribution), which follows the brake in a few hundredths of a second, with the load of the rear wheel as a second check. On the Supermoto the rear wheel is off the road a fifth of a full stop (almost half before), and it stops as fast.
- The front wheel of a motorcycle has the inertia of a front wheel with its tyre and discs, 1.2 kg m2 at least (the size of the bike gave 0.33): at that value the tyre force of a hard brake turned the wheel round in a frame, past the road speed and back, and the force flipped between about -3000 and +3000 N three times a second. That was the hopping of the fork and the rear wheel that was left after the brake limit was put right, in the replays of both bikes (with the ABS on or off). In 18 stops from 100 and 160 km/h on both bikes the rear wheel stays on the road (it was off it half of the stop), without a crash; the stops take as long as before.
- The wheels of a heavy car (the stadium truck) rocked forwards and backwards at a standstill: the grip of the tyre damps the slide with the weight of the whole car (TyreBody.js, the damping at a standstill), and a light wheel took that in one explicit step, past the road speed and back, so the force of the tyre flipped between its limits (about 4 000 N) every frame and the wheel turned at 3 rad/s either way. The wheel takes the damping implicitly now (the inertia plus the time step times the slope of the damping), in the cylinder tyre of every car and bike.
- The pictures of the cars in the menu are made again when the model file changes: they were kept by what car.json says (the id, the model name, the size), so a model that was exported again under the same name kept its old picture, in the browser and as a `preview.webp`. The size and the date of the model file are in the key now, and a `preview.webp` that is older than the model is not used.
- The cinematic camera of a replay can look ahead of the car: *Look ahead* in the bar of the camera (0 to 40 m: the camera aims that far in front of the car along the way it points, level, and still turns smoothly), and in the video editor a *look ahead* field per clip that has the Cinematic camera (it is used while the clip plays and in the mp4 and the replay that are made of it). 0 is as it was: at the car.
- The aspect of the picture of a replay and of a video: 16:9, 9:16 or 1:1 (*Frame* in the bar of the replay, and at the top of the video editor; it is kept in the browser). The replay shows it as an orange outline in the window, the largest rectangle of that shape: what a video takes of the picture. The *Video* of the replay records the middle of the picture in it (at most 1920 pixels on the long side), the video editor renders in it (1280 x 720, 720 x 1280 or 960 x 960: the monitors, the render and the mp4 all have it) and shows what is outside the frame as a hatched passepartout with an orange line round the picture. The aspect cannot be changed while the editor records. A portrait picture has the same vertical field of view as the wide one, so it shows less of the sides.
- A drive shaft for a car with drive to all wheels: a part of the model called `propshaft` (or `susp_propshaft`, `cardan`, `susp_driveshaft_center`) stays where it is and turns about its own length (its longest side) with the wheels, at the speed of the wheels. The differentials at the front and the rear, and the half shafts (`susp_driveshaft_FL` and so on, which get longer and shorter with the travel), are the parts of the model as before.
- Car editor, *Drive* and *Drop car* on a motorcycle: the front end turns as one (the handlebar parts and the grips stayed still, only the wheel and the leg steered, about the vertical) and the front wheel and the swingarm follow the travel of the suspension as in the game: the fork and the swingarm were never updated on the test bench, because the wheel of the physics has no object of the model there.
- The reset key without a saved place goes to the nearest point of the racing line of the AI, in its direction, or else to the nearest point of the line from the gate that was passed last to the next gate; the start only when there is neither.
- The hands keep their turn on the grips when the rider hangs off a bend: the turn of a hand relative to its grip is found once, in his neutral posture, and kept (it was found again every frame from the arm that the posture of the moment gave, and the fingers pointed up in a bend).
- A bike that is down slides with a steady 0.6 g of friction and the rider with 0.55 g, so they slide together (the bike lost its speed as a share of it, 3 g at first, and caught the rider up from behind at full speed; that was the rider that "shot off the bike").
- The version is 2.2.0: the replay file is version 4 and the physics of a motorcycle changed; every browser loads the new scripts (the ?v= of the scripts is the version).
- The front of a wheelie comes down without the bounce: the fork and the shock of a motorcycle have no blow-off (the damper of a car lets go past 0.3 m/s, and the spring threw the bike 2.6 m/s back up after a wheelie to the vertical) and hold the rebound a quarter more. Their damper, and the damping of the stops, is solved implicitly, so it is stable at any strength: the one wheel of an end has twice the spring of a wheel of a pair, and the explicit damper rang at 240 Hz and blew up on a hard landing or on the brakes.
- The fork legs, the caliper and the mudguard move with the wheel again: they hang on the model's wheel_FL, which is the lost wheel of the front pair since the motorcycle has two wheels; the lost wheel now goes with the live one.

**Motorcycles (a prototype)**
- A car with `"type": "motorcycle"` is a motorcycle: two wheels (a pair at each end in the physics), a rider that keeps the bike up and leans it into the corner (`lean = atan(a / g)`), steering that gets smaller with the speed, and the bodywork that may lean over the road. The first one is the **Honda CBR 600 RR** (`web/cars/honda-cbr600`, a model made by `tools/make-cbr600.py`): 270 kg with the rider, 118 hp to 15 500 rpm, six gears, a fork of 17 kN/m and a shock of 24 kN/m. On the flat pad it holds 12 m/s at leans of 20 to 47 degrees, brakes from 90 km/h in 36 m (0.96 g) and goes 0 to 90 km/h in 4.5 s. See `MOTORCYCLE.md`.
- **The CBR was hard to ride with the keyboard** (a straight line was fine, but braking or steering threw it round): the steering assist of the keyboard turned the front wheel into every wobble (off for a bike now); the steering was an angle that at speed was several g of sideways acceleration (now a wish for a bend, cut to 0.9 g, with a short delay); the engine braking dragged the rear wheel at 30 % slip when the throttle was shut (0.3 now); a hard brake lifted the rear wheel and let the rear brake lock (the brakes now follow the load of the wheels); and the rider damps the yaw that was not asked for. On the circuit and on the pad: braking from 25 to 33 m/s, with and without a key held for a bend, stays straight or on its lean (28 to 35 degrees), without a swing.
- **The motorcycle is a controller of its own** (`web/js/Motorcycle.js`), and the cars do not go through it: the keys, the stick or the wheel are a lean command (the same at every speed, a held key keeps its lean; before, the lean fell back while the key was held and was small at speed), the rider follows it in 0.16 s, the front wheel is turned to what that lean needs and first goes the other way to start it (countersteering), a leaned tyre pushes the bike into the bend (camber thrust), the throttle stands it up a little in a corner, the front brake is let off only at the edge of a stoppie, and the camera rolls with the lean. On the circuit at 10, 20 and 30 m/s a held key gives about 50 degrees in 1.3 s and the bike is upright 0.75 s after the key is let go; the physics check of the mustang and the stadium truck is as it was.
- **The crash camera of a motorcycle sits on a circle of 9 m round the bike**, on the far side from the rider (the bike always between the camera and the rider), 3 m up, and it moves smoothly as the two slide apart; the field of view opens to keep both in the picture.
- **Car editor: a motorcycle turned 90 degrees and lost its wheels on *Show the shell* or *Edit nodes*.** The test bench gave the bike the track of its model, 0 m (the game gives it 4 cm), which is a division by zero in the physics: every step was not a number, the car was put back at heading 0 and the wheels had no place. The bench gives a bike 4 cm now.
- **A crash on a motorcycle.** When the bike is nearly on its side (past 69 degrees of lean), or it is hit (a change of speed of more than 2.8 g, or more than 2.2 g sideways at speed: a wall, a kerb), the rider lets go: the bike is a body that slides and tumbles on the road (no flying: its speed only goes down, it does not spin up), the rider is thrown off as a loose body that tumbles and slides to a stop (the limbs in their rest pose; not a jointed ragdoll yet), the camera stays where it was and looks at the two of them (the field of view widens so that both stay in the picture), and the keys do nothing. **Reset** puts the bike back at the saved place and **Repair** puts it on its wheels where it lies, both with the rider on it and the camera of the game again.
- **Wheelie control** for a motorcycle (menu, tab Car, on by default): the rider rolls the gas off as the front wheel goes light and keeps it at about a tenth of its load. A bike with a lot of torque from low revs (the CBR with a torque curve of 60 Nm from 500 rpm) looped over backwards at full throttle from a standstill; with the control it pulls away at full throttle, 0 to 90 km/h in 5.3 s, the front wheel just off the road. Off, it loops as before.
- **The replay shows the rider as he rode**: the hang-off, the knee out and the foot on the road at a standstill come from the recording (the lean of the body, the speed and the pedals); they were not in the replay at all.
- **At a standstill the rider puts the right foot down** (flat on the road beside the bike) and the bike rests against it, 7 degrees to the right (`motorcycle.foot_lean_deg`); on the gas the foot comes back to the peg in two seconds and the bike comes upright with it. Not while the bike is pushed back.
- **The instability of a motorcycle at speed is found and fixed.** A replay of the circuit (the long straight after the first bend, 150 to 165 km/h, a lean to the right and the throttle shut) showed the tail stepping out and the bike going round. In the game, with the gear and the wheels of the test set right (the earlier tests started every run in first gear at 40 m/s with the wheels standing still, which dragged the rear tyre for half a second and hid the real behaviour, so some of the earlier findings were wrong): the same lean with the throttle open was fine, with the throttle shut the yaw ran away. The cause: the bike was a car with a pair of tyres at each end. The sideways force of a bend came from the slip angles of the tyres, in proportion to their loads, so whichever end carried the weight did the turning: the throttle shut (or the brake) loads the front, the front tyre then turned the bike and the light rear could not hold it. The camber thrust, the force of a leaned tyre that does most of the turning of a real bike, was one force at the centre of gravity, which neither turns nor rights anything.
  - The camber thrust is now 0.85 of load × tan(lean) (it was 0.3 × sin), at the **contact patch of every wheel**, shared front and rear as the weight of the bike at rest (so the brake and the gas do not turn it into a yaw), and it rights the bike as the real thing does. The slip angles of the tyres stay small and the loads of the ends no longer decide the line.
  - The front wheel points along the bend (`delta = L g tan(lean) / u²`, the whole of it) and the rider **holds the yaw rate that the lean gives** (`g tan(lean) / u`) with a torque about the vertical (it was damped towards the steering angle, and only with the front wheel in the air towards the lean).
  - On the brake and upright the front wheel **castors** into its own slip angle (`front_comply` 3: it keeps a quarter of it), so a front that carries 90 % of the weight does not push the nose round after a bump or a kick: a stop from 155 km/h over the crest of the start straight with a kick of 0.5 m/s stays straight (heading 1 degree; it went round 90 degrees before). Not in a bend and not off the brake, where it made the yaw hunt.
  - The rider's roll torque is about the bike's own axis, not the horizontal one (on a slope a part of it was a yaw).
  - Measured, each three times with the same result: a lean of 36 degrees to the right at 45 m/s with the throttle shut: the bike turns at 1.6 g, slides 0.9 m/s at most and comes back upright (it went round before); the same at 50 and 35 m/s; the brake in a bend of 42 degrees at 25 m/s holds the lean; left-right-left every 0.4 s at 22 m/s: 16 degrees per second of yaw at most; from 40 degrees left to 40 right in 0.85 s at 12 m/s and 0.91 s at 25; a lean of 42 degrees is 1.2 g; a stop from 108 km/h at 0.88 g, straight.
- **Lean only is the standard steering of a motorcycle** (menu: *Motorcycle steering*). With *Countersteer + lean* the countersteering is **off while the brake is on**, and it comes back slowly after the brake: nothing for 1 s, then in over 1.5 s (`counter_wait`, `counter_ramp`; tested: gain 0 on the brake, 0.11 after 1.3 s, 1 after 2.7 s). What keeps a bike straight in a hard stop is not that pulse but the rider steering out a lean that came from a bump or a crest: that is now a part of the rider that is always there, on a straight (lean asked for below 2 to 6 degrees), in both modes (the stop over the crest with a kick of 0.3 to 0.5 m/s stays straight in both: heading 1 degree, sideways speed 0.4 m/s at most; in lean only without it the bike turned 85 degrees).
- **Lean is limited by the grip at speed** (`grip_g` 1.05, `fast_from` 38 m/s): the lean that the keys can ask for is cut by the circle of grip (what the speed that is going away, from the engine, the air or the brake, takes from the grip that is left for the sideways) and, from 38 m/s, goes down to 18 degrees at 60 m/s. A replay showed the bike that was steered to the left at 190 km/h with the gas off, which first went left and then slid out to the right. In my tests on that straight it slides less (from 40 m/s and slower the steady slide is 1.5 to 4 degrees), but at 45 m/s and at 50 m/s with the gas open it still slid 11 to 17 degrees: not solved there.
- **A motorcycle that is braked hard at speed no longer breaks away**: a replay of a hard stop at 41 m/s over a crest (the circuit, the start straight) showed the tail swinging out from a lateral speed of 0.2 m/s to 17 m/s in 0.6 s, with no wheel locked: the rear tyre had no load left (the front brake was only let off when the rear wheel was almost in the air), so nothing held the tail. The front brake is now let off earlier (from 28 % of the load of the rear wheel to 10 %, a rear wheel with about 6 to 12 % of the weight left instead of 1 %). Braking at that spot with a kick of 0.3 to 0.5 m/s or a yaw rate of 0.1 rad/s stays straight (the sideways speed at most 0.4 m/s; before 19.8 m/s and 85 degrees); a straight stop from 43 m/s takes 3.4 s.
- **Two settings for a motorcycle in the menu** (Esc, tab Car, only with a motorcycle): *Motorcycle steering*: **Countersteer + lean** or **Lean only** (without the short steer the other way at the start of a lean), and *Motorcycle follow camera*: **Motorcycle lean chase** (the picture rolls with the bike) or **Classic chase** (as a car).
- **A video editor** (`VideoEditor.js`, `CameraTrack.js`; the button *Video editor* in the bar of a replay, or *Edit* in the list of Replays): a page with a **source monitor** on the left (the replay with a scrubber, play, and In and Out markers: I and O) and a **program monitor** on the right (the result, 1280 x 720, the same picture as the game), and a timeline with a ruler, the clips and a camera lane. *Insert at playhead* puts the part between the markers where the playhead is (a clip there is cut in two), *Add to end* after the last clip. A clip has a speed (0.05 to 8) and **keys for a ramp** (the speed changes smoothly between them), it can be split, moved and deleted. The **camera track** uses the free camera of the replay (WASD, mouse look, zoom) with the toggles *Parent to car* and *Point at car*; *Key here* puts a key at the playhead and the camera moves smoothly between the keys; without keys the camera of the game follows the car. At the top right: **Save as replay** (a new replay in the list of Replays, cut and with the speeds applied, with the camera track in it; the smoke, the skid marks and the damage are not in it) and **Save as mp4** (the program recorded in real time, without sound; with a webm browser the server makes the mp4 with ffmpeg). Both ask for a name. A replay with a camera track plays it in the viewer too (the free camera takes over when it is chosen). Keys: Space play, Shift+Space the source, I and O, Home and End, the arrows one frame (Shift: a second), Delete, K a key (in the free camera).
- **A motorcycle slides less when the keys go from left to right quickly**: the front wheel was turned to what the lean was going to be (0.1 s ahead), so with the keys going to and fro the front wheel pushed the bike sideways against the lean that it had. The front wheel is turned to the lean that the bike has now: with the keys changing every 0.4 s at 22 m/s the sideways speed of the bike went from 1.8 to 1.1 m/s and the yaw rate from 40 to 20 degrees per second; the quick change from left to right (0.7 s) is the same.
- **Choose your car goes on with one click**: a click on a car takes you to the next step (it was a click and Next, or a double click). The Next button is still there for when you come back to the step.
- **A motorcycle steers by its lean with the front wheel in the air**: in a wheelie the front wheel has no say, so the rider takes the bike round at the rate that its lean gives (`g tan(lean) / speed`); the front wheel in the air no longer fights it (on a wheelie with the key to the left held, 41 degrees of lean and 40 to 70 degrees per second of yaw).
- **A motorcycle is kept square to the road**: the rider balances against the mean normal of the road under the wheels (smoothed over 0.15 s; the sky in the air), not against the sky. On a bank or over a crest the bike no longer stands upright in the world and slides down it. The rear brake comes back slowly (0.3 s) after the rear wheel was off the road, so that it does not lock when it lands. (Tested on the flat and with hops while braking; I could not test on a real bank.)
- **The keys < and > (comma and full stop) put the rider back or forward** while they are held (a bike): back is sitting up with the weight to the rear, forward is flat on the tank. They are in the settings menu ("Motorcycle: rider back / forward"); > was the reverse key of a car, which a bike does not use.
- **A motorcycle steered the wrong way at a standstill and at a walking pace** (below about 4 m/s the angle of the front wheel was the key the other way round; it is the same way as the key now, and a bend to the left is a left turn at every speed).
- **The offsets of the hands and the feet of a rider were not used in the game**: the car editor can take a driver for a car (a steering wheel) before the bike has loaded, and then the pivots were saved as places in the body (`hands.left`, `feet.left`) that a rider ignores, with offsets of 0. A driver now looks again for the grips and the pegs until it has found them, and a rider whose offsets are all 0 and whose old pivots are within 35 cm of the grips and the pegs gets them as offsets (so the hands and the feet stay where they were set).
- **A motorcycle has no reverse**: full braking to a stop and holding the brake key turned on the automatic reverse, and the bike drove backwards with its rear wheel in front, which broke away at once. Now holding the brake at a standstill for 0.6 s (or the reverse key, or R of the selector) makes the rider **push the bike back** at about 1.2 m/s while he sits on it: the feet leave the pegs and shuffle one after the other on the road, and when the key is let go it stops. The gas key rides off as always.
- **The look-at point of the follow camera can be moved in the car editor**: a second dot (light green) on the layer "1 Follow camera: looks at", with a gizmo for the height and for how far ahead of the car it looks (`cameras.follow.look_z`, 0 as it was; negative: behind the middle). The thumbnail shows what the camera sees.
- **The rider of a motorcycle is a mass that moves** (`motorcycle.rider_mass`, 80 kg, there with or without a driver model; car.json `mass` is now the bike without the rider, the CBR has 195 + 80 kg): back on a hard brake (a stoppie is harder to make), flat and forward on the throttle (a wheelie is harder to make, and less drag: `dragScale`, 0.7 flat to 1.35 sitting up), and off the seat to the inside of a bend (0.3 m: the bike leans less for the same bend, `tan(lean of the whole) = tan(lean of the bike + atan(rider share * hang off / height of the centre of gravity))`, about 8 degrees at full hang off, so it corners at 1.4 to 1.5 g at a bike lean of 51 degrees). The weight of the rider, where he is, and the acceleration of the bike (the apparent weight) are a torque about the pitch and the yaw of the bike. The driver model follows: lying flat on the tank, sitting up, the body over to the inside, and the knee on the inside goes out and down to the road and the other one out a little. The main menu shows the weight with the rider.
- **A quicker, braking-in-a-bend motorcycle**: the steering keys bring the lean twice as fast (`key_steer_rate`), the rider follows in 0.1 s and holds with a stiffer spring: from a lean of 40 degrees to the left to 40 to the right takes 0.75 s at every speed (it was 1.3 s). **The rider is a ragdoll in a crash**

He comes to rest and lies still. Before, he was one stiff body that tumbled.

- **The limp rider (the key 9) gets no help at all**: the front wheel is let go and comes straight, no castor, no balance, no throttle; the tyres and the lean decide where the bike goes, it leans over within a second or two at any speed (also at a walking pace: no direct fall any more) and past 40 degrees, or once it has stopped, it is a body that slides, as in a crash.

- A reset at speed (the bike put back at once) is not a hit: the hit that crashes is a loss of speed, not a change of place.
- **A burnout**: the brake and the throttle together at a standstill (or nearly) hold the front brake and let the rear brake off, so the rear wheel spins (it sat at 4 000 rpm with both wheels held).
- **Reset puts the engine at idle with the clutch open** (also for a car): it kept its speed, and in gear it spun the wheels up again the moment the car was put down.
- **A wheelie past the balance point is not a crash**: the lean (the roll) is now how far the bike's own left axis dips below the road, so a wheelie or a stoppie reads 0 degrees of lean however steep it is (measured from the up of the bike it read 180 degrees past the vertical, and that was a crash at once). The tail scrapes and the rear brake can still bring it down; it is a crash once the bike is on its back (its up more than 100 degrees from the road).
- The foot comes up once the bike itself moves at 7 km/h, or when the nose is up more than 30 degrees (a wheelie), in about a second: not on the throttle (it came up the moment the throttle was opened) and not on the speed of the rear wheel, so it stays down in a burnout. - A burnout goes on: the traction control and the wheelie control stay out of it, and a tyre that spins a lot faster than the road melts and gives less (from 4 m/s of spin to a little over half at 14; a locked wheel or a slide is not this). ### Track editor

### Physics, tyres and engine

**The shell tyre**
- **The shell is the tyre of every car.** The older cylinder body without the air pressure (TyreBody.js) is gone with the *Tyre model* setting (`tyres.model` in car.json is not read any more). The ring of nodes (NodeTyre.js, tyre model "nodes") and the 2D physics of a track are gone, with the ray tyre of the old physics, the *Tyre of nodes* settings and the `?physics=2d` switch; a track that says `"physics": "2d"` runs with the 3D physics. The test benches (the car editor, tools/physics-bench.js) run on a flat road with the triangles the tyre needs (`OpenRacer.FlatGround`).
- *Tyre: nodes round the wheel* is what the tyre gets, from 4 (a square) to 72; the shell doubled the setting and took at least 48, so the slider did nothing below 24. Fewer nodes cost less (the road is felt at four places between each two nodes); the settings are named *Tyre: ...* instead of *Tyre cylinder: ...* and *Tyre shell (experiment): ...*.
- The places between two nodes where the road is felt lie on the arc of the tyre, not on the chord between the nodes, and they are about 3 cm apart whatever the number of nodes: a tyre of 8 nodes rolls as round as one of 36 (the Mustang: steady loads, 0.14 ms per wheel per step against 0.4 with 36 nodes); with 4 nodes the load still comes in lumps as the corners pass.
**Tyre shell (experiment)**
- **Camber and toe on the shell tyre, and a tyre bench.** The tyre bodies (cylinder and shell) get the camber of the wheel: its static camber less what the roll of the body takes (and a bent knuckle), as the ring of nodes had it; the up of the tyre and its axle lean about its forward axis, so the inside shoulder carries more and a leaning shell tyre pushes to the side it leans to (camber thrust, a twentieth of the cornering stiffness per radian). The wheel of the model leans with the camber (it only leaned when it was bent). Toe was already the steering angle of the wheel. `node tools/tyre-bench.js` runs a tyre model alone on a test rig and holds the figures against a real passenger car tyre (see the README): the Mustang's shell tyre now gives 17 mm of deflection and 300 N/mm at 2.2 bar (a harder tyre goes in less, which it did not), a slip stiffness of 15 per unit slip (4.6 before: the tread was capped for the step of the wheel), a cornering stiffness of 14 per radian, peaks of 0.95 at 30 % slip and 0.98 at 10 degrees, and settles with wheels of 20 to 90 kg on it.
- **Shell tyre: what the bench found and the fixes.** The bristles of a node kept their bend off the road and the lateral force stepped up after one turn of the wheel: they are straight again when a node leaves the road. A car tyre has a flat crown now (a motorcycle tyre a round one): the outer rings never touched before. The carcass goes slack at 1.8 times the static deflection (`carcass` 0; a value in mm as before, up to 80 in the menu), which gives the tyre the car's tyre rate and lets the stiffness follow the pressure. The torque about the hub was taken from the hub of the start of the step while the rim ran along the path of the hub. The shell turns the wheel itself in its substeps with the torque of the tread, and the car adds the engine and the brake (the grip saturates within millimetres of turn, which the 3 ms step of the car cannot follow: a wheel at a standstill was thrown between the two limits of the grip at 4 rad/s). The car's `tyre_damping` damps the hub against the road (it held the hop of a wheel on the tyre; the smoothing of the load is 1.5 ms). The dampers of the beams and of the shear across the wheel are lower (they were a rolling resistance that grew with the speed), the shear along the wheel is damped more (it is what holds the wheel against the tread at a standstill). Still open: the rolling resistance grows with the speed (Crr 0.01 at 18 km/h, 0.04 at 54, 0.10 at 108), the relaxation length is short (0.09 m), the patch is long; see the README.
- The shell tyre no longer spins and squeals at a standstill (the stadium truck, the Mustang): the delay of the force filter (6 ms) now holds only for the load, not for the friction and the torque (a delay on the stiff grip of a wheel made it ring), the bristles that hold give the wheel their damping and stiffness to take implicitly (`dampSlope`), and the shear of the tread is limited to a quarter of what a wheel can follow in one step (a half before). At a standstill the wheels of the truck turn 0.15 rad/s against 4 to 5 before.
- *Tyre cylinder: nodes round the wheel* can be set down to 4 (it was 8).
- The shell tyre no longer stutters near a rail or a wall: every node was tested against every triangle near the wheel in every substep, which with the many small triangles of a rail came to millions of tests per step. The triangles are now sorted once per step (per block of nodes, then per node) and nodes that nothing can reach are not tested at all: 22 ms against 0.8 ms per step for a wheel next to 400 triangles, with the same loads. A shell wheel on open road costs about 0.7 ms per step (the cylinder 0.15 ms), so it is still heavier than the cylinder.
- Tyre model *Shell* (`tyres.model` "shell", *Tyre model* in the tuning menu, *Inner shell* and *Carcass softness* beside it, `tyres.shell.inner_ratio` and `carcass`): an outer shell of free nodes and an inner shell that follows the rim, joined by beams (cables that pull and are taut by the air pressure, belt and shear springs, a bump stop). The pressure (bar, per axle) pushes the outer nodes out and the rim back, so it changes the patch, the load and the deformation; the debug view shows both shells, the strain of the beams, the contact and the pressure in bar and psi. The road is felt at four places between each two nodes, and the force to the car passes a filter of 6 ms, which keeps the fast modes of the tyre from ringing through a light wheel (the Mustang flipped between 7000 and 0 N per frame without them). Checked on the Mustang, the stadium truck and the sports motorcycle: loads at rest and under acceleration and braking match the cylinder tyre. The tyre of the wheel model deforms too (`TyreDeform.js`): the vertices of the tread are pushed in by the vertex shader as far as the outer nodes of the shell are, flat under the load and out where the air pushes, and the rim stays as it is (no change to the model; `OpenRacer.TyreDeform.gain` in the console draws it deeper, for a look).

**3D physics**
- The road is no longer one height per place. Every triangle of the track is in a 3D grid, the wheels feel the road with rays along their own axis, and the body is a cloud of spheres, so a loop, a road on a wall or a bridge works: a car with enough speed goes up the wall of a loop, over the top upside down and lands on the exit.
- The collision shape of a car is made from its model when the car loads (about 40 to 90 spheres), instead of a box.
- A wall counts where the car is lower than its top, measured along the car; the seams between triangles do not push the car; only a car that goes into a wall is hit hard.
- It is the default for every track. A track can keep the old physics with `"physics": "2d"` in `track.json` (or `?physics=2d`).

**Tyres**
- The repair key (Backspace) now also puts the car back on its wheels where it is: the same place and heading, the roof up, 30 cm above the road, not moving (a car that lies on its roof or its side). Tested with a kart on its roof: after the key it stands again on its wheels with the normal loads.
- The classic tyre (the slip curves, Pacejka's magic formula) is gone: the tyre body of nodes and beams (the *cylinder*) is the tyre of every car. *Tyre model* has two choices now, *Cylinder* (the default, also for `tyres.model` "classic" or nothing in an older car.json) and *Ring of nodes* (the simple ring, used on tracks with 2D physics, which have no 3D road for the cylinder).
- Tyre of nodes: a car that slid off a ramp kept rocking on its tyres (yaw of ±5°, creeping at 0.25 m/s) for as long as it stood: the damping near a standstill was too low for the whole car (the yaw as well as the car as a share of its tyres). It is doubled (`restDamping` 2); after the same drop (taken from a replay) the yaw is 0° and the car stands (0.017 m/s), with the same classic result. 0 to 40 km/h on the flat takes 1.25 s instead of 1.23 s.
- Tyre model *Cylinder* (experimental, `tyres.model` "cylinder" in car.json, *Tyre model* in the tuning menu and the car editor): the tyre is one body of nodes and beams, a rounded cylinder (rings of nodes across the tread, the outer rings lower on the shoulder, nodes round the wheel, `tyres.shell`: `width`, `rings`, `segments`; the tuning table has them as *Tyre cylinder*), and it replaces the single ring and the slip curve. The nodes and the places on the beams between them are tested against every triangle of the track (road, kerb faces, the sides of ramps, walls), so the tyre does not go through the side of a ramp; they are pushed back by springs (the stiffness and the pressure of the tyre) and drawn flattened where they touch. Every node in contact holds the surface with a bristle (spring and damper, up to the friction of the surface times what presses it, with a Stribeck fall), so the grip, the combined slip, the stick at a standstill and the way the tyre lets go come out of the nodes; the sums go to the wheel (the load along the suspension, the forces along the road, and what presses across the suspension, such as the side of a ramp, to the car at the hub). The debug view (0) draws the nodes and beams per wheel (green where a node holds, red where it slides). On the openracer-frame car: loads on the tyres as with classic (3032 and 2440 N), 0 to 100 km/h in 3.2 s against 3.4 s, braking from 100 km/h in 74 m against 71 m, 0.45 g against 0.44 g after 1.5 s of steering at 40 km/h, and no rocking after a drop from a ramp. It is not the default yet, and it costs about 0.8 ms per frame against 0.15 ms; the visible tyre model does not deform yet, and the tyre cylinder cannot be set graphically in the car editor yet, only by its numbers.
- Tyre of nodes: a node keeps its deflection when it leaves the contact patch and relaxes only as the wheel rolls (it was set to zero), so the patch that moves a little with the load no longer lets a parked car creep. Tyre damage needs a harder hit (5 times the static load) after a bounce off a post punctured the rear tyres.
- A repair key (Backspace, *Esc → Controls → Other → Repair the car where it stands*, and a 🔧 button on touch screens) repairs the car where it stands: the dents of the shell, the parts that came off, the wheels and the punctured tyres. *Back to the start* (R) now repairs the tyres too (it repaired the body only).
- Tyre of nodes: a car at a standstill no longer rocks on its tyres. The spring of the nodes and the mass of the car rang with too little damping when the car stood still (it showed 1.1 m/s and 1.4 rad/s of rocking, and went away once the car rolled); near a standstill and while the wheel hardly slips the damping is now a critical one for the share of the car that the tyre carries, fading out with speed and slip (rocking 0.007 rad/s). 0 to 100 km/h is 13.6 s with both tyre models.
- The tyre of nodes has a carcass now: every tread node is a radial spring that the road presses in by the round of the tyre minus the height of the road under it (the wheel already measures the road at five places along the tyre), and the load is shared over the nodes by how deep they are pressed in. The patch comes out of the stiffness and the pressure of the tyre, and a kerb or a bump under a part of the patch moves the load to the nodes on it. 0 to 100 km/h 13.17 s and braking from 100 km/h 44.3 m on the Hyundai i30, as before (13.19 s, 44.9 m).
- Tyre damage (*Tyre damage: On* in the tuning menu, `tyres.damage` in car.json): a hard hit (a load of more than 5 times the static one on a tyre, one hit in three or so) can puncture it; the tyre leaks and loses its pressure, so its grip, its sidewall stiffness and its vertical stiffness fall, and with the tyre of nodes the contact patch grows. `vehicle.puncture(wheel)` and `vehicle.repairTyres()` do it by hand. The patch of the tyre of nodes now follows the vertical stiffness of the tyre (and so its pressure). Beams between the carcass nodes (a sidewall that deforms) are not done yet.
- An experimental tyre model made of nodes (*Tyre model: Nodes* in the tuning menu, `tyres.model` in car.json): a ring of tread nodes with springs and friction instead of slip curves. The default stays the classic model.

**Light vehicles**
- The joints of the suspension in the collision shell (the lower ball joint and the arms) scale with a wheel smaller than 0.3 m: those of a car wheel stood in the road under a kart and held back its weight and its speed (12 contacts with the road at rest: a kart took 8.8 s to 30 km/h, now 5.6 s).
- A vehicle under 600 kg (a kart) gets light wheels and a softer tyre: the wheels are 2.85 % of the mass (instead of 35 kg each), the tyre stiffness and damping go with the square root of the mass over 1230 kg, and the wheel inertia with the mass and the square of the diameter (a car wheel of 1.6 kg m² made the wheels most of the mass of a kart: a kart needed three times the power to get going).
- The automatic clutch starts to grip at `drivetrain.clutch_start_rpm` and grips fully at `clutch_full_rpm` (1050 and 2000 by default, also in the tuning table): a kart with a centrifugal clutch and one gear lets the clutch slip until it locks at its engagement speed.

**Engine**
- A turbo with a boost pressure in bar: the power follows the pressure (pressure ratio, compressor and intercooler efficiency, exhaust back pressure); the older share of torque stays for cars without it.

**Gearbox**
- The automatic gearbox shifts up when the engine is near the rev limiter with the wheels spinning faster than the road (a lot of power in a low gear): before, it only looked at the speed of the road and stayed on the limiter until the throttle was lifted. After such a shift it does not kick down for three seconds.

**Debug view**
- The 0 key (also on the numeric keypad) shows what the physics sees: the triangles near the car (road, walls and the rest), the collision spheres, the nodes and beams of the shell, the contact of every wheel and the tread nodes of the tyre of nodes. See `DebugView.js`.

**Performance**
- A wall of tyres no longer drops the frame rate when the car drives close to it: the physics tested every triangle near the car (a tyre has 832, so 15,000 and more within 2 m) against every sphere of the car, 740 ms per step on Kartbaan Oldenzaal. Small props of 200 triangles and more (less than 1.5 m across) now hit the car as their box (12 triangles); the physics triangles of that track went from 2.4 million to 42 thousand, and a step next to the wall takes about 2.6 ms. What you see is unchanged.
- Decor that is only drawn (tyre walls, trees) is merged per material and per 40 m cell when a track is loaded, so a track with thousands of loose parts needs a few hundred draw calls instead of thousands (Kartbaan Oldenzaal: 3852 parts in 68 groups). The collision and the road are unchanged.

## 2.1.0 (2026-10-19)

**Replay**
- Everything is recorded from the first frame of the game: all cars, thirty times a second. Press Esc and choose **Replay** to watch it.
- A slider over the whole recording, rewind (backwards), pause, play, jumps of ten seconds, and the speed from 0.1x to 5x.

Everything is smoothed, and a slider (0.1 to 3) sets the speed.
- **Save replay** writes a `.openracer-replay` file and **Open a replay** in the main menu plays one that someone sent you. The file is checked, says what is missing (the track and the cars are not in the file), and nothing goes to a server.

**Fixes**
- The end and the sides of a ramp were walls for a car that was above them. A wall now only counts where the car is lower than its top.
- The sky trembled: in some frames it stood a few metres beside the camera. It is now centred on the camera in every render.
- An error on loading a game, before the wheels of the car had loaded.
