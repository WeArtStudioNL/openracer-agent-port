# Changelog

What is new in every version of OpenRacer. The version is `OpenRacer.VERSION` in `web/js/Version.js`.

## Unreleased

**Look and menu**
- **Still pictures of the cars in the menu**: the turning previews that loaded slowly are a picture of each car, from the front at an angle (`preview.webp` in the folder of the car, made once by the menu and kept in the browser too; the car editor takes it away when the car is saved again). The first time takes a moment, after that the menu shows them at once, and nothing is drawn while you choose.
- The menu is a few screens, one choice each, and starts with the OpenRacer logo as on openracer.eu: **Play** (and Garage, Replays, Create, Settings under it), then *Free drive / Race / Skidpad*, the car, the track and, for a race, its options (laps, opponents, difficulty as buttons, the rest under *More options…*), with *Back* and *Next* and a line of what you chose. The page of 1900 px with all the choices and eight buttons is gone; the editors are one *Create* window, and the skidpad is a way to play. Enter goes on, Esc goes back.
- One look everywhere: `web/theme.css` has the colours of openracer.eu (dark, orange accent); the menu, the windows, the settings, the tuning menu, the replay bar, the results and the two editors use them, and the logo mark is in the editors too.

**Race**
- **Objects in the track editor**: an *Objects* card where **+ Add a model** takes a glb of your own (a barrel, a block, a tree; its origin is its base), *Place object* puts it on the road with a click and a drag for the heading, and every model is *rigid* (sleeps until a car hits it, with an optional mass in kg) or not (it stays). The glb goes in the folder of the track (`obj-<name>.glb`) and the objects in `track.json` (`objects` with `model`); the objects of the file that were written by hand stay. Saving a track under another id kept no `model` before; it does now.
- **Video as mp4**: the replay video is recorded as H.264 mp4 where the browser can (Chrome 126 and up, Safari); else as webm, and the local server (`tools/serve.py`) turns it into mp4 with ffmpeg (`POST /api/convert/mp4`; the webm is kept when ffmpeg is not there).
- Checkboxes are orange squares without a tick, in the game, the menu and the editors.
- **Rigid bodies in tracks** (`RigidObjects.js`): barrels, boxes, cones and balls that lie asleep and cost nothing until a car (the player's or an AI car) comes near and touches them; then they are simulated (Rapier, loaded only for a track that has them), they push back on the car (a wall of boxes slows a truck, a cone does not), and they go to sleep again after 1.5 s without moving. They come from the model of the track (every part whose name starts with `rigid`, a convex hull, a mass from its box or from `rigid_50kg` in the name) and from `objects` in track.json (shape, place, size, mass, colour, a `grid` and `layers` for a block or a stack). The Desert has barrels at the edge of the road, a wall of boxes and a slalom of cones. They are not in a replay yet.
- The **fixed camera** is gone: the cameras are Follow, Cockpit and Free (a saved choice of the fixed one becomes Follow; the replay bar has no *Fixed* button any more). The **free camera** starts 10 m higher than the camera that it comes from. The car editor has no fixed camera any more (the cameras are numbered Follow 1 and Cockpit 2), `chase` is no longer in `car.json` or in the defaults (an old file that has it is read all the same).
- **The start of a race**: it waits for the loading screen to go and for every AI car to have loaded (so they stand on the grid and do not pop in), then the camera flies along the grid from the front to the back (6 seconds; Enter, Space or a click skips it), then the view of the player comes back and the countdown starts. The brake and the handbrake of the grid are let go at GO (the brake stayed on before).
- **Checkpoints in the game**: a track can show its gates (two posts, a bar and a strip over the road at every gate; the finish line white, the next gate green, the ones passed dim, the others orange). It is a checkbox of the track editor, *Show the checkpoints in the game* (`show_gates` in track.json; on for a new track, off for a track that has it not). The editor saves the height of every gate now. `GateMarkers.js`.
- With *Show the checkpoints in the game* on: an **arrow at the top of the screen** points at the next gate (as the camera looks) with the distance in m or ft, and a gate gives a sound: a rising double beep for the right gate, a low buzz for another gate (a gate that was passed already is no mistake). Made in the browser, follows the sound option and the volume; the arrow follows the HUD option.
- **Photo** (F2, a key you can change): saves the picture of the game as a PNG (the canvas only, so without the HUD; frame it with the free camera).
- A **loading bar** over the game while the track, the car and the AI cars load, and a first-run card in the main menu (*Drive*: the stadium truck on the Desert; *Close* or Drive hides it for good, `openracer.welcomed` in the browser).
- Performance: a **draw distance** in the graphics settings (500, 1000, 2000 or 5000 m; the presets have 500, 1000, 2000 and 5000, High is 2000): the far plane of the camera, with the sky shrunk to stay inside it. AI cars more than 450 m from the camera are not drawn and their model (wheels, suspension, soft body, parts) is not moved; they drive on as before. Everything else is culled by three.js as it was (the Desert is in parts for that).
- A **physics check** page, `web/tools/physics-check.html`: pick cars and a track, and it runs the ride height, a landing from 3 m and from 10 m, the wheels on a straight at 30 m/s (flickers) and a bend (yaw against the steering), and marks what is outside the limits. Run it after a change to the physics or to a car.
- An AI car that is lost is put back: when it has been stuck for a long time (not moving with the throttle open), more than 45 m from its line, or on its roof, for 7 seconds in all, it stands on the line again, a few metres ahead and facing along it, in the state that it is in (damage and tyres stay). `Opponent.recovered` counts them. An AI car that was 104 m off the line in the Desert was back on it after 7 s.
- The Desert is dressed and cut in parts: 160 rocks (low, flat shaded stones from 0.8 to 4 m that the car drives into) and 260 cacti (only to look at, no collision), put on the sand away from the road, and the terrain is 64 parts of 250 m and the road 16 parts, so that the game draws only the parts that are in the picture (a single part of 2 km is always in it). `tools/make-desert-track.py` makes it all.
- Sound of the road and of landings (made in the browser, no files): the tyres on loose ground hiss, crunch or squelch with the speed (sand low and soft, gravel high and sharp, mud dull, grass soft), and a landing or a hard hit on the bump stop is a thud, as loud as it was hard (a drop of 4 m is 0.7). `EngineSound.setGround` and `EngineSound.impact`; quieter inside the car.
- New surfaces: **sand** (grip 0.60, sliding 0.50, a rolling resistance of 4.5 times), **gravel** (0.78, 0.66, 3 times) and **mud** (0.50, 0.44, 6 times), each a role of the track editor (the colours of the legend, and a part called sand, gravel, zand, mud and so on is given the role by itself) next to road, pit lane, grass and ground. The off-road tyre grips on all of them. The Desert has sand now.
- *Import height map* has an optional *Surface map*: a picture in which a colour is a surface (grey road, yellow sand, white gravel, brown mud, green grass, orange ground; the nearest colour counts), which makes a part of the terrain for every surface (`terrain_sand`, …) with its role. Without it the whole terrain has the surface of the list.
- Dust: a tyre that rolls on a loose surface throws up a thin plume (from 5 m/s, following the speed), a tyre that lands makes a puff that dies away in a tenth of a second (more with a harder landing), and the dust has the colour of the surface (`dust` of a surface in Helpers.js): sand, grey for gravel, dark for mud.
- *Slower at speed* is in the Keyboard tab of the settings too (it was only for the gamepad): how much slower the steering keys turn the wheels the faster the car goes, from 0 (the same at every speed, and the lock does not get smaller) to 3; 1 is how it was (6.9 times slower at 100 km/h). At 20 m/s a key held for 0.6 s turns the wheels 0.36 rad when it is off, 0.07 as it was and 0.05 with 2.
- The wheels of the stadium truck flickered up and down at speed: its tyre was a 12-sided polygon (`tyres.shell.segments` 12, taken over from an example), which let go of the road for a frame now and then, and a wheel that lost contact was drawn at the end of its travel at once (17 cm). The tyre has 36 sides now, and a wheel without contact is drawn where the suspension has it (`measureWheels`). At 45 m/s on the straight of the circuit the wheels let go of the road 33 to 48 times per 10 s with 12 sides, and 3 to 8 times with 36, which is the road and the jumps themselves.
- The tyre squeal is quieter and later: it starts from a real slide (the slip must pass 1.2, it was 0.85, and the slide must go faster than 1.2 m/s), the sound itself is silent until a fifth of the slide and then grows softly (power 1.6 instead of 1.3), it is a quarter quieter, and it comes in over 0.12 s. A half throttle start and a bend below the limit are silent now; a bend over the limit, a full throttle start and a hard stop squeal as before.
- A car whose contacts blow up is held back: a gain of more than 35 m/s in one update (over 50 m/s) keeps the speed that it had, and a spin of more than 60 rad/s is brought back to 60; it is said once in the console (`Vehicle: the speed jumped from … to …`, with the place). An AI kart in a replay of the Desert was launched from a stand-still to 100 and later 169 m/s.
- When the browser takes the graphics away (`webglcontextlost`) there is a note on the screen (*Reload the page if the picture stays wrong*) and a line in the console; a picture that went black without an error was not visible before.
- Fix for `--https`: the page could keep loading or time out, because the handshake of a connection that said nothing (a browser opens some in advance) was waited for in the loop that accepts connections, which stopped the whole server. The handshake is now made in the thread of the connection, with a time limit of 15 s.
- `start-server.sh --https` serves over https with a certificate that is made at the first start (openssl, or mkcert when it is installed, which the browser trusts): for localhost and the address on the network, in `tools/.certs` (not in git). The browser warns once about a self-signed one. With it the game can use what https asks for (the clipboard, the gamepad and the motion sensors on another device).
- `start-server.sh` serves on the local network and prints the address to open on a phone, a tablet or another computer (`--local` keeps it to this computer). The server already listened on every address; now it says where. The API that writes and deletes cars and tracks stays closed to the other computers.
- Share by link: *My garage* has a field *Link to a zip* with *Import* (the car or track of a zip somewhere on the web, as long as the site allows it) and *Copy game link*, which makes a link of the game, `?import=<link>`, that asks "Add the car or track from <site> to your garage?" for whoever opens it and then selects the new car or track. Only http and https links are taken, and the question comes first.
- The ten longest jumps of a track are kept (browser): the notice says the place (#3 here, or *Longest jump here*), and the main menu has *Jumps*, a list of them for the chosen track (distance, time in the air, height, car, date) with *Start over*.
- Jumps: when the car has been in the air for more than 0.6 s and lands, a notice shows how far it went (m), how long it was in the air and how high, and *Longest jump here* when it is the best on the track (kept per track in this browser; `JumpStats.js`).
- Ghosts as files: in the settings menu (Esc) the row *Ghost* has *Save lap* (your best lap with this car on this track as a `.openracer-ghost` file, to send) and *Race a ghost* (the lap of a file that someone sent is the ghost, in place of your own best lap; kept for the track until *Own lap*). A ghost of another track is refused with a message. The ghost is drawn with your car; there is no server for this.
- The stadium truck is easier on sand: four wheel drive (35% to the front, 65% to the rear; with rear wheel drive the 640 Nm spun the rear tyres on sand with a slip ratio of 6), 12% more grip on the rear tyres (`tyres.grip_balance` 1.12), 54% of the weight on the front and a centre of gravity of 0.78 m. At the limit on sand it now understeers a little (the yaw rate is 0.8 of what the steering asks for) instead of snapping into oversteer, and a drift at 16 m/s is caught in half a second. The off-road tyre slides with 97% of its peak grip on grass and ground (`looseSlide` in Wheel.js), so it does not fall off a cliff when it lets go.
- Hard landings: the bump stop and the droop stop damp the wheel both ways (a stop that only damped going in gave back the energy and threw the car up like a trampoline), the damper holds on better when it goes out (the blow-off past the knee is 0.7 instead of 0.35), and a tyre that is pushed in deeper than 22% of its radius gets stiffer the deeper it goes (`TyreBody.js` and the ray tyre), so a landing does not sink the wheel into the road. `chassis.unsprung_mass`, `chassis.tyre_rate` and `chassis.tyre_damping` can now be set in car.json; the stadium truck has 90 kg of wheel, 170 kN/m and 7000 Ns/m for its big tyres.
- A new track, **Desert** (web/tracks/desert): a closed road of 12 m and 3.8 km that winds through dunes (the smallest bend is 67 m), asphalt with edge lines and a dashed middle line on sand (the role ground, where the off-road tyre grips), eight jumps (crests of 3 to 4 m: the stadium truck leaves the ground for up to a second at 26 m/s), ten gates, a line for the AI, and two cinematic cameras at every jump. Made by `tools/make-desert-track.py` (numpy; run it again to change the shape, the seed is in the file).
- A new tyre compound, *Off-road (knobbly)*: about 14% less grip on tarmac than the road tyre, but 50% more on grass and ground (the surface that sand and dirt are given in the track editor) and it digs in less there (the rolling resistance on a loose surface is 0.6 times). Set as `tyres.compound: "offroad"`, in the tuning menu and in the car editor; the stadium truck has it. A compound has `looseGrip` and `looseRolling` for this (Wheel.js), and the surfaces grass and ground are `loose` (Helpers.js).
- Long travel suspension for off-road cars: `chassis.suspension_length` (the room for the travel, 0.28 m by default; it was fixed), `chassis.spring_progression` (a progressive spring: stiffer at the end of the bump travel, no change in the ride height) and `chassis.damping_rebound` (the rebound against the compression damping, 1.7 by default); they are in the tuning menu too. The example car `stadium-truck` (web/cars/stadium-truck) has 0.38 m of bump travel and a model to model around, `stadium-truck.glb` (tools/make-stadium-truck.py).
- The same for cars: the rims, the brake calipers and the number plate of a car model are pictures with an alpha channel, and are cut out too, so the calipers of the i30 are no longer drawn over the tyre and the wheel arch (`cutOut` in Helpers.js, used by TrackLoader.js and CarLoader.js).
- Pictures with an alpha channel on a track (trees, fences, signs: png textures, `alphaMode` BLEND in a glTF) are cut out instead of blended, so a tree that is behind another one is no longer drawn over it now and then (the blending depends on the order of the objects). A material that is see-through as a whole (opacity below 1, glass) stays blended.
- The reset key (R, and ↺ on a touch screen) only puts the car back at the saved position, as it is: the damage, the tyres (heat, wear, pressure) and the engine stay, and the car stands still on its wheels. The key *Save the place of the car for the reset* (4, rebindable in *Esc → Controls → Other*) saves where the car is now (the place, the heading and the height; kept per track in the browser); until one is saved the reset goes to the start. Repairing is for the repair key (Backspace).
- A value that is not a number (NaN) in the state of a car no longer kills the car and the sound (`setTargetAtTime ... non-finite` in the tyre squeal): the sound ignores such a value, and the vehicle checks its state after every update, reports the field once in the console (`Vehicle: a value is not a number (...)`) and puts the car back upright where it was last fine.
- In the race column of the main menu, a list with a tick for every car chooses which cars may race against you (shown when a race of laps or AI opponents is chosen; *all* and *none* above it; all are ticked by default and the choice is remembered). The AI cars are taken from the ticked cars in turn (`aicars` in the address); with none ticked every car may race.

**Replay**
- *● Video* in the replay bar records the replay from the start to the end, at normal speed, as a video (.webm, 8 Mbit/s, the picture of the game without the bar and without sound) and saves it when it is done; the button is *■ Stop* while it runs, and a pause or the end stops it too.
- In a replay the cameras Fixed, Follow and Cockpit follow the car that is chosen, like the Free and the Cinematic camera: Tab, or the new *Car* button in the camera row (when there are more cars), goes to the next car, and the choice holds when the camera changes. (The cockpit camera of another car is put where the cockpit of your own car is.)
- The head of the driver is drawn in a replay with the Free or the Cinematic camera, also when the camera of the game is the cockpit one (it was hidden there, as in the cockpit view).
- The Cinematic camera has a zoom of your own on top of the field of view that it chooses itself: the slider *Field of view ×* (0.2 to 3; below 1 zooms in) under the camera buttons, or `[` (in) and `]` (out). The zoom is kept when the camera goes to another one.
- A replay begins at the beginning (wound all the way back to 0:00), not at the end; one that is opened from the list of Replays plays at once, one from the game waits paused.
- Before the game goes back to the main menu after a race or practice (the *Main menu* button of the pause menu, of the race results and of the skidpad), it asks *Save the replay?* (Save replay, Don't save, Cancel) when there is a recording that was not saved. *Save replay* in the replay bar now keeps the replay in the list as well.
- **Open a replay** of the main menu is now **Replays**: a window with the saved replays (track, cars, length, date, size; *Watch*, *Download* as a `.openracer-replay` file, *Delete*) and *Import replay…* for a file that someone sent. They are kept in the browser (IndexedDB).

**Car editor**
- Make a car in one go: the main menu has *Add your car* (it opens the car editor), and a .glb that is dropped on the car editor (or picked) is set up at once: the wheels are found, the wheelbase, tracks, tyre and size are taken from them, the model is put on the floor and the name comes from the file. *Save and drive* (next to *Save the car*) saves the car and starts it on the track that was chosen last. *New car* now empties the name, the id and the description.
- The steering wheel of the model turns with the *Steering* slider of the Driver card (about the ring of the hands), so the hands and the wheel go together; it goes back to rest when the driver is switched off.
- *Mirror the + keys to the − side* (Driver card, keyframes) replaces the keys at negative degrees by the mirror image of the keys at positive degrees: left hand keys become right hand keys and the other way round, mirrored about the plane through the middle of the wheel, with the chest and the head turned the other way (y and z change sign). The key at 0 stays.
- The Parts list of the Layers panel shows only the parents, folded: an arrow in front of a part opens what is inside it (the i30: 44 rows instead of 128). It is folded again when a model is loaded, and the parents of a part that is chosen in the view open by themselves.
- In the test of the car (the physics of the game) the driver goes with the car that is tested (in its frame, as in the game) and is no longer left behind where the car stood; the nodes are hidden then, and when the test stops the driver and its nodes are back.
- A part that is chosen in the view (a click on a loose part, which opens its window in the Soft body card) is lit in the Parts list of the Layers panel, and the list scrolls to it; it goes out when the part window is closed.
- A click on the driver (the figure or one of its nodes) is for the driver only: it no longer also opens a loose part of the car behind it in the Soft body card, which started the crash test.

**Track editor**
- The track editor starts with a choice: *Open track*, *Import height map* or *New track*. *Import height map* (also the button *Height map…* in the Track card) makes a track from a 16 bit greyscale PNG (the heights: black is low, white is high) and a colour picture of the same square (any size): the size of the square in metres, the height of white in metres, how fine the mesh is (every 2nd, 4th or 8th pixel; 1024 × 1024 with every 4th is 256 × 256 points) and the surface of the whole map are set in the window. It becomes a mesh `terrain` with the colour map as its texture, saved as terrain.glb in the folder of the track, and then it is a track like any other: place the start, add gates, save. The PNG is read by the editor itself (`heightmap.js`, grey and colour, 8 and 16 bit, not interlaced), because the browser would make it 8 bit. The top of the pictures is towards -z.
- Cinematic cameras have a range: a click on a camera chooses it (it turns yellow, with a gizmo to move it), the round button above a camera shows its range as a circle on the floor with a node in the middle (moves the circle) and a node on the edge (sets the radius); the radius is a field in the list too. It is saved as `range` (`x`, `z`, `r`) of a camera, and a camera without one has a circle of 30 m round itself in the editor. In the replay the camera whose circle the car is in is used (the one that the car is nearest to the middle of, as a share of the radius), and when the car is in none the camera that is the nearest to a circle; the others rules below stay.
- Cinematic cameras: the mode *Add camera* in the view puts a camera above the place that is clicked (the height of a new one is a field in the card *Cinematic cameras*, and every camera has x, y and z fields and can be removed); they are saved as `cameras` (`x`, `y`, `z`) in track.json. In the replay a fifth camera button, *Cinematic* (only on a track that has cameras), uses the camera that is nearest to the car: it does not move, it only turns to follow the car (smoothly), it goes to another camera when that one is clearly nearer (at least 15% closer, with a cut), and its field of view is chosen so that the car stays about the same size in the picture.
- The AI racing line is drawn in the view (yellow, with arrows in the direction it is driven; *Show the line* switches it off), and can be edited with nodes: *Edit the line* shows nodes about 8 m apart (blue line while editing), a click takes a node and the gizmo moves it over the road (the height follows the road), Shift + click on the road adds a node on the nearest part of the line, *Add node* adds one after the chosen node, Delete or *Delete node* takes one away, *Apply line* saves a smooth curve through the nodes (a point every 2 m) as `racing_line` of the track, *Cancel* keeps it as it was. A designed track without a recorded line can be edited from the middle of its road.

**Light vehicles**
- The joints of the suspension in the collision shell (the lower ball joint and the arms) scale with a wheel smaller than 0.3 m: those of a car wheel stood in the road under a kart and held back its weight and its speed (12 contacts with the road at rest: a kart took 8.8 s to 30 km/h, now 5.6 s).
- A vehicle under 600 kg (a kart) gets light wheels and a softer tyre: the wheels are 2.85 % of the mass (instead of 35 kg each), the tyre stiffness and damping go with the square root of the mass over 1230 kg, and the wheel inertia with the mass and the square of the diameter (a car wheel of 1.6 kg m² made the wheels most of the mass of a kart: a kart needed three times the power to get going).
- The automatic clutch starts to grip at `drivetrain.clutch_start_rpm` and grips fully at `clutch_full_rpm` (1050 and 2000 by default, also in the tuning table): a kart with a centrifugal clutch and one gear lets the clutch slip until it locks at its engagement speed.

**Car editor**
- A driver model that faces the back of the car (a Rigify mannequin from Blender does) is turned round when it is loaded (rotation 180° about y): the editor looks at which side the left and the right shoulders are on. A model whose rotation was set by hand keeps it.
- The driver supports a Rigify rig (a glb with all the control bones, 400 bones for a mannequin): the bones that move the mesh are taken (DEF-upper_arm, DEF-forearm, DEF-hand, DEF-thigh, DEF-shin, DEF-foot, DEF-spine for the hips and DEF-spine.006 for the head), not the controls (ORG-, MCH-, _fk, _ik, tweak) or the twist bones in between, and the lists of bones show the DEF- bones first. The arm and the leg that stand on the left of the car take the left hand and the left foot whatever the model calls them (a model that faces the other way has its L on the right). Tested with a Rigify mannequin: the arms take the right bones and the hands reach their grips exactly, and the head scales away for the cockpit view.
- The driver is edited in the view: click a node to take it (the coloured balls are the hands and the feet, the small cubes the elbows and the knees, which can be moved now and are saved as `driver.poles`; *Reset the elbows and knees* goes back to the automatic ones), click the figure to take the model, click the grey ring to take the wheel of the hands. The ring has a gizmo (move W, rotate E, scale R: its size is where the hands hold) to match it to the steering wheel of the model, which stays where it is; it is saved as `driver.wheel` (`position`, `rotation` in degrees, `radius`) and the hands turn about it in the game, the same way as the steering wheel of the car turns. Without it the ring is between the two hands, or the steering wheel of the model.
- The chest and the head have keys too: with keys made, take the chest or the head and turn it (E) with the steering slider at a place: a key is made (yellow and purple marks under the slider). They turn from key to key, hold outside the first and last key, and the left and the right side of the steering have their own keys. Saved as `driver.keys.chest` and `driver.keys.head` (`t`, `rot`); without keys they turn as `driver.chest` and `driver.head` say.

- Keyframes for the hands: instead of the automatic hand over, the movement of each hand can be set by hand in the car editor (Driver card, *Keyframes of the hands*). *Make keys from the automatic movement* gives a start; then turn the Steering slider to a place (the positive side), take a hand and move it (W), turn it (E) or set its grip: a key is made at that turn (marks under the slider; click one to go there; *Delete the key here*, and *rides the wheel* for a key whose place and turn go round with the wheel like a hand on the rim, otherwise they are fixed in the car). The hand goes through the keys (a smooth curve), turns and closes its fingers from key to key, holds the first and the last key outside them, and the way back is the same movement the other way. Both sides of the steering are set by hand (the keys are at the place of the Steering slider, from -450 to 450 degrees; there is no mirroring). Saved as `driver.keys` (`left` and `right`: `t` degrees, `pos`, `rot`, `grip`, `wheel`); *Back to automatic* removes them.

- Hand grip and hand orientation: with a hand selected the Driver card has a *Grip* slider (0 open to 100% closed) that curls all the fingers of that hand (every bone below the hand except the palm; a Rigify rig curls its DEF- fingers; every joint, the thumb too, turns 65 degrees about its local x axis at full grip, like a Blender rig; a select turns it the other way (-x)), and Rotate (E) turns the hand (the small cross on the ball shows how). The turn is saved as `driver.hands.rotation` (degrees, in the space of the car), the grip as `driver.hands.grip` (0 to 1) and `driver.grip_curl`. In the game the hand turns about the middle of the wheel as it goes round it, so a hand that holds the wheel keeps the same hold. Tested with the Rigify mannequin: 15 finger bones per hand curl, the hand turns 90 degrees with the wheel.

- Hand over, changed: the right hand lets go at 130 degrees and goes by the chest to the top (as before); when it has taken the top the left hand lets go and goes in one smooth movement by the chest to the top, from there to a little before the end of the lock, and the right hand does not let go again. The hands are a function of the turn of the wheel, so the way back to the middle is exactly the same movement the other way. The car now gives the driver the angle of the steering wheel itself (a wheel that turns more than 360 degrees was read wrong from the rotation).
- The driver turns and scales about its hips (the pivot of the gizmo is in the body, not at the feet; a driver saved with the origin at the feet is converted and stays where it was). The chest and the head can be turned too: two small markers with a rotate gizmo (`driver.chest.rotation`, `driver.head.rotation`, degrees); the head stays hidden in the cockpit view.
- A driver: a *Driver* card in the car editor loads a .glb with a simple skeleton in the T pose (made in Blender), saved with the car as driver.glb (`driver` in car.json: `file`, `position`, `rotation`, `scale`, `bones`, `hands`, `feet`, `elbow_out`, `knee_out`). It is placed with gizmos: the model (move with W, turn with E, scale in a field), the two grips of the hands on the steering wheel and the two feet on the pedals; the arms and the legs follow with inverse kinematics (two bones with an elbow or knee that bends to a pole; the bones are guessed from their names, Blender, Rigify and Mixamo style, and can be set by hand). The sliders *Steering*, *Throttle* and *Brake* try it out. In the game the hands turn with the steering wheel of the car and turn a long way the way a driver does (to the right: hands at 9 and 3 o'clock; at 130 degrees the right hand lets go and goes to the top in one slow, smooth movement by the chest (out of the way of the left arm; a curve through the place where it let go, the chest and the top, that starts and ends slowly, about a second), the left hand rides on to about 4 o'clock, then the right hand takes the top (12 o'clock) while the left hand lets go (to the chest) and comes forward to the top when the right one has pulled a little, both ride on, a hand that comes to 4 o'clock takes the top again; back at the middle both go back to 9 and 3), the right foot follows the throttle and the left foot the brake along the way of the pedal, and the head is not drawn in the cockpit view. Tested with a skeleton made for the test: the lead hand is at 3 o'clock at 170 degrees and at 4 o'clock at 200 degrees, the other hand takes the top at about 210 degrees, and the feet reach the pedals exactly. AI cars have no driver yet.
- Exhaust points: one gizmo at a time (the buttons *Move* and *Rotate* in the window of the point, or the W and E keys) instead of the arrows and the rings over each other, and fields for the place (m) and the turn (degrees) that follow the gizmo and can be typed. Nothing is aligned for you.
- A texture that reads a set of texture coordinates that its mesh does not have (the badge of the Lotus Elise model says texCoord 6) no longer breaks the shader ("uv6: undeclared identifier": the part was not drawn): it falls back to the first set when the car is loaded (`CarLoader.js`).
- The test bench of the car editor loads the tyre scripts (`NodeTyre.js`, `TyreBody.js`) with the rest of the physics: without them the test crashed with "OpenRacer.NodeTyre is not a constructor". The floor of the test bench has no 3D road, so the tyre there is the ring of nodes.
- Engine sound: layouts and presets for one, two and three cylinders: *Single 0.25 L (kart)*, *Single 0.4 L*, *Twin 0.8 L (parallel, 360 degrees)* and *Inline-3 1.0 L* (the layouts *Single cylinder* and *Inline 2* are new; *Inline 3* was there without a preset). Played offline, every one gives sound without errors, at about the level of the existing presets.
- Small cars: a model with a wheelbase down to 0.6 m, tracks down to 0.4 m and wheels down to 0.1 m is accepted (a kart: 1.09 m wheelbase and 0.30 m wheels was refused: "The wheels of the model do not make a car", the limits were 1.2 m and 0.3 m). The same limits are in the game for the wheels of the model (`Car.js`).

**Debug view**
- The 0 key (also on the numeric keypad) shows what the physics sees: the triangles near the car (road, walls and the rest), the collision spheres, the nodes and beams of the shell, the contact of every wheel and the tread nodes of the tyre of nodes. See `DebugView.js`.

**Performance**
- A wall of tyres no longer drops the frame rate when the car drives close to it: the physics tested every triangle near the car (a tyre has 832, so 15,000 and more within 2 m) against every sphere of the car, 740 ms per step on Kartbaan Oldenzaal. Small props of 200 triangles and more (less than 1.5 m across) now hit the car as their box (12 triangles); the physics triangles of that track went from 2.4 million to 42 thousand, and a step next to the wall takes about 2.6 ms. What you see is unchanged.
- Decor that is only drawn (tyre walls, trees) is merged per material and per 40 m cell when a track is loaded, so a track with thousands of loose parts needs a few hundred draw calls instead of thousands (Kartbaan Oldenzaal: 3852 parts in 68 groups). The collision and the road are unchanged.

**Tyres**
- The repair key (Backspace) now also puts the car back on its wheels where it is: the same place and heading, the roof up, 30 cm above the road, not moving (a car that lies on its roof or its side). Tested with a kart on its roof: after the key it stands again on its wheels with the normal loads.
- The classic tyre (the slip curves, Pacejka's magic formula) is gone: the tyre body of nodes and beams (the *cylinder*) is the tyre of every car. *Tyre model* has two choices now, *Cylinder* (the default, also for `tyres.model` "classic" or nothing in an older car.json) and *Ring of nodes* (the simple ring, used on tracks with 2D physics, which have no 3D road for the cylinder).
- Tyre of nodes: a car that slid off a ramp kept rocking on its tyres (yaw of ±5°, creeping at 0.25 m/s) for as long as it stood: the damping near a standstill was too low for the whole car (the yaw as well as the car as a share of its tyres). It is doubled (`restDamping` 2); after the same drop (taken from a replay) the yaw is 0° and the car stands (0.017 m/s), with the same classic result. 0 to 40 km/h on the flat takes 1.25 s instead of 1.23 s.
- Tyre model *Cylinder* (experimental, `tyres.model` "cylinder" in car.json, *Tyre model* in the tuning menu and the car editor): the tyre is one body of nodes and beams, a rounded cylinder (rings of nodes across the tread, the outer rings lower on the shoulder, nodes round the wheel, `tyres.shell`: `width`, `rings`, `segments`; the tuning table has them as *Tyre cylinder*), and it replaces the single ring and the slip curve. The nodes and the places on the beams between them are tested against every triangle of the track (road, kerb faces, the sides of ramps, walls), so the tyre does not go through the side of a ramp; they are pushed back by springs (the stiffness and the pressure of the tyre) and drawn flattened where they touch. Every node in contact holds the surface with a bristle (spring and damper, up to the friction of the surface times what presses it, with a Stribeck fall), so the grip, the combined slip, the stick at a standstill and the way the tyre lets go come out of the nodes; the sums go to the wheel (the load along the suspension, the forces along the road, and what presses across the suspension, such as the side of a ramp, to the car at the hub). The debug view (0) draws the nodes and beams per wheel (green where a node holds, red where it slides). On the openracer-frame car: loads on the tyres as with classic (3032 and 2440 N), 0 to 100 km/h in 3.2 s against 3.4 s, braking from 100 km/h in 74 m against 71 m, 0.45 g against 0.44 g after 1.5 s of steering at 40 km/h, and no rocking after a drop from a ramp. It is not the default yet, and it costs about 0.8 ms per frame against 0.15 ms; the visible tyre model does not deform yet, and the tyre cylinder cannot be set graphically in the car editor yet, only by its numbers.
- Tyre of nodes: a node keeps its deflection when it leaves the contact patch and relaxes only as the wheel rolls (it was set to zero), so the patch that moves a little with the load no longer lets a parked car creep. Tyre damage needs a harder hit (5 times the static load) after a bounce off a post punctured the rear tyres.
- A repair key (Backspace, *Esc → Controls → Other → Repair the car where it stands*, and a 🔧 button on touch screens) repairs the car where it stands: the dents of the shell, the parts that came off, the wheels and the punctured tyres. *Back to the start* (R) now repairs the tyres too (it repaired the body only).
- Tyre of nodes: a car at a standstill no longer rocks on its tyres. The spring of the nodes and the mass of the car rang with too little damping when the car stood still (it showed 1.1 m/s and 1.4 rad/s of rocking, and went away once the car rolled); near a standstill and while the wheel hardly slips the damping is now a critical one for the share of the car that the tyre carries, fading out with speed and slip (rocking 0.007 rad/s). 0 to 100 km/h is 13.6 s with both tyre models.
- The tyre of nodes has a carcass now: every tread node is a radial spring that the road presses in by the round of the tyre minus the height of the road under it (the wheel already measures the road at five places along the tyre), and the load is shared over the nodes by how deep they are pressed in. The patch comes out of the stiffness and the pressure of the tyre, and a kerb or a bump under a part of the patch moves the load to the nodes on it. 0 to 100 km/h 13.17 s and braking from 100 km/h 44.3 m on the Hyundai i30, as before (13.19 s, 44.9 m).
- Tyre damage (*Tyre damage: On* in the tuning menu, `tyres.damage` in car.json): a hard hit (a load of more than 5 times the static one on a tyre, one hit in three or so) can puncture it; the tyre leaks and loses its pressure, so its grip, its sidewall stiffness and its vertical stiffness fall, and with the tyre of nodes the contact patch grows. `vehicle.puncture(wheel)` and `vehicle.repairTyres()` do it by hand. The patch of the tyre of nodes now follows the vertical stiffness of the tyre (and so its pressure). Beams between the carcass nodes (a sidewall that deforms) are not done yet.
- An experimental tyre model made of nodes (*Tyre model: Nodes* in the tuning menu, `tyres.model` in car.json): a ring of tread nodes with springs and friction instead of slip curves. The default stays the classic model.

**Engine**
- A turbo with a boost pressure in bar: the power follows the pressure (pressure ratio, compressor and intercooler efficiency, exhaust back pressure); the older share of torque stays for cars without it.

**Gearbox**
- The automatic gearbox shifts up when the engine is near the rev limiter with the wheels spinning faster than the road (a lot of power in a low gear): before, it only looked at the speed of the road and stayed on the limiter until the throttle was lifted. After such a shift it does not kick down for three seconds.

**Controls**
- Automatic gearbox: holding the brake key at a standstill (or below a speed that can be set) puts the car in reverse while it is held; the gas key brakes in reverse and is D again at a standstill.

**Camera**
- The fixed and the follow camera no longer go through walls or over a crest: three rays from the car to the camera (and to a place beside it) against the triangles of the track bring the camera in front of what is in the way; it comes in at once and goes out again smoothly.

**Drag strip**
- The Top 50 has a tab for every distance.
- A Top 50 of the drag runs in the main menu (the car with its tuning, the 1/4 and 2/4 mile times, the top speed, and the ticket of the run); the drag strip is a layer in the track editor with a gizmo for its place and its direction.
- A *Drag strip* tool in the track editor (a window for the distance and the width; the start line is where you click, the strip goes the way you drag), and in the game: the distance to the start line, the lights, false starts, and the times and speeds at the standard distances within the strip. At the finish a time slip slides in from the top at the right (a cross or Escape closes it).

**Panels**
- The tuning menu, the free camera panel, the replay bar and its speed box, and the exhaust window of the car editor can be moved (drag a part that is not a control) and scaled (the bottom right corner); where they are is kept in the browser.

**Extras**
- Optional extras with a checkbox in the tuning menu (the settings open below it): **pops and bangs** (sound and flames at the exhaust), **anti-lag** and a **boost gauge**. They are kept in car.json under `extras`.
- The exhaust points of a car are placed in the car editor (the *Exhaust* layer, with a gizmo for the place and one for the direction); the flames can be your own pictures (`web/images/flames/`).

**Launch**
- The rev limiter is a hard fuel cut with a set rate (*Cuts per second*), so the engine bounces against the limit.
- **Two-step** (Y switches it on; the clutch pedal in at a standstill, or Q on a keyboard), **transbrake** (F) and **launch control** (J, an option) with their own engine speeds in the tuning menu and the car editor; the pills LC, 2S and TB show them.

**3D physics**
- The road is no longer one height per place. Every triangle of the track is in a 3D grid, the wheels feel the road with rays along their own axis, and the body is a cloud of spheres, so a loop, a road on a wall or a bridge works: a car with enough speed goes up the wall of a loop, over the top upside down and lands on the exit.
- The collision shape of a car is made from its model when the car loads (about 40 to 90 spheres), instead of a box.
- A wall counts where the car is lower than its top, measured along the car; the seams between triangles do not push the car; only a car that goes into a wall is hit hard.
- It is the default for every track. A track can keep the old physics with `"physics": "2d"` in `track.json` (or `?physics=2d`).

## 2.1.0 (2026-10-19)

**Replay**
- Everything is recorded from the first frame of the game: all cars, thirty times a second. Press Esc and choose **Replay** to watch it.
- A slider over the whole recording, rewind (backwards), pause, play, jumps of ten seconds, and the speed from 0.1x to 5x.
- Cameras in the replay: Fixed, Follow, Cockpit, and **Free**. The free camera moves with WASD or the arrows, Shift and Control go up and down, the mouse (or the right stick) looks around, `[` and `]` zoom, F aims at the car, L fixes the camera to the car and Tab chooses the car. Everything is smoothed, and a slider (0.1 to 3) sets the speed.
- **Save replay** writes a `.openracer-replay` file and **Open a replay** in the main menu plays one that someone sent you. The file is checked, says what is missing (the track and the cars are not in the file), and nothing goes to a server.

**Fixes**
- The end and the sides of a ramp were walls for a car that was above them. A wall now only counts where the car is lower than its top.
- The sky trembled: in some frames it stood a few metres beside the camera. It is now centred on the camera in every render.
- An error on loading a game, before the wheels of the car had loaded.
