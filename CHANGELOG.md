# Changelog

What is new in every version of OpenRacer. The version is `OpenRacer.VERSION` in `web/js/Version.js`.

## 2.2.0 (2026-10-24)

Since 2.1.0 (267 commits): the track in 3D with a soft body for every car, motorcycles with a rider and a ragdoll crash, a driver in the car, replays as files with a video editor, a new menu, the drag strip, and much more. Per subject:

### Car editor: Save and test

- *Save and drive* is now **Save and test**: after the car is saved a window asks for the track and the conditions (weather, time of day, ground; they are the options of the game), with *Start*, or *Use last settings* (it says which: the track, the weather and the time) to go at once.

### The tuning menu of the game follows the car editor

- **The Engine card of the game builds the engine too:** the block, the exhaust, the chart of the torque curve with *Run dyno*, and the settings of the build (bore and stroke, compression, cam, valves, flows and lengths, fuel and mixture, turbo or supercharger, the dyno room) as sliders with limits that follow from each other, as in the editor (`EngineBuildUI.js`, one piece of the page; the model is `EngineBuild.js`). The torque curve, the turbo and the sound (layout, bore, stroke, cam, flows, exhaust) follow the build live and are kept in the tune. The old rows for the layout and the impulse response of the sound are gone from the menu.

- The tuning menu has the cards of the car editor, with the same names and in the same order: Wheels, Suspension and driveline, Chassis, Drivetrain, Engine, Engine sound, Assists and extras, Cameras, Soft body (and Motorcycle, and the Mirrors of the game). Every card holds its groups (Chassis: Chassis settings, Brakes, Aerodynamics and steering, Size and steering wheel; Engine: Fuel, Engine settings), the card Engine shows the power and the card Drivetrain the top speed. The structure is one list for both (`OpenRacer.CarTuning.CARDS`). The torque-curve rows (peak power and its rpm) are gone from the menu: the curve follows from the engine that is built in the editor.

### Automatic gearbox: the limiter

- **An automatic gearbox shifts up when the engine sits on the rev limiter with the throttle open.** It only did so when the wheels spun by more than 20% and 300 rpm, so a powerful car whose wheels spun by about 20% (the V12 rebuild with a final drive of 6.5 on slicks) stayed in the gear on the limiter for seconds: 5 s in 2nd gear at 100 km/h in a test, now 2 s. The shift comes after the engine has been at 97% of the limiter for a third of a second.

### Automatic gearbox: lifting the throttle

- **The downshift is at the speed of the torque band.** It was at a part of the rev limit (23% with the throttle off: 2415 rpm for a limit of 10500, with a peak torque at 5500), far too low for a car that revs high. Now the automatic shifts down when the engine falls below 60% of the engine speed of the peak torque with the throttle off, up to 90% with the throttle wide open (the more you ask, the earlier it kicks down), so that it lands near the peak torque (a 2.6/1.79 step from 3300 rpm lands at about 4800). To keep it from hunting the shift up is not lower than 125% of the peak torque speed, also with the throttle off, and the shift down stays below the speed where it would go back up. Measured on a lift at 95 km/h in 4th: shifts down at about 3300 rpm each time (4th to 3rd, 3rd to 2nd, 2nd to 1st), instead of at 2000.

- **The engine speed no longer drops to idle when you lift off.** The automatic clutch closed with the engine speed only, so a lifted throttle let the engine fall below the closing speed (2000 rpm in the V12 rebuild), the clutch opened, and the engine dropped to idle while the car still ran at speed. It now also counts the engine speed that the wheels ask for: the clutch stays closed while the car rolls in gear, the engine follows the wheels down (it brakes) and only lets go when the car is too slow for the gear.
- **Downshifting on a lifted throttle is calmer.** With the throttle off the gearbox keeps the higher gear until the road speed asks for the clutch (the shift speed of a 10500 rpm limit was far too high) and goes down then. The blip of a dual clutch on a downshift stops when the engine has the speed of the new gear: with a light engine (inertia 0.02) it went to 5600 rpm and kicked the car. Measured with the tune on a lift from 103 km/h: the engine speed falls 4069, 3050, 2352, 1876 (45 km/h), 1603 (first gear), 971 and then idle, without a jump.

### Repair

- **Repair is a service.** It repaired the body, the parts, the wheels and the punctures, but the tyres stayed worn and the tank as it was. Now it also gives new tyres (60 degrees, as a pit stop does) and a full tank.

### Cameras: closer

- **The near clipping of the cameras is lower** so that you can look closer: the free camera of the game, of the replay, of the camera track, of a take and of the video editor 3 cm (was 30 cm), the cockpit camera 3 cm (was 10 cm), the chase camera 20 cm (was 1 m).

### The rev counter

- **The dial of the rev counter ends at the rev limit.** The dial went to the next full thousand (7000 for a limit of 6300), so the red zone, which ends at the limit, stopped short of the end of the ring. Now the ring runs to the limit, the numbers stand at the thousands below it, and the red zone is at the end.

### Finishes of the paint and the rims

- **The body and the rims have a finish next to their colour** (tuning menu of the game, Chassis settings): Gloss, Satin, Matte, Metallic, Pearl (the colour shifts with the angle: an iridescent clear coat), Metal flake (a sparkle from a normal map of tiny mirrors), Candy (deep and glassy), Chrome and Brushed metal. The finish sets the roughness, the metal and the clear coat; the colour stays the colour of the picker (or of the model). They are kept in the tune (`colors.bodyFinish`, `colors.rimFinish`) and shown in the pictures of the main menu. Only meshes called body or rim take them (the other parts of a model keep their paint). With *Metal flake* a *Flake size* row shows (25 to 400%, for the body and for the rims separately).

### Tyres

- **Drag slick** (*Drag slick (straight line)*): the most grip in a straight line (1.7, the slick has 1.45), half the stiffness across the tyre (`lateral`: 0.5, so it is poor in a bend), warms up and wears fast. In a test from a standstill on the road it reaches 100 km/h in 5.0 s against 5.6 s for the slick. A kind of tyre may now have a `lateral` factor for the stiffness against the slip angle.
- The settings of the tyre on top of its kind (the stiffness of the tread, the grip, the grip of the rear, the stiffness against the slip angle, the rolling resistance, the heating up and the life) are folded away in *Fine-tune the tyre (on top of the kind)*, in the car editor and in the tuning menu; the kind, the pressures and the sizes stay on top.

### Traction control

- **Traction control holds worn tyres too.** It could only take the power down to a fifth of the pedal; with a big engine on worn or hot tyres, or on loose ground, that was still too much and the driven wheels kept spinning (3 times the torque, worn tyres on sand: the driven wheel turned 50 times the allowance). A wheel that spins far past the allowance now takes the power all the way off (the floor falls from a fifth to nothing between 1.3 and 2.5 times the allowance). The same test now holds it at about twice the allowance; at normal power nothing changes.

### Reset

- **The reset key puts the car back on the racing line of the AI, nose forward.** The car remembers where it was last within 3 m of the line. Within 3 m the reset goes to the nearest point of the line; further away (off the road, a spin, a cut over the grass) it goes to the middle of the line at the last place within 3 m, and not to the point of the line that happens to be nearest to where the car is now (that can be another part of the track). Before, a place saved with the save key came first. Only a track without a racing line (no best lap, no recorded line, no design) still uses the saved place, then the line from the last gate to the next, then the start.

### Car editor, after the audit
- **Exhausts that sound different.** A measurement of the engine sound showed why Smooth and Open exhaust hardly differed: the engine gives a narrow band around its firing frequency (95% between 150 and 500 Hz), a convolution cannot add what is not in the signal, and two of the impulse responses were nearly the same (Sharp is the same file as Open exhaust 3). The *Exhaust* choice of the Engine card is now a whole chain, `exhaust_type` of `engine_sim`: Standard, Sport, Performance, Straight cut, Open headers and Quiet, each with its own impulse response (synthesised: pipes as delay lines, mufflers as chambers; `web/sounds/ir/make_exhaust_irs.py`), a drive that adds the harmonics of sharp pulses, bass, mid and treble, a muffler (lowpass), the volume, and the flow and length of the exhaust in the engine simulation. Measured at 5500 rpm on the V8: the part above 500 Hz goes from 5% (old) to 53% (Performance), the centre of the sound from 326 Hz to 1.8 kHz (Straight cut) and 4.7 kHz (Open headers). A block brings a fitting exhaust (a kart a straight cut, a superbike a performance exhaust); a car with the old setting shows *Custom* and sounds as before.
- The engine sound follows the Engine card: the block gives the layout, bore, stroke, cam, flows and lengths of the sound, and a new *Exhaust* choice under the block (Smooth, Open exhaust 1-3, Mild, Sharp) is the exhaust of the sound. The preset, layout and exhaust dropdowns of the Engine sound card are gone; the card keeps the strength of the exhaust effect and *All settings…*.
- The chart and *Run dyno* of the Engine card are pinned next to the sidebar while the card is open (windows of 1000 px and wider), so the settings can be changed with the chart in view.
- **The torque curve can no longer be drawn, typed or generated by hand.** Every engine is built from a block (the choice is at the top of the Engine card, in Simple and in Advanced); the curve follows from it. A car that still has an old curve keeps it until a block is chosen (the ready check says so); Simple shows the power read-only and the max rpm is the rev limit of the build. The dots in the chart, the numbers table and the typical-curve generator are gone.
- The settings of the engine build are sliders with limits that follow from the others: the stroke and the rev limit (28 m/s mean piston speed at most), the bore against the stroke, the compression against the fuel and the boost (knock), the boost against the compression, fuel and the kind of turbo or supercharger, the flows against the bore and the valves, the exhaust against the header, a long cam against the valves, the valves and the size of the turbo against the bore and the displacement. A number typed beyond a limit is pulled back; hover a slider for the reason.
- **Build the engine** (Engine card, Advanced): choose a block (the 18 presets of the engine sound; the sound follows) and set bore, stroke, compression, cam duration, valves, inlet and exhaust flow and length, the fuel (95, 98, E85, race) and the mixture, and a turbo (single, twin-scroll, twin, variable geometry, three sizes) or a supercharger (Roots, twin-screw, centrifugal) with boost and intercooler. The torque curve is worked out from the build (`engine.build` in car.json; `torque_curve` holds the result, so the game is unchanged) and is not edited by hand; *Run dyno* measures it, in the air of the dyno room (temperature, altitude) with a DIN correction and the boost drawn in green. A car without a build keeps its own curve. 4-stroke petrol only; the model is in `web/js/EngineBuild.js`.
- *Run dyno* under the chart of the engine: a full-throttle pull in the gear closest to 1:1, measured at the wheels (the drivetrain efficiency, the rolling of the tyres and the inertia, as a chassis dyno with the car on rollers: no air drag, no wheelspin). The result is drawn as dashed lines in the chart, with a line of text; nothing else changes.
- The torque curve is in the Engine card now, no window: *Simple* (the default) has power (hp) and max rpm, which scale the curve and keep its shape; *Advanced* has the chart to draw in (drag a dot, click to add one, double-click to remove it), idle, rev limit, inertia, the numbers and a typical curve from the peak power. The choice is remembered.
- The tune bar: the tune file actions (delete, export, import) are folded under *Tune file*, a line says what Save writes to (car.json or the tune), and a line shows mass, front weight, peak power and top speed. Saving or choosing a tune clears the unsaved mark.
- *Changes* compares with the form as it was loaded, saved or chosen as a tune (1 difference after one edit instead of 39 with a tune active).
- The move-the-model fields and the finger direction have their own labels; the texts of the differential and the driver card are shorter; on a touch screen the buttons and fields are at least 34 px high.

### Motorcycles

**After the first drive**
- **A landing is more forgiving.** After a flight of a quarter second or more the rider braces for the touchdown: for half a second the balance and the yaw damping are up to twice as strong (a replay of a jump on the test track: the bike landed, bounced, and on the second touchdown fell into a lean of 49 degrees and a spin of 100 degrees a second with no input, and went down in a highside). The bike is down from 70 degrees of lean (63), and a slide sideways is a lowside after 0.8 s (0.5).
- **A rider that lies in the bike no longer shoves it away.** The push he gives the bike is the hit in the first 0.3 s of the crash and after that no more than half his weight: wedged in the spheres of a bike on the road, his sticks pushed him back into them every substep and the bike got 1.4 kN for seconds, so the two of them slid off together.
- **The crash camera follows the rider** instead of standing on a circle round the bike with the two of them in the picture: parented to him with the sky up (it goes with his place, not with his tumble; the offset it had at the crash is kept, 2.5 to 5 m away and at least 1.2 m up), smoothed, looking at him, and it stays in front of a wall as the chase camera does.
- **The ragdoll's knees bend as knees.** The limit of a knee or an elbow (knees forward, elbows back) was taken in the forward of the torso, which is along the thigh of a rider that sits and anything at all once he lies on the road: the knees folded forwards and sideways (128 degrees). It is in the frame of the limb now (the line from the hip to the foot and the side axis of the body), the bend stays within 35 degrees of the plane of the joint, and a limb is never quite straight, so the skeleton always knows which way its knee goes (a straight leg had its pole on the line and the knee of the model snapped from one side to the other). The limbs keep out of each other (a leg went through the other leg, a hand through the chest): the particles that no stick joins are held apart. A limit moves a particle without giving it speed, by at most 2 cm an iteration and never into the road: a knee that a limit pushed down and the road pushed up again every iteration, with the little bounce the road gives, was a motor that threw the rider 10 m in the air after a crash.
- **Full throttle with the bars at the lock** is a tight circle (a Supermoto settles at 17 km/h and 19 degrees of lean on a circle of 3.5 m), not a fall: the balance of the rider used to fade at a walking pace with the bars turned far, so that the bike leaned in and went over; with the feet going up and down around 5 km/h that was a bike that fell to 58 degrees, was thrown round by its rear tyre at 200 degrees a second, stood up and did it again every two seconds. The throttle is eased to a third there (a tenth).
- A motorcycle gets going from a standstill with the bars at the lock: the throttle was eased to 8 % whenever the bars were turned far at a walking pace, from a standstill too, so a Supermoto with the bars turned could not move off; the ease comes in from 0.3 m/s and is full from 1.2 m/s.
- **A hard landing no longer goes through the road.** The tyre gave at most six times its static load, which a landing from 3 m needs more than (25 now), and a hub driven below the road turned the push of the tyre the wrong way (the up of the wheel decides the side now); the rim itself stops the hub when the tyre is flat (`TyreShell.js`, a very stiff spring with a damper). The Supermoto 700 lands drops of 2, 3 and 5 m on the circuit and stands up again (it fell through at 3 m).
- **The landing angle in the air** (`motorcycle.air_pitch`, 1): what spins the rear wheel up (the gas) or slows the wheels (the brakes) turns the bike the other way, as the angular momentum is kept: gas brings the nose up, the brake brings it down. The wheelie control does not cut the gas in the air any more.
- The rear shock of a motorcycle (`rear_spring`, or `rear_shock`, `rear_damper`, `shock_rear`) goes with the swingarm: its upper end stays on the frame, its lower end goes round with the arm, and the part is turned and stretched between the two every frame (it stood still on the Supermoto 700).
- The toes of the rider went through the floor at a standstill: they go down only so far that their tip is on the road, with the ankle 9 cm above it.
- A motorcycle has no handbrake: the handbrake key (Space) is the rear brake alone (it locked the rear wheel with the handbrake of a car).
- **Both feet down at a standstill, the bike upright.** The bike no longer rests against the right foot: both feet go down on the road under the pegs, the toes towards the nearest point of the road. Above 5 km/h (they go down again below 4) the feet go back to the pegs in a second, the left foot first and the right one a third of a second later.
- **The video editor shows the posture of the rider and the feet.** The lean forward and back (tuck, sit), the hanging off sideways and the feet were eased from the frame before, so a jump in time, a pause or a frame without a step in the editor left them where they were; they are the posture of the sample now after a jump.
- The brakes: the rear keeps at least 22 % of its load while a lean is asked for or the bike leans or slides (a rear with no load has no grip sideways), and both brakes come off as the slip of the bike grows past 5 degrees, as the cornering ABS of a modern bike does. A hard brake with a little steering at 180 km/h still slides; that goes deeper, into the rear tyre under the brakes, and is an open point.
- The tyres squeal later on a motorcycle (the camber of its tyres carries a bend).
- On the brakes the rider holds the bars harder (the hold on the yaw is up to three times as strong, 9 000 N m): with the rear nearly off the road the front alone turned the bike 14 degrees out of line in a second at 145 km/h.
- A lowside: a bike that slides sideways past 15 degrees for half a second while it leans goes down (it crabbed along for seconds at the limit of the lean).
- Steering at speed: the lean the keys can ask for at the top of the speed is 30 degrees (`fast_lean_deg`; it was 18, and the bike hardly steered at 200 km/h), and from 110 km/h the rider hangs off for a small lean already (fully at 180), so his weight to the inside turns the bike where it hardly leans. The lean at the top of the speed is in the Escape menu (*Motorcycle: lean at top speed*, 15 to 45 degrees).
- A jump landed on a lean: the pressing force of the tyre across its suspension (what a tyre gives against a ramp side or a wall) is capped at the static load of the wheel on a motorcycle; a tyre that landed leaned on a slope at 170 km/h pressed with 4 000 N and threw the bike backwards at 100 km/h ("the bike shot into the ground"). A bike that is spun round anyway (more than 4 rad/s of yaw above 18 km/h) is a crash now (reason "spin"): it slides out instead of shooting off backwards.
- The brake lever and the clutch lever of a bike turn when they are pulled (about their hinge, the inboard end of the lever: up to 17 degrees with the front brake, and the clutch lever with every shift). In a replay the shift lever and the clutch hand follow the recorded gear (they followed the gear of the game, which does not change in a replay).
- Debug view (the 0 key) on a motorcycle: the mass of the rider as a block (orange) where the physics has it, moved by the posture, with his weight as an arrow (red: gravity less the acceleration of the bike), and the fork (the cylinders white, the legs orange, so the travel is seen). The - key hides the model of the car and the driver while the view is on, so only the lines are seen.
- A bike that loses its front or its rear wheel (the soft body tears it off) stands on its fork or its swingarm: its body touches the road from then on (it fell through the road, since the spheres of an upright bike stay off it and there was no wheel to hold it up). It is no crash by itself: the rider holds it up until it leans over.
- The fork legs and the mudguard of a motorcycle (the `brake_..._FL` parts) hang on the wheel that is drawn: the physics has a pair of wheels 4 cm apart and the model one wheel, and the parts hung on the empty slot, 4 cm beside the tyre; the wheel of the model is drawn in the middle of the pair.
- Cockpit view: the torso of the driver (the hips, the spine, the chest, the neck, the shoulders and the upper arms, by the names of the bones; on a Rigify rig the DEF- bones) is not drawn, as the head was not; the forearms, the hands and the legs stay. The cockpit thumbnail of the car editor shows the same.
- The front wheel of a motorcycle goes along the fork as the suspension works (it went straight up and down: the set-back along the rake was applied to the ghost wheel, not the wheel that is drawn), and the swingarm (a part called `swingarm`) turns about its pivot with the rear wheel, which is on the end of it (it was frozen to the body). In the game and in the test of the car editor.
- The tyres of a bike jittered back and forth at a standstill: the tyre force can only bring a wheel to the speed of the road in a step, not past it (a light wheel on a stiff tyre reversed every frame). Not while the engine drives the wheel: a wheel kept at the road speed has no slip, the drive went through the tyre unchecked and the bikes looped over backwards at every start (found a day later, and put right).
- Car editor: *New motorcycle* next to *New car*, and a *Type* field (car or motorcycle). A motorcycle shows only what a motorcycle has: one wheel at each end (Front wheel, Rear wheel), no differential, no reverse, no steering wheel, no tracks, and a *Motorcycle* card with the rider and the lean (max lean, lean at top speed, rake, rider mass, hang off, max lateral g, balance). A new motorcycle could not be made before: the type came only from a car that was loaded.
- A new motorcycle: the **Supermoto 700** (the GasGas SM 700 of 2023 as a setup: a 693 cc single, 65 hp at 7500 rpm, 9000 rpm, 155 kg, long travel), on the "2022 Husqvarna FS 450" model by OUTPISTON (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, see CREDITS.md). The origins of its grips and levers were moved to the parts (the hands of the rider pivot on them).
- The rider looks for his grips and pegs again when his frame changes or the bike changes: in the car editor he kept the grips of the bike that was loaded before (or of the model view, once he was on the test bench), and his hands went up in the air; a hand moved then was saved with a nonsense offset.
- The steering axis of a motorcycle can be set in the model: a node called exactly `steerhead` (an empty is best) gives the pivot (its origin) and the direction (its local up axis: Z in Blender, up the fork). Everything that steers (`steerhead_…`, the handlebar parts, `brake_…_FL`, the front wheel) turns about that line, and the wheel goes along it with the travel. Without the node it is as before: through the middle of the front wheel at the rake of the car.
- The geometry of the fork and of the swingarm is in the physics: the wheel does not go straight up, it goes along the fork (the `steerhead` axis, or the rake) and on the arc of the swingarm (from its pivot to the rear axle). A road force along the wheel loads the spring with the part of it that goes along the travel: under the brake the fork dives (at 30 m/s the front goes in 11 to 12 cm on the Supermoto, 6 to 7 cm on the CBR, against 2 to 7 cm before), and a swingarm with its pivot above the axle gives anti-squat. The body gets the same total force; only the spring goes in further. `motorcycle.fork_dive` (the *Motorcycle* card of the editor) scales it, 0 is off.
- Braking hard: the front brake no longer hunts. The limit of the front brake (the rear wheel keeps a few percent of its load) was a target from the load of the rear wheel with a delay, and it hopped about twice a second: the fork went in and out and the rear wheel came off the road and down again, with the ABS on or off. It is now a limit on the braking force of the road (what the tyres give, against the weight that the brake can throw onto the front by statics: the wheelbase, the height of the centre of gravity and the weight distribution), which follows the brake in a few hundredths of a second, with the load of the rear wheel as a second check. On the Supermoto the rear wheel is off the road a fifth of a full stop (almost half before), and it stops as fast.
- The front wheel of a motorcycle has the inertia of a front wheel with its tyre and discs, 1.2 kg m2 at least (the size of the bike gave 0.33): at that value the tyre force of a hard brake turned the wheel round in a frame, past the road speed and back, and the force flipped between about -3000 and +3000 N three times a second. That was the hopping of the fork and the rear wheel that was left after the brake limit was put right, in the replays of both bikes (with the ABS on or off). In 18 stops from 100 and 160 km/h on both bikes the rear wheel stays on the road (it was off it half of the stop), without a crash; the stops take as long as before.
- The wheels of a heavy car (the stadium truck) rocked forwards and backwards at a standstill: the grip of the tyre damps the slide with the weight of the whole car (TyreBody.js, the damping at a standstill), and a light wheel took that in one explicit step, past the road speed and back, so the force of the tyre flipped between its limits (about 4 000 N) every frame and the wheel turned at 3 rad/s either way. The wheel takes the damping implicitly now (the inertia plus the time step times the slope of the damping), in the cylinder tyre of every car and bike.
- The pictures of the cars in the menu are made again when the model file changes: they were kept by what car.json says (the id, the model name, the size), so a model that was exported again under the same name kept its old picture, in the browser and as a `preview.webp`. The size and the date of the model file are in the key now, and a `preview.webp` that is older than the model is not used.
- The cinematic camera of a replay can look ahead of the car: *Look ahead* in the bar of the camera (0 to 40 m: the camera aims that far in front of the car along the way it points, level, and still turns smoothly), and in the video editor a *look ahead* field per clip that has the Cinematic camera (it is used while the clip plays and in the mp4 and the replay that are made of it). 0 is as it was: at the car.
- The aspect of the picture of a replay and of a video: 16:9, 9:16 or 1:1 (*Frame* in the bar of the replay, and at the top of the video editor; it is kept in the browser). The replay shows it as an orange outline in the window, the largest rectangle of that shape: what a video takes of the picture. The *Video* of the replay records the middle of the picture in it (at most 1920 pixels on the long side), the video editor renders in it (1280 x 720, 720 x 1280 or 960 x 960: the monitors, the render and the mp4 all have it) and shows what is outside the frame as a hatched passepartout with an orange line round the picture. The aspect cannot be changed while the editor records. A portrait picture has the same vertical field of view as the wide one, so it shows less of the sides.
- A drive shaft for a car with drive to all wheels: a part of the model called `propshaft` (or `susp_propshaft`, `cardan`, `susp_driveshaft_center`) stays where it is and turns about its own length (its longest side) with the wheels, at the speed of the wheels. The differentials at the front and the rear, and the half shafts (`susp_driveshaft_FL` and so on, which get longer and shorter with the travel), are the parts of the model as before.
- Car editor, *Drive* and *Drop car* on a motorcycle: the front end turns as one (the handlebar parts and the grips stayed still, only the wheel and the leg steered, about the vertical) and the front wheel and the swingarm follow the travel of the suspension as in the game: the fork and the swingarm were never updated on the test bench, because the wheel of the physics has no object of the model there.
- The reset key without a saved place goes to the nearest point of the racing line of the AI, in its direction, or else to the nearest point of the line from the gate that was passed last to the next gate; the start only when there is neither.
- The hands keep their turn on the grips when the rider hangs off a bend: the turn of a hand relative to its grip is found once, in his neutral posture, and kept (it was found again every frame from the arm that the posture of the moment gave, and the fingers pointed up in a bend).
- A bike that is down slides with a steady 0.6 g of friction and the rider with 0.55 g, so they slide together (the bike lost its speed as a share of it, 3 g at first, and caught the rider up from behind at full speed; that was the rider that "shot off the bike").
- The version is 2.2.0: the replay file is version 4 and the physics of a motorcycle changed; every browser loads the new scripts (the ?v= of the scripts is the version).
- The front of a wheelie comes down without the bounce: the fork and the shock of a motorcycle have no blow-off (the damper of a car lets go past 0.3 m/s, and the spring threw the bike 2.6 m/s back up after a wheelie to the vertical) and hold the rebound a quarter more. Their damper, and the damping of the stops, is solved implicitly, so it is stable at any strength: the one wheel of an end has twice the spring of a wheel of a pair, and the explicit damper rang at 240 Hz and blew up on a hard landing or on the brakes.
- The fork legs, the caliper and the mudguard move with the wheel again: they hang on the model's wheel_FL, which is the lost wheel of the front pair since the motorcycle has two wheels; the lost wheel now goes with the live one.

**Motorcycles (a prototype)**
- A car with `"type": "motorcycle"` is a motorcycle: two wheels (a pair at each end in the physics), a rider that keeps the bike up and leans it into the corner (`lean = atan(a / g)`), steering that gets smaller with the speed, and the bodywork that may lean over the road. The first one is the **Honda CBR 600 RR** (`web/cars/honda-cbr600`, a model made by `tools/make-cbr600.py`): 270 kg with the rider, 118 hp to 15 500 rpm, six gears, a fork of 17 kN/m and a shock of 24 kN/m. On the flat pad it holds 12 m/s at leans of 20 to 47 degrees, brakes from 90 km/h in 36 m (0.96 g) and goes 0 to 90 km/h in 4.5 s. See `MOTORCYCLE.md`.
- **The CBR was hard to ride with the keyboard** (a straight line was fine, but braking or steering threw it round): the steering assist of the keyboard turned the front wheel into every wobble (off for a bike now); the steering was an angle that at speed was several g of sideways acceleration (now a wish for a bend, cut to 0.9 g, with a short delay); the engine braking dragged the rear wheel at 30 % slip when the throttle was shut (0.3 now); a hard brake lifted the rear wheel and let the rear brake lock (the brakes now follow the load of the wheels); and the rider damps the yaw that was not asked for. On the circuit and on the pad: braking from 25 to 33 m/s, with and without a key held for a bend, stays straight or on its lean (28 to 35 degrees), without a swing.
- **The motorcycle is a controller of its own** (`web/js/Motorcycle.js`), and the cars do not go through it: the keys, the stick or the wheel are a lean command (the same at every speed, a held key keeps its lean; before, the lean fell back while the key was held and was small at speed), the rider follows it in 0.16 s, the front wheel is turned to what that lean needs and first goes the other way to start it (countersteering), a leaned tyre pushes the bike into the bend (camber thrust), the throttle stands it up a little in a corner, the front brake is let off only at the edge of a stoppie, and the camera rolls with the lean. On the circuit at 10, 20 and 30 m/s a held key gives about 50 degrees in 1.3 s and the bike is upright 0.75 s after the key is let go; the physics check of the mustang and the stadium truck is as it was.
- **The crash camera of a motorcycle sits on a circle of 9 m round the bike**, on the far side from the rider (the bike always between the camera and the rider), 3 m up, and it moves smoothly as the two slide apart; the field of view opens to keep both in the picture.
- **Car editor: a motorcycle turned 90 degrees and lost its wheels on *Show the shell* or *Edit nodes*.** The test bench gave the bike the track of its model, 0 m (the game gives it 4 cm), which is a division by zero in the physics: every step was not a number, the car was put back at heading 0 and the wheels had no place. The bench gives a bike 4 cm now.
- **A crash on a motorcycle.** When the bike is nearly on its side (past 69 degrees of lean), or it is hit (a change of speed of more than 2.8 g, or more than 2.2 g sideways at speed: a wall, a kerb), the rider lets go: the bike is a body that slides and tumbles on the road (no flying: its speed only goes down, it does not spin up), the rider is thrown off as a loose body that tumbles and slides to a stop (the limbs in their rest pose; not a jointed ragdoll yet), the camera stays where it was and looks at the two of them (the field of view widens so that both stay in the picture), and the keys do nothing. **Reset** puts the bike back at the saved place and **Repair** puts it on its wheels where it lies, both with the rider on it and the camera of the game again.
- **Wheelie control** for a motorcycle (menu, tab Car, on by default): the rider rolls the gas off as the front wheel goes light and keeps it at about a tenth of its load. A bike with a lot of torque from low revs (the CBR with a torque curve of 60 Nm from 500 rpm) looped over backwards at full throttle from a standstill; with the control it pulls away at full throttle, 0 to 90 km/h in 5.3 s, the front wheel just off the road. Off, it loops as before.
- **The replay shows the rider as he rode**: the hang-off, the knee out and the foot on the road at a standstill come from the recording (the lean of the body, the speed and the pedals); they were not in the replay at all.
- **At a standstill the rider puts the right foot down** (flat on the road beside the bike) and the bike rests against it, 7 degrees to the right (`motorcycle.foot_lean_deg`); on the gas the foot comes back to the peg in two seconds and the bike comes upright with it. Not while the bike is pushed back.
- **The instability of a motorcycle at speed is found and fixed.** A replay of the circuit (the long straight after the first bend, 150 to 165 km/h, a lean to the right and the throttle shut) showed the tail stepping out and the bike going round. In the game, with the gear and the wheels of the test set right (the earlier tests started every run in first gear at 40 m/s with the wheels standing still, which dragged the rear tyre for half a second and hid the real behaviour, so some of the earlier findings were wrong): the same lean with the throttle open was fine, with the throttle shut the yaw ran away. The cause: the bike was a car with a pair of tyres at each end. The sideways force of a bend came from the slip angles of the tyres, in proportion to their loads, so whichever end carried the weight did the turning: the throttle shut (or the brake) loads the front, the front tyre then turned the bike and the light rear could not hold it. The camber thrust, the force of a leaned tyre that does most of the turning of a real bike, was one force at the centre of gravity, which neither turns nor rights anything.
  - The camber thrust is now 0.85 of load × tan(lean) (it was 0.3 × sin), at the **contact patch of every wheel**, shared front and rear as the weight of the bike at rest (so the brake and the gas do not turn it into a yaw), and it rights the bike as the real thing does. The slip angles of the tyres stay small and the loads of the ends no longer decide the line.
  - The front wheel points along the bend (`delta = L g tan(lean) / u²`, the whole of it) and the rider **holds the yaw rate that the lean gives** (`g tan(lean) / u`) with a torque about the vertical (it was damped towards the steering angle, and only with the front wheel in the air towards the lean).
  - On the brake and upright the front wheel **castors** into its own slip angle (`front_comply` 3: it keeps a quarter of it), so a front that carries 90 % of the weight does not push the nose round after a bump or a kick: a stop from 155 km/h over the crest of the start straight with a kick of 0.5 m/s stays straight (heading 1 degree; it went round 90 degrees before). Not in a bend and not off the brake, where it made the yaw hunt.
  - The rider's roll torque is about the bike's own axis, not the horizontal one (on a slope a part of it was a yaw).
  - Measured, each three times with the same result: a lean of 36 degrees to the right at 45 m/s with the throttle shut: the bike turns at 1.6 g, slides 0.9 m/s at most and comes back upright (it went round before); the same at 50 and 35 m/s; the brake in a bend of 42 degrees at 25 m/s holds the lean; left-right-left every 0.4 s at 22 m/s: 16 degrees per second of yaw at most; from 40 degrees left to 40 right in 0.85 s at 12 m/s and 0.91 s at 25; a lean of 42 degrees is 1.2 g; a stop from 108 km/h at 0.88 g, straight.
- **Lean only is the standard steering of a motorcycle** (menu: *Motorcycle steering*). With *Countersteer + lean* the countersteering is **off while the brake is on**, and it comes back slowly after the brake: nothing for 1 s, then in over 1.5 s (`counter_wait`, `counter_ramp`; tested: gain 0 on the brake, 0.11 after 1.3 s, 1 after 2.7 s). What keeps a bike straight in a hard stop is not that pulse but the rider steering out a lean that came from a bump or a crest: that is now a part of the rider that is always there, on a straight (lean asked for below 2 to 6 degrees), in both modes (the stop over the crest with a kick of 0.3 to 0.5 m/s stays straight in both: heading 1 degree, sideways speed 0.4 m/s at most; in lean only without it the bike turned 85 degrees).
- **Lean is limited by the grip at speed** (`grip_g` 1.05, `fast_from` 38 m/s): the lean that the keys can ask for is cut by the circle of grip (what the speed that is going away, from the engine, the air or the brake, takes from the grip that is left for the sideways) and, from 38 m/s, goes down to 18 degrees at 60 m/s. A replay showed the bike that was steered to the left at 190 km/h with the gas off, which first went left and then slid out to the right. In my tests on that straight it slides less (from 40 m/s and slower the steady slide is 1.5 to 4 degrees), but at 45 m/s and at 50 m/s with the gas open it still slid 11 to 17 degrees: not solved there.
- **A motorcycle that is braked hard at speed no longer breaks away**: a replay of a hard stop at 41 m/s over a crest (the circuit, the start straight) showed the tail swinging out from a lateral speed of 0.2 m/s to 17 m/s in 0.6 s, with no wheel locked: the rear tyre had no load left (the front brake was only let off when the rear wheel was almost in the air), so nothing held the tail. The front brake is now let off earlier (from 28 % of the load of the rear wheel to 10 %, a rear wheel with about 6 to 12 % of the weight left instead of 1 %). Braking at that spot with a kick of 0.3 to 0.5 m/s or a yaw rate of 0.1 rad/s stays straight (the sideways speed at most 0.4 m/s; before 19.8 m/s and 85 degrees); a straight stop from 43 m/s takes 3.4 s.
- **Two settings for a motorcycle in the menu** (Esc, tab Car, only with a motorcycle): *Motorcycle steering*: **Countersteer + lean** or **Lean only** (without the short steer the other way at the start of a lean), and *Motorcycle follow camera*: **Motorcycle lean chase** (the picture rolls with the bike) or **Classic chase** (as a car).
- **A video editor** (`VideoEditor.js`, `CameraTrack.js`; the button *Video editor* in the bar of a replay, or *Edit* in the list of Replays): a page with a **source monitor** on the left (the replay with a scrubber, play, and In and Out markers: I and O) and a **program monitor** on the right (the result, 1280 x 720, the same picture as the game), and a timeline with a ruler, the clips and a camera lane. *Insert at playhead* puts the part between the markers where the playhead is (a clip there is cut in two), *Add to end* after the last clip. A clip has a speed (0.05 to 8) and **keys for a ramp** (the speed changes smoothly between them), it can be split, moved and deleted. The **camera track** uses the free camera of the replay (WASD, mouse look, zoom) with the toggles *Parent to car* and *Point at car*; *Key here* puts a key at the playhead and the camera moves smoothly between the keys; without keys the camera of the game follows the car. At the top right: **Save as replay** (a new replay in the list of Replays, cut and with the speeds applied, with the camera track in it; the smoke, the skid marks and the damage are not in it) and **Save as mp4** (the program recorded in real time, without sound; with a webm browser the server makes the mp4 with ffmpeg). Both ask for a name. A replay with a camera track plays it in the viewer too (the free camera takes over when it is chosen). Keys: Space play, Shift+Space the source, I and O, Home and End, the arrows one frame (Shift: a second), Delete, K a key (in the free camera).
- **A motorcycle slides less when the keys go from left to right quickly**: the front wheel was turned to what the lean was going to be (0.1 s ahead), so with the keys going to and fro the front wheel pushed the bike sideways against the lean that it had. The front wheel is turned to the lean that the bike has now: with the keys changing every 0.4 s at 22 m/s the sideways speed of the bike went from 1.8 to 1.1 m/s and the yaw rate from 40 to 20 degrees per second; the quick change from left to right (0.7 s) is the same.
- **Choose your car goes on with one click**: a click on a car takes you to the next step (it was a click and Next, or a double click). The Next button is still there for when you come back to the step.
- **A motorcycle steers by its lean with the front wheel in the air**: in a wheelie the front wheel has no say, so the rider takes the bike round at the rate that its lean gives (`g tan(lean) / speed`); the front wheel in the air no longer fights it (on a wheelie with the key to the left held, 41 degrees of lean and 40 to 70 degrees per second of yaw).
- **A motorcycle is kept square to the road**: the rider balances against the mean normal of the road under the wheels (smoothed over 0.15 s; the sky in the air), not against the sky. On a bank or over a crest the bike no longer stands upright in the world and slides down it. The rear brake comes back slowly (0.3 s) after the rear wheel was off the road, so that it does not lock when it lands. (Tested on the flat and with hops while braking; I could not test on a real bank.)
- **The keys < and > (comma and full stop) put the rider back or forward** while they are held (a bike): back is sitting up with the weight to the rear, forward is flat on the tank. They are in the settings menu ("Motorcycle: rider back / forward"); > was the reverse key of a car, which a bike does not use.
- **A motorcycle steered the wrong way at a standstill and at a walking pace** (below about 4 m/s the angle of the front wheel was the key the other way round; it is the same way as the key now, and a bend to the left is a left turn at every speed).
- **The offsets of the hands and the feet of a rider were not used in the game**: the car editor can take a driver for a car (a steering wheel) before the bike has loaded, and then the pivots were saved as places in the body (`hands.left`, `feet.left`) that a rider ignores, with offsets of 0. A driver now looks again for the grips and the pegs until it has found them, and a rider whose offsets are all 0 and whose old pivots are within 35 cm of the grips and the pegs gets them as offsets (so the hands and the feet stay where they were set).
- **A motorcycle has no reverse**: full braking to a stop and holding the brake key turned on the automatic reverse, and the bike drove backwards with its rear wheel in front, which broke away at once. Now holding the brake at a standstill for 0.6 s (or the reverse key, or R of the selector) makes the rider **push the bike back** at about 1.2 m/s while he sits on it: the feet leave the pegs and shuffle one after the other on the road, and when the key is let go it stops. The gas key rides off as always.
- **The look-at point of the follow camera can be moved in the car editor**: a second dot (light green) on the layer "1 Follow camera: looks at", with a gizmo for the height and for how far ahead of the car it looks (`cameras.follow.look_z`, 0 as it was; negative: behind the middle). The thumbnail shows what the camera sees.
- **The rider of a motorcycle is a mass that moves** (`motorcycle.rider_mass`, 80 kg, there with or without a driver model; car.json `mass` is now the bike without the rider, the CBR has 195 + 80 kg): back on a hard brake (a stoppie is harder to make), flat and forward on the throttle (a wheelie is harder to make, and less drag: `dragScale`, 0.7 flat to 1.35 sitting up), and off the seat to the inside of a bend (0.3 m: the bike leans less for the same bend, `tan(lean of the whole) = tan(lean of the bike + atan(rider share * hang off / height of the centre of gravity))`, about 8 degrees at full hang off, so it corners at 1.4 to 1.5 g at a bike lean of 51 degrees). The weight of the rider, where he is, and the acceleration of the bike (the apparent weight) are a torque about the pitch and the yaw of the bike. The driver model follows: lying flat on the tank, sitting up, the body over to the inside, and the knee on the inside goes out and down to the road and the other one out a little. The main menu shows the weight with the rider.
- **A quicker, braking-in-a-bend motorcycle**: the steering keys bring the lean twice as fast (`key_steer_rate`), the rider follows in 0.1 s and holds with a stiffer spring: from a lean of 40 degrees to the left to 40 to the right takes 0.75 s at every speed (it was 1.3 s). In a bend the front brake is hardly used (it stands a bike up: from 8 to 30 degrees of lean it goes down to a tenth) and the rear brake takes the bike deeper into the bend (`corner_brake`, 0.2 more lean): at 14 m/s and 50 degrees of lean the yaw rate goes from 45 to 87 degrees per second in 1.5 s of braking, and the bike stays on its lean. In a straight line it brakes from 30 m/s at 1.0 g with the wheelbase of the new model (1.385 m).
- **A bike model that gives both wheel slots of an end the same part** (`FR` and `FL` both `wheel_FL`, `RL` and `RR` both `wheel_RL`, as the car editor writes it when a model has no dummy wheels): the second slot found nothing (the first one had taken the part), its place was the origin, and the wheelbase came out as half (0.69 m): the front wheel stood under the engine and the fork and the mudguard stayed where they were. The loader now gives the second slot the place of the first.
- **A rider on a motorcycle**: a driver on a bike that has the parts `footpeg_L/R`, `grip_L/R`, `frontbrake_R`, `clutch_L` and `shifter_L` puts the feet on the pegs and the hands on the grips (they turn with the handlebar, about the axis of the fork), the right wrist rolls with the throttle, the fingers but the thumb point at the brake lever (right) and the clutch lever (left), and the left foot goes with the shift lever, which gets a tap with every shift (first gear: down, higher gears: up, neutral: a small tap up from the first and down from the second). The CBR model has these parts now.
- **Car editor and a motorcycle**: *Drop car* gave "The wheels of the model do not make a car" for a bike (the tracks of a bike are 0, the limit was 0.4 m); a bike has no track limit now (the physics gets 4 cm), and the editor keeps the `type` and the `motorcycle` settings of a bike when it saves. The tyre diameter was the mean of the four wheel slots, and the two dots of a millimetre of the bike made it **0.306 m instead of 0.61**: the editor wrote that in `car.json` (a bike that hardly accelerated), and now the dots are left out (also in the game, `CarLoader.js`).
- **The pivot of a hand of a rider is its grip**: for a driver on a bike the hands of the driver editor sit on `grip_L` and `grip_R` and what you move is the **offset** from the grip (`driver.hands.offset`, in the frame of the grip, so it goes round with the steering and the throttle).
- A rider's **feet** have the same offset as the hands: the pivot of a foot is its peg (`footpeg_L/R`) and the driver editor sets `driver.feet.offset` from it. The **fingers** no longer all point at the lever: the middle finger is the base, turned about its x axis to the angle that points best at the lever, and that one angle is added to the other fingers (not the thumb), as the fingers close together at a grip. Turn the hand (`hands.rotation`) to put the lever in the plane of the fingers.
- New engine sound presets for motorcycles (a thumper, a triple, a 600 cc and a 1000 cc four), and `tools/check-engine-sound.js`: it runs the real engine sound without a browser and reports the level, clipping and the pitch against the firing frequency of every preset.

**A motorcycle has two wheels**
- The physics of a motorcycle had two pairs of wheels on a track of 4 cm (the car physics has four). The second wheel of each pair is now a lost wheel for good: no road, no spring, no drive, no brake; the wheel that stays carries the whole end on the spring of both, with the brakes of both, and the drive goes to it alone. The pair on its little track held the bike up at a standstill as a sidecar does, and it gave a load transfer and an anti-roll bar across 4 cm. Braking, the launch, a bend, the foot at a standstill, the key 9 and the replay (which keeps the lost wheels) were tried; the braking is at the limit of the rear wheel lifting, as before.
- The ragdoll against a wall or a kerb: he bounces back off it (he was frozen where he stood, for good, and a body half on a ramp hung in the air with its limbs stiff).
- The gas and the brake on a touch screen come up as the keys do: the time and the curve of the keyboard settings (they had a ramp of their own), and let go at once.
- A burnout holds its gear (the automatic shifted up on the spinning wheel) and smokes within three or four seconds (a tyre that spins on a car that stands keeps its heat).
- A replay of a motorcycle from before it had two wheels opens with two.
- The ragdoll pushes the bike: where a solid sphere of the bike pushes a part of him out, the bike gets the impulse of that part (a fifteenth of the rider), capped; the seat he sits in does not. His head follows his neck (up to 50 degrees) instead of staying in its rest pose.
- The front brake of a motorcycle lets the rear wheel come down to 6 % of its load before it eases (10 %): a rider brakes right at the point where the rear comes up.

**The rider is a ragdoll in a crash**
- The rider that is thrown off the motorcycle is a jointed ragdoll: fifteen particles (the hips, the chest, the head, the shoulders, the elbows, the hands, the hip joints, the knees and the feet) held together by sticks, with a rigid torso and a limit on how far an arm or a leg folds; they fly, fall and slide over the road (0.7 g of friction, a little bounce, a barrier is a wall), and the skeleton is posed on them every frame. He comes to rest and lies still. Before, he was one stiff body that tumbled.
- The ragdoll collides with the bike: its body spheres push the particles out (a move, never a speed: nothing can be shot away at speed; the spheres move with the bike as a body, not with the dents of its shell), and carry him along where he touches them.
- **A limp rider** (the key 9, "Motorcycle: the rider goes limp", to try it): he slumps where he sits, the seat and the tank hold his hips and knees while the bike is upright, the rest hangs; the bike rides on without him, with the castor of its front wheel for balance and no throttle, and his weight off the middle leans it; it goes down when the speed is gone or he pulls it over, and he slides off a bike that leans more than 30 degrees.
- **A hit crashes**: a wall or a barrier that takes more than 6 m/s of speed in a tenth of a second at more than 20 km/h (riding into a barrier at 40 km/h made the bike bounce back with the rider on it) throws the rider at the speed the bike had before the hit.
- **The crash camera** moves smoothly from where it was to a circle of 2.5 m round the bike (it was 9 m, and it jumped there), the bike between it and the rider.
- **The joints have their limits**: the elbows bend only backwards and the knees only forwards, the upper arm stays within 120 degrees of hanging down and out, the thigh within 100 degrees of down and a little forward; a limb that swings into a limit stops there (most of its speed is taken out, no bounce).
- The limp rider (the key 9) now brings the bike down: the castor of the front wheel is not quite enough to hold it, it leans within a second, he slides off past 30 degrees, and a bike with nobody on it goes down as a body; below 30 km/h it just falls over.
- The crash limits are wider (a hard brake in a bend threw the rider at once): 3.5 g of acceleration (was 2.8), 3 g sideways (was 2.2), 74 degrees of lean (was 69), a hit of 8 m/s in a tenth of a second (was 6).
- **ESC is off on a motorcycle**: it took the small angle of the front wheel in a bend for oversteer and braked the front, which broke the bike away on the brakes at speed.
- **Every part starts with the speed it has**: the speed of the bike at that place (its speed and its turn) and the rider's own movement on it, measured from the frame before (at most 5 m/s); no kick upwards and no random tumble any more. The spheres of the bike move on with their speed within a frame (a rider held to a bike at 25 m/s shook, as the spheres stood still for the frame).
- **Leaning too far is a lowside**: the rider can ask about 10 degrees more lean than the tyres hold (`over_lean_deg`), the tyres let go past that and the bike goes down past 63 degrees (`crash_lean_deg`; the default `max_lean_deg` is 60). Holding full lean at 110 or 160 km/h puts it down in about two seconds.
- **The limp rider (the key 9) gets no help at all**: the front wheel is let go and comes straight, no castor, no balance, no throttle; the tyres and the lean decide where the bike goes, it leans over within a second or two at any speed (also at a walking pace: no direct fall any more) and past 40 degrees, or once it has stopped, it is a body that slides, as in a crash.
- **A walking pace with the bars at the lock**: the throttle is eased to a tenth (the bike went on accelerating round the turn), the rider cannot hold it up any more and it leans into the turn and goes over; with the bars half turned nothing changes.
- A reset at speed (the bike put back at once) is not a hit: the hit that crashes is a loss of speed, not a change of place.
- **A burnout**: the brake and the throttle together at a standstill (or nearly) hold the front brake and let the rear brake off, so the rear wheel spins (it sat at 4 000 rpm with both wheels held).
- **Reset puts the engine at idle with the clutch open** (also for a car): it kept its speed, and in gear it spun the wheels up again the moment the car was put down.
- **A wheelie past the balance point is not a crash**: the lean (the roll) is now how far the bike's own left axis dips below the road, so a wheelie or a stoppie reads 0 degrees of lean however steep it is (measured from the up of the bike it read 180 degrees past the vertical, and that was a crash at once). The tail scrapes and the rear brake can still bring it down; it is a crash once the bike is on its back (its up more than 100 degrees from the road).
- The foot comes up once the bike itself moves at 7 km/h, or when the nose is up more than 30 degrees (a wheelie), in about a second: not on the throttle (it came up the moment the throttle was opened) and not on the speed of the rear wheel, so it stays down in a burnout. Whenever it should be down it reaches for the road itself (a wheelie braked to a walking pace: the right foot goes down towards the ground, as far as the leg goes).
- A burnout goes on: the traction control and the wheelie control stay out of it, and a tyre that spins a lot faster than the road melts and gives less (from 4 m/s of spin to a little over half at 14; a locked wheel or a slide is not this). Before, the rear wheel at a standstill pulled 1.6 g against the front brake, the engine was pulled down to the clutch and the wheel stopped after a few seconds.
- A bike that is down but still stands (the key 9 at a standstill: its wheels on their narrow track held it up) falls over, to the side it leans to already, and lies on its bar and its fairing.
- **The replay shows the ragdoll**: the places of the particles are recorded every sample while the rider is one, and a replay file (version 4) keeps them; a file from before opens as it did.

### Replay, cameras and video

- **Video editor: the mp4 has sound:** *Save as mp4* records the engine and tyre sound of the program together with the picture (it was silent: the editor muted the sound while recording, and the sound was never put in the recording). The browser has to have started the sound (a key or a click once); if not, the editor says so and records without it.
- **Reflections without the rain:** the picture of the surroundings that the paint, the mirrors and the wet road reflect (the cube map) no longer contains the falling rain, the snow and the thrown pieces: they jump about at every frame, and now that the map is made at every frame they made the paint and the puddles sparkle.
- **Weather, ground and particles are in the replay:** a replay now records (file version 5; older files still open, without these) the time of day, the sun direction, the moon and the weather and ground state (once a second and at every change, so the rain, the snow, the puddles and the sky play back as they were, also when you wind back); what the cars did to the snow (the scraping of the wheels, the heat of a car, as events), so the bare places and tracks are there as they were at any time, and the rings of a wheel in a puddle; and the grass, sand, snow, gravel and spray pieces that the wheels threw up (each with its place, speed, kind, size and colour: they fly and fall again by the same code and the pieces that lay on the ground are there too). After the replay the live weather and the snow of the game are put back. Limits: a replay that is saved from a part of a long recording has the snow only from where that part starts (what the cars cleared before is not in it), the edit of the video editor (Save) has no weather records and shows the weather of the settings, and the debris adds to the size of the file (about 44 bytes a piece; gzip makes it a third).
- **A light wheel on a stiff spring no longer throws the car** (`Vehicle.js`): the damper of a car and the damping of its stops are solved implicitly, as a motorcycle's were; explicit, the bump stop of an 8 kg kart wheel on a 50 kN/m spring took c·dt/m past 2 and the kart was launched hundreds of metres (a race tune of the kart stood on its nodes with its wheels hovering, crept backwards and slid backwards under power). The digressive damper keeps its knee and its blow-off (the secant of its curve at the speed of the step).
- **The shell of a car stays on the model when its centre of gravity changes** (`CollisionProxy.js`): the layout of the nodes is in the frame of the body, from the centre of gravity; a tune that lowers the centre took the whole shell down with it. The car editor writes the centre and the tyre the layout was drawn at (`softbody.layout_cg`, `layout_radius`), the game shifts the nodes by the difference; a layout without them counts as drawn for the car as made. A change of the centre of gravity or the tyre in the tuning menu builds the car again.
- **The ground clearance comes from the model** (the underside of the body above the road; `chassis.ground_clearance` overrides it) instead of 13 cm for every car, a node at the floor becomes small (2 cm at least) rather than standing through it, and the sphere of a wheel hub is a little over half the wheel's radius (6 cm at least) instead of 12 cm at least: on a kart's 13 cm wheel that sphere nearly filled the tyre and carried the kart on its hubs.
- **No suspension, for a kart** (`chassis.unsprung`; *No suspension (a kart)* in the car editor, *Suspension: None* in the Chassis group of the tuning menu): the wheels are fixed to the frame, the load of the tyre goes straight into the body and the tyre is the only spring; the suspension sliders are hidden for it.
- **A kart frame that twists** (`chassis.frame_torsion`, N·m per degree, 0 or missing is a rigid frame; *Frame stiffness against twisting* in the Chassis group of the tuning menu, shown with *Suspension: None*): the difference between the roll moment of the front axle and the one of the rear axle goes through the frame as a torque, the frame twists by that torque over its stiffness, and the wheels of the lighter diagonal give way along their axes (the front axle one way, the rear the other, a little smoothed), so the four tyres share the load more evenly in a bend. The openracer-kart has 80 N·m/°. Tried on the kart in a bend at 16 to 30 km/h: the twist stays under 2 mm of wheel travel, it swings with the steering, and it does not run away. Not done: the inner rear wheel that lifts in a fast bend (a stiff frame only; the soft tyres at 0.8 bar kept all four wheels on the road in this test).
- **The inner rear wheel of a kart lifts in a fast bend** (`chassis.inner_lift`, metres; *Inner rear wheel lift in a fast bend* in the tuning menu, shown with *Suspension: None*): from 0.2 g of lateral acceleration the rear wheel on the inside of the bend is pulled up, smoothly, to the full lift at 0.8 g. The openracer-kart has 15 mm. Checked in a left bend on the wet track (0.3 g): the left rear wheel is the one that lifts, by about 1 mm; the full lift needs more grip than the wet kart track gives.
- **A kart with a king pin and a fixed tie rod** (`SuspensionRig.js`): an upright (`susp_upright_FL`, `_FR`) turns about its own origin, the pin, which can sit beside the wheel, and the wheel turns with it about that pin (the hub moves along an arc) instead of about its own middle; the steering angle of each front wheel (with the Ackermann) drives its own upright. A model without a rack (`steer_rack`) can have fixed tie rods (`steer_tierod_FL`, `_FR`: the origin is the pin on an arm of the steering column, the far end sits on the arm of the upright): the column turns about the axis of the steering wheel to the angle at which both rods keep their length best, and the steering wheel (and the hands) follow it. The go-kart-250cc: 54° of the column at the lock of 34°, the rods stretch at most 5 % (the Ackermann of the physics is not that of the rods). The steering wheel of a model whose wheel has a column on it (a part with `column` or `shaft` in its name) takes its middle and its axis from the wheel alone; the column pulled the axis 39° off.
- **Go-kart 250cc** (`cars/go-kart-250cc`): a four-stroke single of 250 cc with a six-speed gearbox, an open exhaust (`minimal_muffling_01`, a short pipe, 12 500 rpm), the knuckles and tie rods above, the rear axle with the sprocket and the drawn chain (`Chain.js`) of the model. The tuning is a first guess, not measured.
- **The rear tyres can have their own width** (`tyres.shell.width_rear`, cm in the tuning menu: *Tyre: width of the rear tread*; 0 is the same as the front): the nodes of the tread of the rear wheels are as wide as that, the front ones as `tyres.shell.width`. The go-kart-250cc has 15 cm in front and 20 cm behind (the shell was 20 cm all round and stood out beside the narrow front tyres).
- **The top speed of a car is worked out and shown** (`OpenRacer.topSpeed(config)` in `CarDefaults.js`): in every gear the speed at which the thrust at the wheels (the torque curve through the gears, the final drive and the efficiency) is as big as the drag of the air and the rolling resistance, up to the rev limit; the highest of them (a CVT: its lowest ratio). It is in the line under every car of the menu (the car step and the tunes of a car: *1230 kg · FWD · 6 gears · 194 km/h*, in mph with the imperial units) and at the top of the Drivetrain card of the car editor, with the form as it is. Downforce and wheel slip are not counted; a torque curve that stops at 8000 rpm gives a top speed at 8000 rpm, whatever the limiter says.
- **A hard stop in the force feedback at the lock of the steering** (the Wheel page of the settings: *Steering lock of the wheel*, 270 to 1080° from lock to lock, and *Match the steering lock of the car*): with it on, the wheel turns as far as the steering wheel of the car does, and no further: a kart with fixed tie rods stops where the rods do (the go-kart-250cc: 50° each way, from the angle of the column at the lock of the wheels, `rig.lockAngle`), another car at `steering_wheel.degrees` of car.json (450 without), and the force feedback pushes the wheel back with all its force over the last 3 % before the lock and 3 % past it (`vehicle.wheelStop`, `ForceFeedback.js`), with a little shake. - **Setups** (`OpenRacer.Setups` in CarTuning.js): the car is saved under a name and loaded again, in the tuning menu of the game and on the Tuning card of the car editor; a setup is the whole configuration of the car (everything car.json has but the model); a car with two or more setups gets a list on its card in the main menu to choose the setup for the game, which is laid over car.json when the game starts. The Tuning card of the car editor has the tyre damage tick too, and the car editor now writes the extras of the card (the pops, the anti-lag, the boost gauge) into car.json and a setup: they were dropped on saving.
- **Damage on or off** (a row of every way to play in the main menu: free drive, the race set-up and the skidpad; `&damage=0|1`): off, every car is a plain rigid body: no shell that dents, no parts or wheels that come off, no punctures.
- **The AI steers towards its line** (`Opponent.drive`): besides the pure pursuit on a point ahead, a pull of up to 8 degrees for 5 m beside the line; a car that slips tracked a wider arc than the geometric steering and drifted off the line without asking for more. **The simple tyre takes its friction at a tenth of the load**, as the shell does per node: with the whole load in one call it had a quarter less grip than the shell on the same car.
- **The speed of the racing line knows the surface and the crests** (`RacingLine.computeSpeeds`): on sand or grass the AI drives at the share of the grip that surface gives, and at the crest of a dune no faster than 15 % over the speed at which the car leaves the ground; on the Desert the AI trucks drove the line at the speed of tarmac, slid off it on the sand and flew off the crests.
- **A racing line from a driven lap is smoothed** (`RacingLine.fromLap`, `smooth`): the joint where the lap closes was a kink the speed profile read as a hairpin, and every AI car braked to a crawl just past the start; the speed of the line is never below 6 m/s.
- **The AI car has its live axles** (`vehicleParams`): its wheels lean with the axle instead of with the roll of the body, as the player's car does.
- **The motorcycle crashes at 78 degrees of lean** (`crash_lean_deg`, was 70), and a powerslide (the rear wheel spinning under power while the bike slides) is no longer read as a lowside.
- **`aitires=shell`** in the address gives the AI cars the shell tyre of the player, to compare.
- **The AI cars drive on a simple tyre** (`Vehicle.simpleTyres`, `Wheel.tyreForce`): rays along the suspension find the road and a slip curve gives the grip, instead of the shell of nodes of the player's tyres, which costs most of the time of a car; one AI car took the frame rate from 115 to 6, three now leave it at about 70.
- **Badges for the systems in the gauge** (`web/images/HUD`): traction control, launch control, ESP, ABS and the two-step are pictures instead of text lights: orange when the system is on, grey when it is switched off (TC has its own OFF badge), blinking while the system is at work, and none for a system the car does not have; LAUNCH and 2-STEP stand one above the other under the gear, where the gearbox mode and the rpm text were (both gone), TC, ESP and ABS on an arc along the bottom of the gauge; a click on a badge switches the system, on the desktop too (it used to be a tap on a touch screen only).
- **Zoom to the mouse in the editors** (`zoom-to-mouse.js`): the wheel zooms towards the point under the mouse (a ray to the model or the track, else the floor) and the orbit centre moves there in the same measure, so the view turns about what was zoomed in on, as in Blender; in the orthographic view of the car editor the point under the mouse keeps its place on the screen.
- **The mouse no longer drives the car** (holding the button and moving it gave gas and steering, with a grey ring where it was pressed): on a desktop the keys, a pad or a wheel drive, the mouse is for the free camera and the menus.
- **The width of a live axle**: a mount at its end (`axle_end`), which moves along the axle only (its middle sits between the wheels); the axle (1 m long as modelled) is scaled to twice that distance.
- **The width of the steering rack**: a mount at its end (`rack_end`), which moves along the rack only and goes with the rack's middle, so it cannot stand askew; the rack (1 m wide as modelled) is scaled to twice that distance.
- A propshaft of the parts library turned about the nearest axis of the car instead of its own length: a slanted shaft wobbled, its ends going round instead of staying at the gearbox and the differential (the front shaft of the Hyundai i30). It spins about its own axis now; a differential does not turn at all.
- The track editor: a part whose role has no colour of its own (a zone) lost its material in the colour view and the view threw an error every frame.
- **Tracks have a picture** on their card in the menu, as the cars have: the *Thumbnail* button of the track editor takes one from the camera as it stands (the track as the game shows it, without the nodes, the markers and the gizmos), saved with the track as `preview.webp`; a track without one gets a picture made in the menu from behind its start (the track is loaded into a scene of its own and drawn once), kept in the browser and written to the track's folder when the server may.
- The Load and New buttons of the garage asked Car or Track in a window that stood behind the garage: it is on top now. **Load** then shows the grid of cars as the start of a race does (or the list of tracks), and a click on one opens it in its editor (`car-editor.html?car=<id>`, `track-editor.html?track=<id>`).
- **Only the settings that apply are shown**, in the tuning menu of the game and in the Tuning card of the car editor (`CarTuning.visible`): the CVT's engine speed only with a CVT, the stall speed and the torque ratio only with a torque converter and the clutch not with one, the shift time not with a CVT, the limited slip's settings only with a limited slip, the centre differential only with all wheel drive (and its split not when it is locked), the turbo's spool, threshold and boost only with a turbo and the intercooler and the compressor only with a boost pressure, and the anti-lag and the boost gauge extras only with a turbo. The rows come and go as the other settings change.
- **The gearbox of the car decides how it is shifted; the gearbox-mode key (G) and its setting are gone.** A manual (`gears`) and a manual with an automatic clutch (`imt`, new: the driver shifts, the clutch is never a pedal) are shifted by the driver (paddles, H-pattern shifter; 1, 2, … N R); an automated manual (`amt`, new: slow shifts of half a second at least with the power off), the dual clutch, the torque converter and the CVT are automatics with the selector P R N D. The gauge names the gearbox (MAN, IMT, AMT, DCT, AUTO, CVT). A car whose file says nothing has a manual: set the gearbox in the car editor or the tuning menu.
- **A dual-clutch gearbox (DCT)** (`drivetrain.gearbox`: `dct`, the Drivetrain card of the car editor and the tuning menu): the next gear waits on the other clutch, so a shift is a hand-over of 0.15 s at most in which the drive never stops (the clutch keeps 40 % and goes to full as the engine is pulled to the new speed), with a short cut of the throttle on an upshift and a blip on a downshift.
- **An automatic with a torque converter** (`drivetrain.gearbox`: `torque-converter`, the Drivetrain card of the car editor and the tuning menu): the gears and the shifting of the automatic with a fluid coupling instead of the clutch: the pump on the engine takes C·ω²·(1 − speed ratio) (C from the stall speed, `tc_stall_rpm`, 35 % of the rev limit by default), the turbine gives that times the torque ratio (`tc_ratio`, 1.9 at stall, 1 from a speed ratio of 0.85), a lock-up clutch closes above 25 km/h once the slip is small, the drive stays on through a shift, and at idle the coupling is a sixth of itself: the car creeps gently and the brakes hold it.
- A CVT chosen in the tuning menu while the car is on the track shows D at once (the label read the file of the car, not what the tuning menu had set), and the belt moves a little faster (`cvt_rate` 1.8: the engine ran past its speed while the car accelerated hard).
- **The garage of the main menu** has Load, New and Import beside its title (open a car or a track in its editor, make a new one, import a zip); the Create button is gone.
- **A CVT** (the Drivetrain card of the car editor and the tuning menu, `drivetrain.gearbox`: gears or cvt): no gears but a ratio that goes smoothly between the first and the last gear ratio, chosen to hold the engine at the speed the throttle asks for (idle to `cvt_rpm` at full throttle, 85 % of the rev limit by default; a little above idle off the throttle, more on the brakes), the belt moving at a limited pace (`cvt_rate`). The HUD shows D (R, N, and P with the wheels locked): the paddles go R, N, D, P, and with the H-pattern shifter the first gate is D and the second is P.
- **The wheels of a live axle lean with the axle** (the travel of its two wheels), not with the roll of the body and the static camber as the wheels of an independent axle do: they stood upright while the axle rolled under them. In the physics too: a rolled live axle cambers its tyres (`Vehicle.liveAxle`, from the type of the axle in the car editor).
- A trailing arm whose end sits on a live axle swings so that its end stays on the axle: the axle at that distance from its middle is at the mean travel of the two wheels plus its roll, less than the travel of the wheel for an arm that sits inboard (the upper arms near the differential came out of the axle when the car twisted).
- The two ends of a propshaft are mounts (`prop_rear_start` at the gearbox, `prop_rear_end` at the differential or the axle; the front likewise), so where a shaft leaves the gearbox and enters the axle can be set.
- A spring or a damper whose foot sits on a wishbone goes round the hinge of the arm with it, not up and down by the whole travel of the wheel: a coilover seated halfway along the lower arm moves half as far as the wheel, and it looked as if it came loose from the arm. The same for a foot on a trailing arm (it goes round the hinge by its distance along the car), and a foot on a live axle (within 15 cm of it along the car) goes up with the axle at its distance from the middle: the mean travel of the two wheels and the roll of the axle.
- **An eye per part of the parts library** in the Suspension and driveline card: orange is placed, grey is hidden (`hidden` of the axle or the driveline in `car.json`), so a car whose own model has its engine or its gearbox keeps those; the Move, Turn and Reset buttons of the mounts are icons.
- The coilover of the parts library stretched past its end: its cylinder, named `coilover`, was taken for the coil (the match on "coil") and scaled with the piston inside it, on top of the slide of the piston; and the slide of a piston in a part that is scaled along its length is divided by that scale now.
- **Both ends of a part are mounts**: a stretched part of the parts library (an arm, a trailing arm, a coilover, a damper, a strut, a tie rod, the Panhard rod) has a gizmo at each end now, where it sits on the body and where its other end goes (the ball joint, the seat of the spring on the arm or the axle, the steering arm, the end of the arm on the axle); the other end was placed from the wheel and could not be set.
- **Edit origins** (the Suspension and driveline card of the car editor): a mode in which a click in the view picks only the mounts of the parts library, through everything (the body of the car was in the way of a click on them), W moves, E turns, Escape leaves, and a *Save the car* button at the bottom of the view saves and leaves; the nodes of the driver and the parts of the model are not picked while it is on.
- **The free camera starts fixed to the car**, in the game (I) and in the replay (L): a camera that stayed where it was while the car drove on was far away by the time it was chosen, and the car was out of the picture. The game remembers the toggle per car as before.
- **Parts library: a suspension and a driveline from loose parts** (`PartsLibrary.js`, the *Suspension and driveline* card of the car editor). A type of suspension for the front and for the rear (double wishbone, MacPherson strut, live axle on trailing arms, live axle on leaf springs, or the parts of the model as before), where the engine is, and the drive decide what is built from glTF files in `web/parts/<type>/<part>.glb` (one set per type; `tools/make-parts.py` writes placeholders): arms aimed and stretched from their hinge to the wheel, coilovers with a sliding piston and a coil, an axle scaled to the track, a rack, tie rods, a differential and driveshafts on a driven independent axle, a propshaft from the gearbox to a driven axle that is far from it (to a live axle it follows the axle), an engine and a gearbox. Where the parts go are mounts with gizmos in the car editor (a default from the wheels), saved in `car.json` (`suspension`, `driveline`). The parts move with the suspension rig as a model's own do; the physics is not changed. See the README.
- **A live axle on trailing arms with coilovers** (the new Trophy Truck model of the stadium truck): the suspension rig knows `rear_solid_differential` / `solid_axle_rear` as the axle, `trailing_arm_RL` and `upper_trailing_arm_RL` as trailing arms, and `coilover_cylinder_RL` / `damper_cylinder_RL` as a spring with parts inside it: the cylinder keeps its length, the piston (a child named `..._piston_RL`) slides along it with the travel and the coil (`..._coil_RL`) gets shorter and longer; a part inside a rig part is no part of its own.
- A part called `sprocket_RL` (or `chainring_RL`, `spin_RL`; on a motorcycle `rear_sprocket`) spins with the wheel of that corner without being a wheel part: a sprocket lies outside the tyre and made the wheel bigger when it was one (the Supermoto 700).
- **A drive chain from one link** (`Chain.js`): a model with `chain_link`, `front_sprocket` (on the engine) and `rear_sprocket` gets a chain: the link is drawn as many times as the chain is long (an InstancedMesh), every frame along the two strands and the arcs round the sprockets, in the plane of the sprockets, so it follows the swingarm; it moves with the rim of the rear sprocket and turns the front sprocket with it. The Supermoto 700: 73 links of 24.9 mm round sprockets of 71 and 244 mm. In the game and on the test bench of the car editor.

**Car editor**
- A driver model that faces the back of the car (a Rigify mannequin from Blender does) is turned round when it is loaded (rotation 180° about y): the editor looks at which side the left and the right shoulders are on. A model whose rotation was set by hand keeps it.

The arm and the leg that stand on the left of the car take the left hand and the left foot whatever the model calls them (a model that faces the other way has its L on the right). Tested with a Rigify mannequin: the arms take the right bones and the hands reach their grips exactly, and the head scales away for the cockpit view.

Without it the ring is between the two hands, or the steering wheel of the model.

Saved as `driver.keys` (`left` and `right`: `t` degrees, `pos`, `rot`, `grip`, `wheel`); *Back to automatic* removes them.

The turn is saved as `driver.hands.rotation` (degrees, in the space of the car), the grip as `driver.hands.grip` (0 to 1) and `driver.grip_curl`. In the game the hand turns about the middle of the wheel as it goes round it, so a hand that holds the wheel keeps the same hold. Tested with the Rigify mannequin: 15 finger bones per hand curl, the hand turns 90 degrees with the wheel.
- Hand over, changed: the right hand lets go at 130 degrees and goes by the chest to the top (as before); when it has taken the top the left hand lets go and goes in one smooth movement by the chest to the top, from there to a little before the end of the lock, and the right hand does not let go again. The hands are a function of the turn of the wheel, so the way back to the middle is exactly the same movement the other way. The car now gives the driver the angle of the steering wheel itself (a wheel that turns more than 360 degrees was read wrong from the rotation).
- The driver turns and scales about its hips (the pivot of the gizmo is in the body, not at the feet; a driver saved with the origin at the feet is converted and stays where it was). The chest and the head can be turned too: two small markers with a rotate gizmo (`driver.chest.rotation`, `driver.head.rotation`, degrees); the head stays hidden in the cockpit view.

AI cars have no driver yet.

### Track editor

- **A library of models, the same for every track:** a model (glb) that you add with + goes into the folder `objects/` (the glb, a see-through picture of it and `objects.json`; the server writes them: `start-server.sh`, a server that was started before this must be restarted) and shows as a small picture, like the cars in the main menu, in every track. **Click a picture and you are placing it** (press on the road, drag for the way it faces); **Escape goes back to Look**. The model is copied into the track that uses it, so a track does not depend on the library; a × on a picture takes a model out of the library, and a model of a track from before the library has a dashed picture and a + to keep it in the library. Its kind (rigid, static, decor) and mass are kept with the library model.
- **Gates can be chosen like objects:** click a gate in the view, or choose it in the new *Gates* section of the Layers panel: its gizmo shows, **W** moves it, **E** turns it about the vertical, **R** makes it wider or narrower (the width in metres), Delete takes it away. **Escape** always goes back to Look and lets go of everything that is chosen (objects, gates, a camera, the drag strip, and the yellow part of the model that a click in Look chose).
- **Draw the AI line:** next to *Record AI racing line* (driving a lap) and *Edit the line* (the nodes of a line that is there), *Draw the line* makes a new line without driving: click on the road, one node after the other in the direction that the AI drives, all the way round (the line closes from the last node to the first), at least four. *Undo the last node* takes one back, Shift + click adds a node between two others, a node can be moved with the gizmo as before, and *Apply line* saves it (a smooth curve, a point every 2 m) in place of the line that was there; *Cancel* keeps the old one.
- **Pit boxes and a pit line instead of a pit area:** the tool *Pit box* places a box of 4 by 8 metres for every player (up to eight, as the grid: box 1 is the player's), yellow and see-through in the game and in the editor, so that everybody knows their box. A car that stands still with its wheels inside its own box (a car without a box of its own, on a track with fewer boxes than cars, may use any) is serviced: fuel and tyres. This also solves that a stop never began in the old pit area: the wheels reported the surface of the road there, not the pit lane, so the car was never "on the pit lane". The tool *Pit line* draws the path that the AI follows: the first point on the racing line, through the middle of every box, the last point back on the racing line (a smooth curve). The AI slows down for the start of the line (at the speed of the pit lane, 58 km/h), brakes for the stop at the point of the line nearest to its box (3.5 m/s2), is serviced, and goes on to the end and back on the racing line. Without a pit line it drives to its box directly. In the test the AI went from the start of the approach to the end of the stop and back on the line in about 35 seconds, without a crash, and filled the tank from 1 to 60 litres. The older pit area (`pit.polygon`) still works for tracks that have it, but it is no longer drawn by hand; the card can remove it.
- **Objects stand where you click:** a model (glb) that is added gets its origin at the middle of its base (a file with its origin somewhere else put the object far from the click). Per model a select keeps the origin of the file when you want that (`pivot` of the object in track.json; objects of older tracks keep the origin of their file).
- **Objects as layers:** the Layers panel of the view lists the placed objects. Choose one there, or click it in the view: its gizmo shows, **W** moves, **E** turns, **R** scales, Delete takes it away, Escape lets go. An object has a full turn (the heading, and `pitch` and `roll` in degrees) and a `scale` (a number, or x, y, z) in track.json; the game places it the same way (`RigidObjects.js`).
- **A start grid and a pit area** in the track editor. *Grid box* places up to eight boxes of the start grid (`grid` in track.json: box 1 is the start of the player, 2 to 8 are where the opponents stand at the start of a race, each on its own box, the ones without a box on the racing line as before); it takes the place of *Place start* (a track from before has its start as box 1). *Pit area* draws a polygon on the ground (`pit`): inside it the road is the pit lane whatever its surface (`GroundWorld.setPitAreas`: the surface reads as the pit lane, and the AI's line through the pit comes from points inside the polygon), so a track without a pit surface in its model gets a pit lane. Both are drawn on the road in the game (`TrackMarks.js`: each box as two white side lines joined by a line across the front (the back is open), without numbers, the pit area as a translucent surface with a line round it) and in the editor.
**Track editor**
- The track editor starts with a choice: *Open track*, *Import height map* or *New track*. *Import height map* (also the button *Height map…* in the Track card) makes a track from a 16 bit greyscale PNG (the heights: black is low, white is high) and a colour picture of the same square (any size): the size of the square in metres, the height of white in metres, how fine the mesh is (every 2nd, 4th or 8th pixel; 1024 × 1024 with every 4th is 256 × 256 points) and the surface of the whole map are set in the window. It becomes a mesh `terrain` with the colour map as its texture, saved as terrain.glb in the folder of the track, and then it is a track like any other: place the start, add gates, save. The PNG is read by the editor itself (`heightmap.js`, grey and colour, 8 and 16 bit, not interlaced), because the browser would make it 8 bit. The top of the pictures is towards -z.
- Cinematic cameras have a range: a click on a camera chooses it (it turns yellow, with a gizmo to move it), the round button above a camera shows its range as a circle on the floor with a node in the middle (moves the circle) and a node on the edge (sets the radius); the radius is a field in the list too. It is saved as `range` (`x`, `z`, `r`) of a camera, and a camera without one has a circle of 30 m round itself in the editor. In the replay the camera whose circle the car is in is used (the one that the car is nearest to the middle of, as a share of the radius), and when the car is in none the camera that is the nearest to a circle; the others rules below stay.
- Cinematic cameras: the mode *Add camera* in the view puts a camera above the place that is clicked (the height of a new one is a field in the card *Cinematic cameras*, and every camera has x, y and z fields and can be removed); they are saved as `cameras` (`x`, `y`, `z`) in track.json. In the replay a fifth camera button, *Cinematic* (only on a track that has cameras), uses the camera that is nearest to the car: it does not move, it only turns to follow the car (smoothly), it goes to another camera when that one is clearly nearer (at least 15% closer, with a cut), and its field of view is chosen so that the car stays about the same size in the picture.
- The AI racing line is drawn in the view (yellow, with arrows in the direction it is driven; *Show the line* switches it off), and can be edited with nodes: *Edit the line* shows nodes about 8 m apart (blue line while editing), a click takes a node and the gizmo moves it over the road (the height follows the road), Shift + click on the road adds a node on the nearest part of the line, *Add node* adds one after the chosen node, Delete or *Delete node* takes one away, *Apply line* saves a smooth curve through the nodes (a point every 2 m) as `racing_line` of the track, *Cancel* keeps it as it was. A designed track without a recorded line can be edited from the middle of its road.

### Menu, race, controls and extras

- **After the finish of a race the car drives itself:** when you have driven your laps, the pedals and the wheel are no longer yours (keys, pad or wheel do nothing): an AI driver takes your car, at half the speed (half of what the line allows in the bends, and not more than half of the top speed that you reached in this race), on the racing line, and the camera is the cinematic one of the track (the cameras that the track editor put in it; a track without cameras keeps the camera of the game; a car without a racing line brakes to a stop). The results with *Race again* and *Main menu* stay on the screen. In the test the car went about half as fast as the AI cars (28 against 56 km/h at the most, 17 against 31 on average). A manual gearbox is shifted by the driver. `Opponent.js` can now drive a vehicle that is not its own (`vehicle`, `noModel`, `paceFactor`, `paceCap`).
- **Parking brake:** a new key (P by default; in the controls list as *Parking brake on/off*) switches the parking brake on and off: the handbrake, which stays on until the key is pressed again (with full throttle the car does not move). A banner says which. P is the aim of the free camera as well: while the free camera is the camera, P is for that. A car that is put back has it off.
- **Graphics, all in one page:** the *Tone mapping* is now on the Graphics page of the settings (it was under *Display and sound*), in sections: Picture, Light and shadow, Look, Atmosphere, Effects. The default is *Neutral* (true colours) with an exposure of 0.9: the picture was overblown (a 1.2 times brighter picture before the tone mapping, and no tone mapping at all by default). New sliders: *Exposure*, *Contrast* and *Saturation*, which work in the linear picture before the tone mapping, so they also work with the tone mapping Off.
- **One sky for every track, time of day and weather:** the sky no longer comes from the track (no sky picture, no environment colours): it is made in the game from the time of day and the weather, with the sun and a moon (a phase of your choice) that stand where they should, stars at night, clouds that move, and the light and the fog that go with them (`Atmosphere.js`). *Time of day* (dawn to night and an hour slider; the sun rises at 5:00 and sets at 19:00), *Sun direction* (where it is at noon), *Moon* (new to full), *Weather*: clear, cloudy, fog, rain and snow (falling past the camera). The track only keeps how bright its materials like the light. Tracks of the old format with a sky in their model keep that sky.
- **The ground follows the weather:** rain wets the road (about 40 s; it dries in about 3 minutes), snow builds up on the ground (about 2.5 minutes to cover it) and stays, melting slowly (faster in rain). A wet surface is darker and shinier, snow turns what faces up white (the tops of barriers and trees too). Both give less grip, for the car and for the AI (the AI drives slower): wet road about 28% less, a road under snow about 62% less (grass and loose ground a little less). The cars wear the snow away (every place that a car has been at is kept, in tiles of 25.6 m with cells of 40 cm, so a bare place stays bare when you drive away and come back; the shader sees 410 m round the car): a tyre that slides scrapes it off, a tyre that rolls takes a little, and the heat of the engine and the exhaust under a car melts it, the longer it stands there and the more often it comes by, the more: the bare asphalt shows with a soft edge, a little wet. Where the snow is gone the wheel has its grip back (and where it is thick the wheel has little). While it snows the bare places fill up again very slowly (about 17 minutes from bare to snow; it was 90 seconds, which also took the tyre tracks away at once), and a tyre that rolls takes more than before, so the tracks show. A race in the snow starts with the asphalt partly bare in patches (the road can be seen; the snow comes back on it in about half an hour of snowfall), which also gives a little more grip there. Where snow lies on sand or grass the dust that is thrown up is a little white. The falling flakes are small and bright now (they were big grey blobs). A *Ground* option fixes it: Follows the weather, Dry, Wet or Snow. The debug text shows the wet, the snow and the grip that is left.
- **Old-format tracks get the sky, the weather and the ground too:** a track without a glb model (`circuit`) had none of it (its own sky and clouds stayed, and the snow and the puddles had no shader). It now gets the sky of the game (its clouds are hidden), the weather, the snow and the wet; the Lambert and Phong materials of such a track get the white of the snow and the dark of the wet, without the shine of the puddles.
- **Puddles:** in the rain puddles form on the asphalt (about a minute and a half to fill, about 7 minutes to dry; they grow and shrink with it): a mirror of the sky and the surroundings (roughness near 0), darker, with the rings of the raindrops on them, and rings that spread from a wheel that goes through, bigger the faster it goes. A wheel in a puddle has less grip than on a wet road (up to about 40% less than dry), and throws white and grey spray (blades and droplets, out and up from the wheel, more the faster, gone when they come down). The pattern of the puddles is made in the shader and in the game by the same hash, so a wheel knows where the water is. It costs next to nothing (102 fps dry, 101 in rain with puddles in the test).
- **Cheap extras in Graphics:** *anisotropic filtering* (1 to 16 times, in the presets: the road ahead stays sharp at a slant), *sharpening* (Off to High; a last pass that also adds a fine noise that takes the bands out of the sky), both in the presets.
- **Reflections update often:** the lighting of the glTF cars (the filtered cube map) was made again after only every 12th update of the cube map, so it followed the surroundings about once a second. It is now every 3rd, in the same target every time (no new textures to throw away). On *High* both happen at every frame (the cube map and the lighting of the paint, in the game and in a replay; before, "every frame" was in fact every other frame), on *Low* the cube map every 8th frame and the lighting after every 3rd.
- **Debug text:** a line under the triangles shows the nodes of a tyre (rings times segments) and the substeps of a step of the physics.
- **AI cars have a driver:** an opponent gets the dummy of its car (`driver` of car.json, or of the tune that was chosen for it) that holds the steering wheel and works the pedals, in the game and in replays (the pedals and the gear come from the recording). The colour that tells the AI cars apart is now only on the meshes called `body`, as the body colour of the tuning menu; the rest of the model and the dummy keep their own colours.
- **Choose your opponents:** a race has a new last step, *Opponents*, after *Set up the race*: a grid of as many places as *Opponents* says, four in a row, each an empty container with a +. Click it to choose a car (as for your own car) and, when the car has a saved tune, the tune (Default is the car as made); the car then takes the place, with its picture. The copy button on a car adds the same car again until the places are full, ✕ removes it, and *Start* begins the race with those cars and tunes (`&opp=car~tune,car`). Without a choice the cars of the garage take the places, as before.
- The graphics settings are dropdowns (the preset, the resolution, the anti-aliasing, the shadows, the reflections, SSAO, SSGI, the vignette, the draw distance): they were buttons that went round their choices one way, and on a phone a tap on the resolution could only go up, until the page gave out.
- **A phone** (an iPhone 16 Plus: a HUD far too big in portrait, black textures and a page that ended): the textures of the car, the driver and the track models are drawn down to 1024 by 1024 on a touch device before they go to the graphics card (`TextureBudget.js`; the Supermoto 700 had two of 4096 and its driver one: hundreds of MB with the mipmaps), a phone starts on the *Low* graphics preset, and on a narrow screen the lap box, the tyre panel, the gauge (130 px instead of 210), the pills and the free camera panel are smaller, the settings menu fills the screen with tabs that scroll, and the debug text at the top is hidden. `?touch=1` in the address makes a desktop browser do the same, to try it.
- **A vehicle selector in practice.** In practice (no laps) the *Car* tab of the Esc menu has *Change vehicle…*: a popup with every vehicle (the picture and the name, the one that is driven marked); a click on one replaces the vehicle that is driven, on the same track and with the same options (the page opens again with it).
**Look and menu**
- On a **touch screen the pills of the HUD are buttons**: a tap on TC, LC (launch control), ESC, ABS or 2S (two-step) switches it on or off, as its key does (the pill turns green or red). The pills are bigger to touch on a phone.

Now the end of any finger lets go of what it held (wherever it ends), a lost capture lets go, no finger on the screen lets everything go, a hidden page lets go, and a new touch on the steering takes over from one whose end was lost.
- **The easing of the keyboard**: the Keyboard tab of the settings has, for the gas, the brake and the steering, a **time** (to full gas, full brake, full lock at a standstill: 0.05 to 2 s) and a **curve** (0.3 to 3: below 1 quick at first and soft at the end, 1 straight, above 1 soft at first; the shape is drawn next to the slider). Before, a key was a fixed rise. The defaults are close to what it was (gas 0.5 s, brake 0.4 s, steering 0.9 s). *Steering speed* is replaced by the steering time.
- *Open track* in the track editor shows every track as a button (no dropdown); a click opens it at once, *Back* goes back to the choices.
- **No more alerts or confirms of the browser**: every question and message is a window of the game (`web/dialog.js`, in the look of the menu): deleting, replacing, importing a link, writing a tuning, the editors, a track that does not load. Importing a car or track that already exists (in the game, the garage or the linked folder) now **shows what is different** (`chassis.max_steer_deg 35 → 42`, added and removed fields) in that window, or says that the settings are the same; *Replace* or *Keep the old one*. The model files are not compared.
- *My garage* shows a small picture of every car, and the home screen of the menu is in the middle of the page.
- Importing a zip (or a link) of a car or track now asks first when one with the same id is already there (in the game, the garage or the linked folder): *Replace it?*; *Cancel* imports nothing. It replaced it without asking before.
- **Still pictures of the cars in the menu**: the turning previews that loaded slowly are a picture of each car, from the front at an angle (`preview.webp` in the folder of the car, made once by the menu and kept in the browser too; the car editor takes it away when the car is saved again). The first time takes a moment, after that the menu shows them at once, and nothing is drawn while you choose.
- The menu is a few screens, one choice each, and starts with the OpenRacer logo as on openracer.eu: **Play** (and Garage, Replays, Create, Settings under it), then *Free drive / Race / Skidpad*, the car, the track and, for a race, its options (laps, opponents, difficulty as buttons, the rest under *More options…*), with *Back* and *Next* and a line of what you chose. The page of 1900 px with all the choices and eight buttons is gone; the editors are one *Create* window, and the skidpad is a way to play. Enter goes on, Esc goes back.

The editor saves the height of every gate now. `GateMarkers.js`.

Made in the browser, follows the sound option and the volume; the arrow follows the HUD option.

Run it after a change to the physics or to a car.

At 20 m/s a key held for 0.6 s turns the wheels 0.36 rad when it is off, 0.07 as it was and 0.05 with 2.
- The wheels of the stadium truck flickered up and down at speed: its tyre was a 12-sided polygon (`tyres.shell.segments` 12, taken over from an example), which let go of the road for a frame now and then, and a wheel that lost contact was drawn at the end of its travel at once (17 cm). The tyre has 36 sides now, and a wheel without contact is drawn where the suspension has it (`measureWheels`). At 45 m/s on the straight of the circuit the wheels let go of the road 33 to 48 times per 10 s with 12 sides, and 3 to 8 times with 36, which is the road and the jumps themselves.

A half throttle start and a bend below the limit are silent now; a bend over the limit, a full throttle start and a hard stop squeal as before.

An AI kart in a replay of the Desert was launched from a stand-still to 100 and later 169 m/s.

Made by `tools/make-desert-track.py` (numpy; run it again to change the shape, the seed is in the file).

Set as `tyres.compound: "offroad"`, in the tuning menu and in the car editor; the stadium truck has it. A compound has `looseGrip` and `looseRolling` for this (Wheel.js), and the surfaces grass and ground are `loose` (Helpers.js).
- Long travel suspension for off-road cars: `chassis.suspension_length` (the room for the travel, 0.28 m by default; it was fixed), `chassis.spring_progression` (a progressive spring: stiffer at the end of the bump travel, no change in the ride height) and `chassis.damping_rebound` (the rebound against the compression damping, 1.7 by default); they are in the tuning menu too. The example car `stadium-truck` (web/cars/stadium-truck) has 0.38 m of bump travel and a model to model around, `stadium-truck.glb` (tools/make-stadium-truck.py).
- The same for cars: the rims, the brake calipers and the number plate of a car model are pictures with an alpha channel, and are cut out too, so the calipers of the i30 are no longer drawn over the tyre and the wheel arch (`cutOut` in Helpers.js, used by TrackLoader.js and CarLoader.js).
- Pictures with an alpha channel on a track (trees, fences, signs: png textures, `alphaMode` BLEND in a glTF) are cut out instead of blended, so a tree that is behind another one is no longer drawn over it now and then (the blending depends on the order of the objects). A material that is see-through as a whole (opacity below 1, glass) stays blended.
- The reset key (R, and ↺ on a touch screen) only puts the car back at the saved position, as it is: the damage, the tyres (heat, wear, pressure) and the engine stay, and the car stands still on its wheels. The key *Save the place of the car for the reset* (4, rebindable in *Esc → Controls → Other*) saves where the car is now (the place, the heading and the height; kept per track in the browser); until one is saved the reset goes to the start. Repairing is for the repair key (Backspace).
- A value that is not a number (NaN) in the state of a car no longer kills the car and the sound (`setTargetAtTime ... non-finite` in the tyre squeal): the sound ignores such a value, and the vehicle checks its state after every update, reports the field once in the console (`Vehicle: a value is not a number (...)`) and puts the car back upright where it was last fine.
- In the race column of the main menu, a list with a tick for every car chooses which cars may race against you (shown when a race of laps or AI opponents is chosen; *all* and *none* above it; all are ticked by default and the choice is remembered). The AI cars are taken from the ticked cars in turn (`aicars` in the address); with none ticked every car may race.

**Controls**
- Automatic gearbox: holding the brake key at a standstill (or below a speed that can be set) puts the car in reverse while it is held; the gas key brakes in reverse and is D again at a standstill.

**Drag strip**
- The Top 50 has a tab for every distance.
- A Top 50 of the drag runs in the main menu (the car with its tuning, the 1/4 and 2/4 mile times, the top speed, and the ticket of the run); the drag strip is a layer in the track editor with a gizmo for its place and its direction.
- A *Drag strip* tool in the track editor (a window for the distance and the width; the start line is where you click, the strip goes the way you drag), and in the game: the distance to the start line, the lights, false starts, and the times and speeds at the standard distances within the strip. At the finish a time slip slides in from the top at the right (a cross or Escape closes it).

**Launch**
- The rev limiter is a hard fuel cut with a set rate (*Cuts per second*), so the engine bounces against the limit.
- **Two-step** (Y switches it on; the clutch pedal in at a standstill, or Q on a keyboard), **transbrake** (F) and **launch control** (J, an option) with their own engine speeds in the tuning menu and the car editor; the pills LC, 2S and TB show them.

**Extras**
- Optional extras with a checkbox in the tuning menu (the settings open below it): **pops and bangs** (sound and flames at the exhaust), **anti-lag** and a **boost gauge**. They are kept in car.json under `extras`.
- The exhaust points of a car are placed in the car editor (the *Exhaust* layer, with a gizmo for the place and one for the direction); the flames can be your own pictures (`web/images/flames/`).

**Panels**
- The tuning menu, the free camera panel, the replay bar and its speed box, and the exhaust window of the car editor can be moved (drag a part that is not a control) and scaled (the bottom right corner); where they are is kept in the browser.
- **The AI uses the grip of its car and its gearbox.** The speed of the racing line came from a fixed 1.05 g and 0.9 g braking (a kart on semislicks holds 1.2 g, and the player was seen at 1.27 g while hard AI cars stayed under 0.9 g); it is the `tyres.grip` of the car now (hard: 1.1 times that). The AI vehicle was built without the gearbox of car.json (`gearbox`, `cvt_*`, `tc_*`), so a kart with a CVT drove in a fixed ratio; it has them now. The bend radius of the racing line is taken over 8 m instead of 4 m, so a kink in a hand-drawn line is no reason to crawl.
- **Tyres of a small car warm up.** The heat capacity and the cooling of a tyre (those of a car tyre: 20 kJ/K, 40 W/K) go with the cube of its radius, so a kart tyre (it stayed at 55 degrees and cooled down on every lap) is at 80 degrees after two minutes of hard driving.
- **The AI cuts the bends within the road** (`RacingLine.shorten`): the racing line is probed to both sides (up to 6 m, until the surface of the line ends, 1.1 m kept free) and every point moves to the smoothest line there is, so a line drawn along the middle is no longer followed through every bend (`?cut=0` keeps it as drawn). The mean speed the line allows goes up by about 10 %.
- **The AI learns the track.** The racing line is driven in pieces of 10 m, each with a factor on the speed the line allows: it grows 7 % when the piece was driven near the line, shrinks when the car ran wide in it (more than 3.5 m) and by 18 % over the three pieces before a crash. The ceiling goes with the difficulty (easy 1.1, normal 1.35, hard 1.6). What a driver learned is kept per track, car and driver in the browser (`openracer.ai.*`), so the next race starts from it. On the Kartbaan hard AI cars go from a lap of 72 s to 52 s in four laps. The steering pull towards the line is weaker (it made the kart scrub speed in bends), and the line keeps 0.8 m from the edge of the road.
- **The best lap is the line of the AI.** The best lap driven on a track (by any car, on this computer; the ghost) is the racing line of the AI from then on, instead of the line saved with the track, so the AI takes the bends the way the fastest driver does. Without a lap the line of the track is used, as before (`?bestline=0` keeps that one). What the AI learned is kept per line, so a new best lap starts the learning again.
- **A car that falls out of the world comes back by itself.** Half a second below everything that is the track (25 m under its lowest point), the car goes back as the reset key does it.
- **The reset puts any car on the road.** The place that the save key keeps now has the car it was saved with and the height of the road there: another car (a truck after a kart) is put on the road there instead of at the height of the body of the first (it stood in the ground), and where roads lie above one another (a bridge, a tunnel) the one that was meant is taken, not the highest. A place saved before this goes back to the road at that point.
- **The lap time starts at GO.** It stood at 0:09 when the countdown was at 2. The countdown number is bigger, higher on the screen and has an outline (it was white on a white helmet), and the hint says that Enter skips the fly-by.
- **An input that goes to another action says so.** Setting a key that another action uses moves it, as before, and the settings menu now tells which actions lost it.
- **No jump records any more.** The notice after a jump (distance, time in the air, height), the ten longest jumps of a track and the *Longest jumps* button of the track menu are gone (`JumpStats.js`). The jumps that an older version kept in the browser (`openracer.jump*`) are removed when the game starts.

### Physics, tyres and engine

- **Grass, sand and snow fly:** a wheel that slips on loose ground (grass, sand, gravel, mud, earth), or on any surface under snow, throws small pieces up (`Debris.js`, next to the dust of the smoke), each with the look of what it is: thin green blades for grass (turned along the way they fly, with a clod of earth now and then), many tiny round grains for sand, small angular tan chunks for gravel, clumps for mud and earth, and soft flakes for snow. They fly the way the tread moves against the ground: backwards from a driven wheel that spins, forwards from a locked one, and the way the car slides sideways. The faster the slip, the more there are and the further they fly. A piece that comes down stays on the ground for 5 to 14 seconds, then fades. Three options in the Graphics page, under Effects: *Debris: spawn rate* (a number you type: 1 is as it was, 0 is none, no upper limit), *Debris: size* (0 to 5 times; 1 is as it was) and *Debris: most pieces at once* (500 to 20000; the oldest go first). The pieces are not made in a replay, and for the player's car only.
- **Triangles in the debug text:** a line *Sim triangles* under the FPS shows what the simulation asks of the triangles of the track in a frame: the rays of the wheels and how many triangles they test (and the most that one ray tests), the spheres of the shell and the triangles they bring, the cheap box tests and the exact distance tests. A place with lag shows up as a high number there (many triangles in a few cells of the grid).
**The shell tyre**
- **The shell is the tyre of every car.** The older cylinder body without the air pressure (TyreBody.js) is gone with the *Tyre model* setting (`tyres.model` in car.json is not read any more). The ring of nodes (NodeTyre.js, tyre model "nodes") and the 2D physics of a track are gone, with the ray tyre of the old physics, the *Tyre of nodes* settings and the `?physics=2d` switch; a track that says `"physics": "2d"` runs with the 3D physics. The test benches (the car editor, tools/physics-bench.js) run on a flat road with the triangles the tyre needs (`OpenRacer.FlatGround`).
- *Tyre: nodes round the wheel* is what the tyre gets, from 4 (a square) to 72; the shell doubled the setting and took at least 48, so the slider did nothing below 24. Fewer nodes cost less (the road is felt at four places between each two nodes); the settings are named *Tyre: ...* instead of *Tyre cylinder: ...* and *Tyre shell (experiment): ...*.
- The places between two nodes where the road is felt lie on the arc of the tyre, not on the chord between the nodes, and they are about 3 cm apart whatever the number of nodes: a tyre of 8 nodes rolls as round as one of 36 (the Mustang: steady loads, 0.14 ms per wheel per step against 0.4 with 36 nodes); with 4 nodes the load still comes in lumps as the corners pass.
**Tyre shell (experiment)**
- **Camber and toe on the shell tyre, and a tyre bench.** The tyre bodies (cylinder and shell) get the camber of the wheel: its static camber less what the roll of the body takes (and a bent knuckle), as the ring of nodes had it; the up of the tyre and its axle lean about its forward axis, so the inside shoulder carries more and a leaning shell tyre pushes to the side it leans to (camber thrust, a twentieth of the cornering stiffness per radian). The wheel of the model leans with the camber (it only leaned when it was bent). Toe was already the steering angle of the wheel. `node tools/tyre-bench.js` runs a tyre model alone on a test rig and holds the figures against a real passenger car tyre (see the README): the Mustang's shell tyre now gives 17 mm of deflection and 300 N/mm at 2.2 bar (a harder tyre goes in less, which it did not), a slip stiffness of 15 per unit slip (4.6 before: the tread was capped for the step of the wheel), a cornering stiffness of 14 per radian, peaks of 0.95 at 30 % slip and 0.98 at 10 degrees, and settles with wheels of 20 to 90 kg on it.
- **Shell tyre: what the bench found and the fixes.** The bristles of a node kept their bend off the road and the lateral force stepped up after one turn of the wheel: they are straight again when a node leaves the road. A car tyre has a flat crown now (a motorcycle tyre a round one): the outer rings never touched before. The carcass goes slack at 1.8 times the static deflection (`carcass` 0; a value in mm as before, up to 80 in the menu), which gives the tyre the car's tyre rate and lets the stiffness follow the pressure. The torque about the hub was taken from the hub of the start of the step while the rim ran along the path of the hub. The shell turns the wheel itself in its substeps with the torque of the tread, and the car adds the engine and the brake (the grip saturates within millimetres of turn, which the 3 ms step of the car cannot follow: a wheel at a standstill was thrown between the two limits of the grip at 4 rad/s). The car's `tyre_damping` damps the hub against the road (it held the hop of a wheel on the tyre; the smoothing of the load is 1.5 ms). The dampers of the beams and of the shear across the wheel are lower (they were a rolling resistance that grew with the speed), the shear along the wheel is damped more (it is what holds the wheel against the tread at a standstill). Still open: the rolling resistance grows with the speed (Crr 0.01 at 18 km/h, 0.04 at 54, 0.10 at 108), the relaxation length is short (0.09 m), the patch is long; see the README.
- The shell tyre no longer spins and squeals at a standstill (the stadium truck, the Mustang): the delay of the force filter (6 ms) now holds only for the load, not for the friction and the torque (a delay on the stiff grip of a wheel made it ring), the bristles that hold give the wheel their damping and stiffness to take implicitly (`dampSlope`), and the shear of the tread is limited to a quarter of what a wheel can follow in one step (a half before). At a standstill the wheels of the truck turn 0.15 rad/s against 4 to 5 before.
- *Tyre cylinder: nodes round the wheel* can be set down to 4 (it was 8).
- The shell tyre no longer stutters near a rail or a wall: every node was tested against every triangle near the wheel in every substep, which with the many small triangles of a rail came to millions of tests per step. The triangles are now sorted once per step (per block of nodes, then per node) and nodes that nothing can reach are not tested at all: 22 ms against 0.8 ms per step for a wheel next to 400 triangles, with the same loads. A shell wheel on open road costs about 0.7 ms per step (the cylinder 0.15 ms), so it is still heavier than the cylinder.
- Tyre model *Shell* (`tyres.model` "shell", *Tyre model* in the tuning menu, *Inner shell* and *Carcass softness* beside it, `tyres.shell.inner_ratio` and `carcass`): an outer shell of free nodes and an inner shell that follows the rim, joined by beams (cables that pull and are taut by the air pressure, belt and shear springs, a bump stop). The pressure (bar, per axle) pushes the outer nodes out and the rim back, so it changes the patch, the load and the deformation; the debug view shows both shells, the strain of the beams, the contact and the pressure in bar and psi. The road is felt at four places between each two nodes, and the force to the car passes a filter of 6 ms, which keeps the fast modes of the tyre from ringing through a light wheel (the Mustang flipped between 7000 and 0 N per frame without them). Checked on the Mustang, the stadium truck and the sports motorcycle: loads at rest and under acceleration and braking match the cylinder tyre. The tyre of the wheel model deforms too (`TyreDeform.js`): the vertices of the tread are pushed in by the vertex shader as far as the outer nodes of the shell are, flat under the load and out where the air pushes, and the rim stays as it is (no change to the model; `OpenRacer.TyreDeform.gain` in the console draws it deeper, for a look).

**3D physics**
- The road is no longer one height per place. Every triangle of the track is in a 3D grid, the wheels feel the road with rays along their own axis, and the body is a cloud of spheres, so a loop, a road on a wall or a bridge works: a car with enough speed goes up the wall of a loop, over the top upside down and lands on the exit.
- The collision shape of a car is made from its model when the car loads (about 40 to 90 spheres), instead of a box.
- A wall counts where the car is lower than its top, measured along the car; the seams between triangles do not push the car; only a car that goes into a wall is hit hard.
- It is the default for every track. A track can keep the old physics with `"physics": "2d"` in `track.json` (or `?physics=2d`).

**Tyres**
- The repair key (Backspace) now also puts the car back on its wheels where it is: the same place and heading, the roof up, 30 cm above the road, not moving (a car that lies on its roof or its side). Tested with a kart on its roof: after the key it stands again on its wheels with the normal loads.
- The classic tyre (the slip curves, Pacejka's magic formula) is gone: the tyre body of nodes and beams (the *cylinder*) is the tyre of every car. *Tyre model* has two choices now, *Cylinder* (the default, also for `tyres.model` "classic" or nothing in an older car.json) and *Ring of nodes* (the simple ring, used on tracks with 2D physics, which have no 3D road for the cylinder).
- Tyre of nodes: a car that slid off a ramp kept rocking on its tyres (yaw of ±5°, creeping at 0.25 m/s) for as long as it stood: the damping near a standstill was too low for the whole car (the yaw as well as the car as a share of its tyres). It is doubled (`restDamping` 2); after the same drop (taken from a replay) the yaw is 0° and the car stands (0.017 m/s), with the same classic result. 0 to 40 km/h on the flat takes 1.25 s instead of 1.23 s.
- Tyre model *Cylinder* (experimental, `tyres.model` "cylinder" in car.json, *Tyre model* in the tuning menu and the car editor): the tyre is one body of nodes and beams, a rounded cylinder (rings of nodes across the tread, the outer rings lower on the shoulder, nodes round the wheel, `tyres.shell`: `width`, `rings`, `segments`; the tuning table has them as *Tyre cylinder*), and it replaces the single ring and the slip curve. The nodes and the places on the beams between them are tested against every triangle of the track (road, kerb faces, the sides of ramps, walls), so the tyre does not go through the side of a ramp; they are pushed back by springs (the stiffness and the pressure of the tyre) and drawn flattened where they touch. Every node in contact holds the surface with a bristle (spring and damper, up to the friction of the surface times what presses it, with a Stribeck fall), so the grip, the combined slip, the stick at a standstill and the way the tyre lets go come out of the nodes; the sums go to the wheel (the load along the suspension, the forces along the road, and what presses across the suspension, such as the side of a ramp, to the car at the hub). The debug view (0) draws the nodes and beams per wheel (green where a node holds, red where it slides). On the openracer-frame car: loads on the tyres as with classic (3032 and 2440 N), 0 to 100 km/h in 3.2 s against 3.4 s, braking from 100 km/h in 74 m against 71 m, 0.45 g against 0.44 g after 1.5 s of steering at 40 km/h, and no rocking after a drop from a ramp. It is not the default yet, and it costs about 0.8 ms per frame against 0.15 ms; the visible tyre model does not deform yet, and the tyre cylinder cannot be set graphically in the car editor yet, only by its numbers.
- Tyre of nodes: a node keeps its deflection when it leaves the contact patch and relaxes only as the wheel rolls (it was set to zero), so the patch that moves a little with the load no longer lets a parked car creep. Tyre damage needs a harder hit (5 times the static load) after a bounce off a post punctured the rear tyres.
- A repair key (Backspace, *Esc → Controls → Other → Repair the car where it stands*, and a 🔧 button on touch screens) repairs the car where it stands: the dents of the shell, the parts that came off, the wheels and the punctured tyres. *Back to the start* (R) now repairs the tyres too (it repaired the body only).
- Tyre of nodes: a car at a standstill no longer rocks on its tyres. The spring of the nodes and the mass of the car rang with too little damping when the car stood still (it showed 1.1 m/s and 1.4 rad/s of rocking, and went away once the car rolled); near a standstill and while the wheel hardly slips the damping is now a critical one for the share of the car that the tyre carries, fading out with speed and slip (rocking 0.007 rad/s). 0 to 100 km/h is 13.6 s with both tyre models.
- The tyre of nodes has a carcass now: every tread node is a radial spring that the road presses in by the round of the tyre minus the height of the road under it (the wheel already measures the road at five places along the tyre), and the load is shared over the nodes by how deep they are pressed in. The patch comes out of the stiffness and the pressure of the tyre, and a kerb or a bump under a part of the patch moves the load to the nodes on it. 0 to 100 km/h 13.17 s and braking from 100 km/h 44.3 m on the Hyundai i30, as before (13.19 s, 44.9 m).
- Tyre damage (*Tyre damage: On* in the tuning menu, `tyres.damage` in car.json): a hard hit (a load of more than 5 times the static one on a tyre, one hit in three or so) can puncture it; the tyre leaks and loses its pressure, so its grip, its sidewall stiffness and its vertical stiffness fall, and with the tyre of nodes the contact patch grows. `vehicle.puncture(wheel)` and `vehicle.repairTyres()` do it by hand. The patch of the tyre of nodes now follows the vertical stiffness of the tyre (and so its pressure). Beams between the carcass nodes (a sidewall that deforms) are not done yet.
- An experimental tyre model made of nodes (*Tyre model: Nodes* in the tuning menu, `tyres.model` in car.json): a ring of tread nodes with springs and friction instead of slip curves. The default stays the classic model.

**Light vehicles**
- The joints of the suspension in the collision shell (the lower ball joint and the arms) scale with a wheel smaller than 0.3 m: those of a car wheel stood in the road under a kart and held back its weight and its speed (12 contacts with the road at rest: a kart took 8.8 s to 30 km/h, now 5.6 s).
- A vehicle under 600 kg (a kart) gets light wheels and a softer tyre: the wheels are 2.85 % of the mass (instead of 35 kg each), the tyre stiffness and damping go with the square root of the mass over 1230 kg, and the wheel inertia with the mass and the square of the diameter (a car wheel of 1.6 kg m² made the wheels most of the mass of a kart: a kart needed three times the power to get going).
- The automatic clutch starts to grip at `drivetrain.clutch_start_rpm` and grips fully at `clutch_full_rpm` (1050 and 2000 by default, also in the tuning table): a kart with a centrifugal clutch and one gear lets the clutch slip until it locks at its engagement speed.

**Engine**
- A turbo with a boost pressure in bar: the power follows the pressure (pressure ratio, compressor and intercooler efficiency, exhaust back pressure); the older share of torque stays for cars without it.

**Gearbox**
- The automatic gearbox shifts up when the engine is near the rev limiter with the wheels spinning faster than the road (a lot of power in a low gear): before, it only looked at the speed of the road and stayed on the limiter until the throttle was lifted. After such a shift it does not kick down for three seconds.

**Debug view**
- The 0 key (also on the numeric keypad) shows what the physics sees: the triangles near the car (road, walls and the rest), the collision spheres, the nodes and beams of the shell, the contact of every wheel and the tread nodes of the tyre of nodes. See `DebugView.js`.

**Performance**
- A wall of tyres no longer drops the frame rate when the car drives close to it: the physics tested every triangle near the car (a tyre has 832, so 15,000 and more within 2 m) against every sphere of the car, 740 ms per step on Kartbaan Oldenzaal. Small props of 200 triangles and more (less than 1.5 m across) now hit the car as their box (12 triangles); the physics triangles of that track went from 2.4 million to 42 thousand, and a step next to the wall takes about 2.6 ms. What you see is unchanged.
- Decor that is only drawn (tyre walls, trees) is merged per material and per 40 m cell when a track is loaded, so a track with thousands of loose parts needs a few hundred draw calls instead of thousands (Kartbaan Oldenzaal: 3852 parts in 68 groups). The collision and the road are unchanged.
